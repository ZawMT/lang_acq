# Adverbs

An adverb describes or gives more information about a verb, an adjective, or another adverb (see [Adverb](./03_epos.md#adverb)). Like nouns and adjectives, adverbs can be classified along a few different, independent axes.

## By formation

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Derived adverb | built from an adjective, usually by adding "-ly" | beautiful → **beautifully**, quick → **quickly** |
| Simple (flat) adverb | not built from an adjective; a word of its own | **often**, **soon**, **very**, **fast**, **well**, **always** |

- Sentence: She sings **beautifully** and **often** performs live.

Note: not every "-ly" word is an adverb (e.g. "friendly" is an adjective), and not every adverb ends in "-ly" (e.g. "fast" is both an adjective and an adverb unchanged).

## By function

### Manner — how something happens

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| quickly, slowly, carefully, well | She drives **carefully**. |

### Place — where something happens

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| here, there, everywhere, outside | Put it **there**. |

### Time — when something happens

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| now, yesterday, soon, later | We'll leave **soon**. |

### Frequency — how often something happens

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| always, often, sometimes, never, rarely | He **always** arrives on time. |

### Degree — how much/to what extent

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| very, quite, too, almost, extremely | It's **too** hot today. |

## Interrogative and relative adverbs

The same small set of words — **when, where, why, how** — can play two different roles, much like the adjective/pronoun pairs covered in [Adjectives](./10_adjectives.md#other-adjective-types-cross-links):

- **Interrogative adverb** — asks a question.
  - **When** did you arrive? **Why** did she leave?
- **Relative adverb** — introduces a clause that describes a noun, replacing "at/in/for which" (see [Clause](./04_sentence_structure.md#clause)).
  - The day **when** we met. The reason **why** she left.

## Conjunctive adverbs

A conjunctive adverb connects two independent clauses or sentences, showing a logical relationship (contrast, result, addition) between them. Usually followed by a comma.

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, meanwhile | It was raining; **however**, we still went out. |

## Degrees of comparison

Like adjectives, many adverbs can compare things.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| fast | faster | fastest |
| carefully | more carefully | most carefully |
| well | better | best |
| badly | worse | worst |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |

- Sentence: She runs **fast**, he runs **faster**, but the champion runs the **fastest**.
