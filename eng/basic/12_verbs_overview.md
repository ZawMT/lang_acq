# Verbs Overview

A verb shows an action or a state of being (see [Verb](./03_epos.md#verb)). Verbs have enough ground to cover that they're split across several files instead of one — this file introduces the basic verb *types* (by function); [forms](./13_verb_forms.md), [tenses](./14_tenses.md), [voice](./15_voice.md), [modals](./16_modal_verbs.md), [mood and conditionals](./17_mood_and_conditionals.md), [non-finite verbs](./18_non_finite_verbs.md), and [phrasal verbs](./19_phrasal_verbs.md) each get their own file.

## Action vs. stative verbs

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Action (dynamic) verb | describes something being done | run, eat, write, jump |
| Stative verb | describes a state, feeling, or fact rather than an action | know, believe, love, own, seem |

- Sentence: She **runs** every morning and **believes** it keeps her healthy.

Stative verbs don't normally take the continuous/progressive form: "I **know** the answer" (correct), not "I **am knowing** the answer." Some verbs can be either, depending on meaning — "think" is stative when it means "have an opinion" ("I **think** it's true") but action when it means "actively consider" ("I'**m thinking** about it").

## Transitive vs. intransitive verbs

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Transitive verb | needs a direct object to complete its meaning | She read **a book**. |
| Intransitive verb | doesn't take a direct object | She sleeps. |

Some verbs can be either, depending on the sentence: "She eats **an apple**." (transitive) vs. "She eats." (intransitive)

## Linking verbs

A linking verb connects the subject to a subject complement (a noun or adjective that describes it), rather than showing an action.

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| be, seem, become, appear, look, feel, taste, smell, sound | She **seems** happy. The soup **tastes** good. |

## Main vs. auxiliary verbs

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Main (lexical) verb | carries the sentence's real meaning | She is **running**. |
| Auxiliary (helping) verb | supports the main verb to build tense, questions, or negation | She **is** running. **Do** you run? She **doesn't** run. |

The three core auxiliary verbs are **be**, **have**, and **do**. Modal verbs (can, must, should, etc.) are a special kind of auxiliary verb — see [Modal verbs](./16_modal_verbs.md).
