# Voice

Voice tells us whether the subject of a sentence performs the action, or receives it.

## Active voice

The subject **does** the action.

- Structure: Subject + verb + object
- Sentence: **The chef** cooked **the meal**.

## Passive voice

The subject **receives** the action. The doer of the action (if mentioned at all) moves into a "by ..." phrase at the end.

- Structure: Subject (was the object) + be + past participle + (by ...)
- Sentence: **The meal** was cooked **by the chef**.

## Active vs. passive, side by side

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | She **writes** the report. | The report **is written** by her. |
| Present continuous | She **is writing** the report. | The report **is being written** by her. |
| Past simple | She **wrote** the report. | The report **was written** by her. |
| Present perfect | She **has written** the report. | The report **has been written** by her. |
| Future simple | She **will write** the report. | The report **will be written** by her. |

Every passive sentence uses a form of **be** + [past participle](./13_verb_forms.md), no matter the tense — only the form of "be" changes to match the tense.

## When to use passive voice

Passive voice is useful when:

- The doer is unknown or unimportant: "The window **was broken**."
- The action itself matters more than who did it: "Mistakes **were made**."
- You want to sound more formal or objective, as in scientific writing: "The sample **was heated** to 100°C."
