# Mood and Conditionals

Mood shows the speaker's attitude toward what's being said — a fact, a command, or a hypothetical. Conditionals ("if" sentences) rely heavily on one particular mood (the subjunctive), which is why they're grouped together here.

## The three moods

| Mood | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Indicative | states a fact or opinion (the most common mood) | She **is** at home. |
| Imperative | gives a command or request | **Close** the door. |
| Subjunctive | expresses a wish, suggestion, or hypothetical/unreal situation | If I **were** rich, I would travel. |

The subjunctive is most visible in English in two places:

- After certain verbs of suggestion/demand: "I suggest that he **be** on time." (not "is")
- In unreal/hypothetical "if" clauses: "If I **were** you, I'd apologize." (not "was")

## Conditionals

A conditional sentence has two parts: an "if" clause (condition) and a main clause (result).

### Zero conditional — general truths

- Structure: If + present simple, present simple
- Sentence: If you **heat** ice, it **melts**.

### First conditional — real/likely future situations

- Structure: If + present simple, will + base form
- Sentence: If it **rains**, we **will stay** home.

### Second conditional — unreal/unlikely present or future situations

- Structure: If + past simple, would + base form
- Sentence: If I **won** the lottery, I **would travel** the world.
- Note: uses subjunctive "were" instead of "was" for "be" — "If I **were** you..."

### Third conditional — unreal past situations (can't be changed now)

- Structure: If + past perfect, would have + past participle
- Sentence: If she **had studied**, she **would have passed** the exam.

### Mixed conditional — mixing past and present

There are two directions this can go, depending on which clause is past and which is present:

- **Past condition → present result**
  - Structure: If + past perfect, would + base form
  - Sentence: If I **had taken** that job, I **would be** rich **now**.
- **Present condition → past result**
  - Structure: If + past simple, would have + past participle
  - Sentence: If I **were** rich (in general), I **would have bought** that house (back then).

### Third vs. mixed conditional — why they look so similar

Both start with "If + past perfect," which is why they're easy to confuse. The difference is entirely in the **result clause**:

| | Result clause | Result is about | Time-word tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Third conditional | **would have** + past participle | the past (event is finished) | usually none needed — both clauses read as past |
| Mixed conditional | **would** + base form | the present (ongoing state) | often has "now," "today," etc. |

- Third: "If she **had studied**, she **would have passed** the exam." → studying and passing are both finished, unreal past events.
- Mixed: "If I **had taken** that job, I **would be** rich **now**." → taking the job was in the past, but "rich" is a present state that's still true today.

## Quick comparison

| Conditional | Time | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Zero | always true | If you **heat** water, it **boils**. |
| First | real future possibility | If it **rains**, I **will bring** an umbrella. |
| Second | unreal present/future | If I **had** wings, I **would fly**. |
| Third | unreal past | If I **had known**, I **would have come**. |
| Mixed | unreal past → present result | If I **had left** earlier, I **would be** there now. |
