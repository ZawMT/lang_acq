# Non-finite Verbs

A **finite** verb is a verb tied to a subject and a tense — "she **runs**," "she **ran**." A **non-finite** verb doesn't show tense or agree with a subject; it acts more like a noun, adjective, or adverb inside the sentence. English has three kinds: gerunds, infinitives, and participles.

## Gerunds

The "-ing" form of a verb used as a **noun**.

- Sentence: **Swimming** is good exercise. (subject)
- Sentence: I enjoy **reading**. (object)
- Sentence: She's tired of **waiting**. (object of a preposition)

## Infinitives

The base form of a verb, usually with "to" ("to walk"), used as a noun, adjective, or adverb.

| Function | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| As a noun | **To travel** is her dream. |
| As an adjective | She has homework **to finish**. |
| As an adverb | He saved money **to buy** a car. |

A "bare infinitive" (without "to") is used after modal verbs and a few other verbs: "She **can swim**." "Let him **go**."

## Participles

There are two kinds, and both can act as **adjectives**:

| Participle | Form | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Present participle | "-ing" | The **boring** movie put me to sleep. |
| Past participle | "-ed" / irregular | The **broken** window needs fixing. |

Note the difference between a gerund and a present participle — both look identical ("-ing"), but a gerund acts as a noun while a present participle acts as an adjective (or builds the continuous tense, see [Tenses](./14_tenses.md)):

- Gerund (noun): **Running** is fun.
- Present participle (adjective): The **running** water is cold.
- Present participle (continuous tense): She is **running**.

## Gerund vs. infinitive after a verb

Some verbs are followed by a gerund, some by an infinitive, and some can take either.

| Pattern | Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| verb + gerund | enjoy, avoid, finish, mind | She **enjoys dancing**. |
| verb + infinitive | want, decide, plan, hope | She **wants to dance**. |
| verb + either (same meaning) | like, love, start, begin | She **likes dancing** / **likes to dance**. |
