# Interjections

An interjection expresses a sudden feeling and often stands on its own (see [Interjection](./03_epos.md#interjection)). They don't fit into the grammar of the sentence around them the way other parts of speech do.

## Primary vs. secondary interjections

- **Primary interjections** — words that exist *only* to be interjections; they have no other grammatical job.
  - Example: **oh**, **ah**, **wow**, **ouch**, **ugh**, **phew**, **hmm**, **shh**
- **Secondary interjections** — ordinary words (nouns, adjectives, adverbs) that get borrowed to work as an interjection.
  - Example: **well!**, **right!**, **no!**, **nonsense!**, **good!**, **congratulations!**

## Interjections by feeling

| Feeling | Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Joy / excitement | wow, yay, hooray | **Yay!** We won! |
| Surprise | oh, what, really | **What!** I didn't expect that. |
| Pain | ouch, ow | **Ouch!** That hurt. |
| Disgust | ugh, yuck, ew | **Ugh**, this smells terrible. |
| Greeting | hi, hey, hello | **Hey!** Long time no see. |
| Approval / agreement | yes, bravo, well done | **Bravo!** That was a great show. |
| Attention / command | hey, look, shh | **Shh!** The baby is sleeping. |
| Hesitation | um, er, uh | **Um**, I'm not sure about that. |
| Farewell | bye, so long | **Bye!** See you tomorrow. |

## Punctuation with interjections

The punctuation after an interjection signals how strong the feeling is:

- **Strong feeling** → exclamation mark, and it often stands as its own sentence.
  - **Wow!** That's amazing.
- **Mild feeling** → comma, folded into the sentence that follows.
  - **Well,** I guess we should leave.
