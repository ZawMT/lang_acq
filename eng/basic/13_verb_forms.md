# Verb Forms

A **form** is simply the shape a verb takes as a word. English verbs have 5 forms. This is different from a **tense** (covered in [Tenses](./14_tenses.md)), which is how forms combine with auxiliary verbs to show time and aspect — students often mix the two up, so keep this distinction in mind: *form* = the word's shape, *tense* = how that shape is used in a sentence.

## The 5 verb forms

| Form | Also called | walk (regular) | go (irregular) | be (irregular) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base form | dictionary form, infinitive (without "to") | walk | go | be |
| 3rd person singular present | "-s" form | walks | goes | is |
| Present participle | "-ing" form | walking | going | being |
| Past simple | past form | walked | went | was / were |
| Past participle | | walked | gone | been |

### 3rd person singular present ("-s" form)

The base form is used for every subject **except** 3rd person singular (he/she/it), which needs the "-s" form instead. This isn't about singular vs. plural the way nouns work — "I", "you", "we", and "they" all use the plain base form.

| Subject | Verb form used | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | base form | I **go**, they **go** |
| he / she / it | "-s" form | she **goes** |

- Sentence: **I go** to work by bus, but **she goes** by car.

## Regular vs. irregular verbs

| Type | Definition | Past simple | Past participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular verb | past forms are built by adding "-ed" | walked | walked |
| Irregular verb | past forms don't follow the "-ed" rule and must be memorized | went | gone |

| Base form | Past simple | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| walk | walked | walked |
| go | went | gone |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| see | saw | seen |
| be | was / were | been |
| have | had | had |
| do | did | done |

## Why forms matter

Each of the 12 tenses in [Tenses](./14_tenses.md) is just a specific combination of these forms with the auxiliary verbs be/have/do:

- Present continuous = **is/am/are** + present participle → "She **is going**."
- Present perfect = **has/have** + past participle → "She **has gone**."
- Past simple = past simple form alone → "She **went**."
