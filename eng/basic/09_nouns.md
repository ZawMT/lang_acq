# Nouns

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea (see [Noun](./03_epos.md#noun)). Nouns can be classified in several independent ways — a single noun can belong to more than one category at once (e.g. "team" is both common and collective).

## Common vs. proper nouns

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Common noun | a general name for any member of a class, not capitalized | dog, city, book |
| Proper noun | the specific name of one particular person, place, or thing, always capitalized | Rex, London, the Bible |

- Sentence: The **dog** ran to **London**.

## Countable vs. uncountable nouns

This axis is about grammar: can it be counted and made plural?

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Countable noun | can be counted, has a singular and plural form, can follow "a/an" or a number | one apple, two apples |
| Uncountable noun | can't be counted or pluralized, doesn't follow "a/an", uses "much/little" instead of "many/few" | water, rice, information |

- Sentence: I have **three apples**, but only **a little water**.

Two well-known named subtypes of uncountable nouns:

- **Material nouns** — name a substance or material something is made from.
  - Example: **gold**, **silver**, **wood**, **cotton**, **sugar**
  - Sentence: This ring is made of **gold**.
- **Abstract nouns** — name a quality, feeling, or idea rather than a physical thing (see below). Most abstract nouns are uncountable, though a few are countable (e.g. "an idea", "a plan").
  - Example: **honesty**, **happiness**, **freedom**
  - Sentence: **Honesty** is the best policy.

## Concrete vs. abstract nouns

This axis is about meaning: can you experience it with your senses?

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete noun | names something physical you can see, hear, touch, taste, or smell | table, dog, music |
| Abstract noun | names a quality, feeling, idea, or state that has no physical form | love, courage, freedom |

- Sentence: Her **courage** helped her climb the **mountain**.

## Collective nouns

A collective noun names a group of people, animals, or things treated as a single unit. It's countable (a team, two teams), but grammatically singular when referring to the group as a whole.

| Collective noun | Group of |
|---|---|
| team | players |
| family | relatives |
| herd | cattle/elephants |
| flock | birds/sheep |
| jury | jurors |
| staff | employees |

- Sentence: The **team** **is** playing well this season.

## Compound nouns

A compound noun is made of two or more words joined together to name one thing. They can be written as one word, two words, or hyphenated.

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| One word | toothbrush, sunrise, blackboard |
| Two words | swimming pool, credit card |
| Hyphenated | mother-in-law, check-in |

- Sentence: She bought a new **toothbrush** and a **swimming pool** membership.
