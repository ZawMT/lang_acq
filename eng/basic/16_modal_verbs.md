# Modal Verbs

A modal verb is a special kind of auxiliary verb (see [Main vs. auxiliary verbs](./12_verbs_overview.md#main-vs-auxiliary-verbs)) that adds meaning like ability, possibility, permission, or obligation to a main verb. Modal verbs never change form (no "-s", "-ing", or "-ed") and are always followed by a base-form verb.

## The core modal verbs

| Modal | Main use | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| can | ability, permission (informal) | She **can** swim. |
| could | past ability, polite request, possibility | **Could** you help me? |
| may | permission (formal), possibility | You **may** leave now. |
| might | weak possibility | It **might** rain later. |
| shall | formal future, suggestion (British) | **Shall** we begin? |
| should | advice, expectation | You **should** see a doctor. |
| will | future, willingness | I **will** call you tomorrow. |
| would | polite request, hypothetical, past habit | **Would** you like some tea? |
| must | strong obligation, logical certainty | You **must** wear a seatbelt. |

## Semi-modals

These aren't true modals grammatically (they change form and use "to"), but they do a similar job and are often taught alongside modals.

| Semi-modal | Main use | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| have to | obligation | I **have to** finish this today. |
| need to | necessity | You **need to** rest. |
| used to | past habit that no longer happens | I **used to** live in Paris. |
| ought to | advice, similar to "should" | You **ought to** apologize. |

## Modals of certainty (degree of confidence)

| Confidence | Modal | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Certain | must | She **must** be home — the lights are on. |
| Likely | should | She **should** be home by now. |
| Possible | may / might / could | She **may** be home already. |
| Unlikely | might not | She **might not** be home yet. |
| Impossible | can't | She **can't** be home — I just saw her at work. |
