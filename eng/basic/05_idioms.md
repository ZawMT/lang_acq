# Idioms and Fixed Expressions

These are groups of words that native speakers use as a ready-made unit, rather than building fresh from grammar rules each time. See also [Expression](./04_sentence_structure.md#expression) for how this fits alongside words, phrases, clauses, and sentences.

## Idioms

An idiom is a phrase whose meaning **can't be worked out from the individual words**. You have to learn the whole phrase as one unit.

| Idiom | Meaning | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| break the ice | do/say something to ease tension | He told a joke to **break the ice**. |
| kick the bucket | to die | The old dog finally **kicked the bucket**. |
| under the weather | feeling slightly ill | I'm **under the weather** today. |
| once in a blue moon | very rarely | We only meet **once in a blue moon**. |
| hit the sack | go to bed | It's late, I'm going to **hit the sack**. |
| spill the beans | reveal a secret | Don't **spill the beans** about the party. |

## Fixed expressions

A fixed expression is a set phrase that **is** understandable from its words, but is still used as a fixed formula — especially for politeness, opinion, or connecting ideas. Unlike idioms, you could technically guess the meaning; you just wouldn't naturally phrase it any other way.

| Expression | Function | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| if I may | polite request to say/do something | **If I may**, I'd like to add one point. |
| by the way | introduce a side note | **By the way**, did you call her back? |
| as far as I know | soften a statement's certainty | **As far as I know**, the shop is open. |
| to be honest | signal a frank opinion | **To be honest**, I didn't like the film. |
| for what it's worth | offer an opinion humbly | **For what it's worth**, I think you're right. |
| in other words | rephrase for clarity | She's busy — **in other words**, no. |

## Collocations

A related but distinct idea: a collocation is simply two or more words that habitually go together, even though each word keeps its normal meaning. Getting these "wrong pairings" is a common sign of a non-native speaker, even when the grammar is correct.

- **make** a decision *(not "do a decision")*
- **heavy** rain *(not "strong rain")*
- **take** a photo *(not "make a photo")*
