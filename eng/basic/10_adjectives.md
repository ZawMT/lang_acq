# Adjectives

An adjective describes or gives more information about a noun (see [Adjective](./03_epos.md#adjective)). Several word groups we've already covered elsewhere can also act as adjectives when they sit directly in front of a noun — this file focuses on the two genuinely new types (descriptive and proper), plus degrees of comparison, and cross-links the rest instead of repeating them.

## Descriptive (qualitative) adjectives

These describe a quality of the noun — size, age, shape, color, or opinion.

| Category | Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Size | big, small, tiny, huge | It's a **huge** house. |
| Age | old, young, new, ancient | She has an **ancient** vase. |
| Shape | round, square, flat | The table is **round**. |
| Color | red, blue, green | He wore a **red** shirt. |
| Opinion / quality | pretty, ugly, kind, boring | She is very **kind**. |

### Origin, purpose, and material

These describe where something is from, what it's for, or what it's made of. English often expresses this two ways: a true adjective, or simply a noun placed in front of another noun to act like one (a "noun adjunct" — see also [Compound nouns](./09_nouns.md#compound-nouns), since these overlap).

| Category | True adjective example | Noun-as-adjective example |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | **Burmese** ruby | **Burma** ruby |
| Purpose | — | **coffee** table |
| Material | **wooden** chair | **bamboo** wall |

## Degrees of comparison

Most descriptive adjectives can change form to compare things.

| Degree | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | describes one thing, no comparison | tall |
| Comparative | compares two things (+ "-er" or "more") | taller / more beautiful |
| Superlative | compares three or more things (+ "-est" or "most") | tallest / most beautiful |

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |

- Sentence: This mountain is **high**, that one is **higher**, but Everest is the **highest**.

## Proper adjectives

A proper adjective is formed from a proper noun, and stays capitalized.

| Proper noun | Proper adjective | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| America | American | I like **American** football. |
| France | French | She bought a **French** pastry. |
| Shakespeare | Shakespearean | It has a **Shakespearean** tragedy feel. |
| Victoria (era) | Victorian | They live in a **Victorian** house. |

## Other adjective types (cross-links)

These words are covered in detail elsewhere, since they're the same words listed as determiners or pronouns — they simply act as adjectives when placed directly before a noun, and as pronouns when standing alone.

- **Quantitative adjectives** — some, any, much, many, few, little, all, no, several.
  - Adjective: **Some** apples are ripe. → Pronoun: **Some** are ripe.
  - See [Indefinite pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#indefinite-pronouns) and [Beyond the eight parts](./03_epos.md#beyond-the-eight-parts).
- **Demonstrative adjectives** — this, that, these, those.
  - Adjective: **This** book is mine. → Pronoun: **This** is mine.
  - See [Demonstrative pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#demonstrative-pronouns).
- **Possessive adjectives** — my, your, his, her, its, our, their.
  - Adjective: **My** book is here. → Pronoun: **Mine** is here.
  - See [Possessive pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#possessive-pronouns).
- **Interrogative adjectives** — which, what, whose.
  - Adjective: **Whose** book is this? → Pronoun: **Whose** is this?
  - See [Interrogative pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#interrogative-pronouns).
