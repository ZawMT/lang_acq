# Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a verb combined with a **particle** (an adverb or preposition, or both) to create a meaning that's often completely different from the individual words (see [Particles](./03_epos.md#beyond-the-eight-parts)).

- Sentence: Please **give up** smoking. (not related to "give" or "up" separately)

## Types of phrasal verbs

### Intransitive (no object)

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| wake up | stop sleeping | I **wake up** at 7 AM. |
| break down | stop working | The car **broke down**. |
| show up | arrive | She **showed up** late. |

### Transitive, separable (object can go in the middle or after)

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| turn off | stop a device | **Turn off** the light. / **Turn** the light **off**. |
| give up | quit | **Give up** smoking. / **Give** smoking **up**. |
| find out | discover | **Find out** the truth. / **Find** the truth **out**. |

Note: if the object is a pronoun (it, him, her, them), it **must** go in the middle: "Turn **it** off." (not "Turn off it.")

### Transitive, inseparable (object must go after)

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| look after | take care of | She **looks after** the children. |
| run into | meet by chance | I **ran into** an old friend. |
| get over | recover from | He **got over** his cold. |

### Three-word phrasal verbs (always inseparable)

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| look forward to | anticipate with pleasure | I **look forward to** seeing you. |
| get along with | have a good relationship with | She **gets along with** everyone. |
| put up with | tolerate | I can't **put up with** this noise. |

## Literal vs. idiomatic phrasal verbs

Some phrasal verbs keep a literal, guessable meaning, while most are idiomatic (meaning can't be guessed from the words):

- Literal: **Sit down**. (sit + in a downward direction)
- Idiomatic: **Break down** = stop functioning (has nothing to do with physically breaking)
