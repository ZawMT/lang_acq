# Eight Parts of Speech

Every word in an English sentence plays one of eight roles, called parts of speech.

## Noun

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea.

- Example: **dog**, **London**, **happiness**
- Sentence: The **dog** ran into the **park**.

## Pronoun

A pronoun replaces a noun, so we don't have to repeat it.

- Example: **he**, **she**, **it**, **they**, **this**
- Sentence: Maria is tired, so **she** is resting.

## Verb

A verb shows an action or a state of being.

- Example: **run**, **eat**, **is**, **think**
- Sentence: Birds **fly** south in winter.

## Adjective

An adjective describes or gives more information about a noun.

- Example: **happy**, **blue**, **tall**
- Sentence: She has a **red** car.

## Adverb

An adverb describes or gives more information about a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It often answers how, when, where, or to what degree.

- Example: **quickly**, **very**, **yesterday**
- Sentence: He ran **quickly** to the bus stop.

## Preposition

A preposition shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and other words in a sentence, often about place, time, or direction.

- Example: **in**, **on**, **under**, **after**
- Sentence: The cat is **under** the table.

## Conjunction

A conjunction joins words, phrases, or sentences together.

- Example: **and**, **but**, **or**, **because**
- Sentence: I wanted tea **but** we only had coffee.

## Interjection

An interjection expresses a sudden feeling or emotion, and often stands on its own.

- Example: **Wow!**, **Ouch!**, **Hey!**
- Sentence: **Wow!** That's amazing.

## Beyond the eight parts

The "eight parts of speech" model is centuries old and doesn't cleanly fit every English word. A few categories are usually squeezed into the eight parts even though they behave differently, so modern grammar treats them separately:

- **Determiners** — a, an, the, this, that, my, some, every. Traditionally called adjectives, but unlike real adjectives they can't be modified ("very the") or compared ("the-er").
  - Sentence: **The** dog chased **a** ball.
- **Auxiliary / modal verbs** — is, have, will, can, must, should. Traditionally called verbs, but they carry grammar (tense, questions, negation) rather than meaning.
  - Sentence: **Do** you **have** a pen?
- **Particles** — up, out, off in phrasal verbs like "give **up**" or "look **out**". They look like adverbs/prepositions but form a fixed unit with the verb.
  - Sentence: Please **turn off** the light.
- **Numerals** — one, two, first, second. Traditionally called adjectives, but they follow their own ordering and can't take "very."
  - Sentence: She finished in **first** place.
- **Expletives (dummy words)** — "it" and "there" when they don't refer to anything real, just fill a required grammar slot.
  - Sentence: **It** is raining. **There** is a cat outside.
