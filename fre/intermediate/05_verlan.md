# Verlan

**Verlan** is a French slang word-formation process that flips a word's syllables around — it's one of the best-known forms of French *argot* (slang), especially associated with youth culture in the *banlieues* (suburbs) since the 1970s–80s, and spread further through hip-hop and cinema. The name is itself an example: **verlan** is the verlan form of **l'envers** ("backwards" / "the reverse").

## How it works

The core mechanic is syllable inversion — split the word into syllable-like chunks and reverse their order, often adjusting the result (adding a schwa, dropping a final vowel) so it's actually pronounceable.

| Original word | Meaning | Verlan form | How it flips |
|---|---|---|---|
| femme | woman | **meuf** | fa-**me** (schwa added) → **me**-fa → meuf |
| métro | subway | **tromé** | mé-tro → tro-mé |
| louche | shady, weird | **chelou** | lou-che (schwa added) → che-lou |
| lourd | annoying, heavy | **relou** | lour-d (schwa added) → reu-lour → relou |
| fou | crazy | **ouf** | a simple two-sound flip: f-ou → ou-f |

Not every verlan word follows the pattern this cleanly — some (like **beur**, below) are compressed or irregular rather than a tidy syllable swap.

## Double verlan (re-verlanization)

Verlan doubles as an evolving in-group code, and once a verlan word becomes too well-known outside its original speakers, it sometimes gets **verlanized a second time** to restore its exclusivity.

- **arabe** → **beur** (the first major verlan word to reach wide public awareness, historically tied to the 1983 *Marche pour l'égalité et contre le racisme* — a term of self-identification for children of North African immigrants in France) → **beur** itself later got re-verlanized into **rebeu**, once *beur* had become common knowledge.

This cycle — coin a word, watch it go mainstream, re-flip it — is a genuine, ongoing feature of verlan, not a one-off historical curiosity.

## Function: identity and in-group code

Verlan originated partly as a way for young people in immigrant and working-class communities to speak among themselves without being easily understood by outsiders — parents, teachers, police. Over time, though, many verlan words crossed all the way into mainstream colloquial French: **meuf**, **teuf**, **keuf**, **ouf**, **chelou**, and **relou** are now used by French speakers of every background and even appear in standard dictionaries (the *Petit Robert* included several of these years ago). That mainstreaming is itself part of why the argot keeps moving — a code that everyone understands has stopped functioning as a code.

## Is verlan only for words? Can it apply to sentences?

**Verlan is fundamentally a word-level (lexical) process, not a sentence-level (syntactic) one.** It never reorders the words *within* a sentence, and it doesn't touch grammar — only specific vocabulary items get swapped for their flipped form, while the sentence's word order, verb conjugation, and agreement stay completely ordinary French.

- Sentence: **Cette meuf est vraiment chelou.** (This girl is really weird.) — *meuf* and *chelou* are verlanized, but the sentence structure ("this [noun] is really [adjective]") is standard French, untouched.

**Even fixed multi-word expressions typically verlanize only one word inside them, not the phrase as a whole.** The classic example is **laisse béton**, from **laisse tomber** ("drop it" / "forget it") — the title of a famous 1977 song by Renaud. Only **tomber** gets flipped (tom-bé → bé-tom → béton); **laisse** is left completely alone.

So there's no such thing as a "verlan sentence" the way there might be a Pig Latin sentence built by a single mechanical rule applied to everything. You can build a sentence out of nothing but verlanized vocabulary, but underneath, its grammar is ordinary French the whole way through.

### Function words are essentially immune

Verlan targets **content words** — nouns, adjectives, occasionally verbs — almost never the small grammatical words (articles, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions) that hold a sentence's structure together. This is exactly why verlan vocabulary can slot into normal sentences without breaking them: the sentence's "skeleton" is left alone, and only specific content-word slots get swapped out.

## Some verlan words now in everyday French

| Verlan | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| meuf | femme | woman, girl |
| keuf | flic | cop |
| teuf | fête | party |
| ouf | fou | crazy, wild (also "amazing") |
| chelou | louche | weird, shady |
| relou | lourd | annoying |
| vénère | énervé | angry, annoyed |
| beur / rebeu | arabe | person of North African descent (self-identifying term) |
