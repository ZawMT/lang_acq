# Prepositions and Conjunctions (Prépositions et conjonctions)

Prepositions and conjunctions are both **function words** (*mots-outils*) — a small, fixed ("closed class") set of words whose job is to connect and relate other words, rather than carry meaning on their own. See [Préposition](./03_classes.md#préposition-preposition) and [Conjonction](./03_classes.md#conjonction-conjunction) for their basic definitions.

## Prépositions

A preposition shows how a *nom* (or pronoun) relates to the rest of the *phrase* — usually place, time, direction, or another relationship.

### Lieu (place)

| Préposition | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| dans | Les clés sont **dans** le tiroir. (The keys are in the drawer.) |
| sur | Le livre est **sur** la table. (The book is on the table.) |
| à | Elle attend **à** la porte. (She's waiting at the door.) |
| sous | Le chat est **sous** le lit. (The cat is under the bed.) |
| au-dessus de | L'avion a volé **au-dessus des** nuages. (The plane flew above the clouds.) |
| entre | Le parc est **entre** deux rues. (The park is between two streets.) |
| parmi | Elle se tenait **parmi** la foule. (She was standing among the crowd.) |
| derrière | La voiture est garée **derrière** la maison. (The car is parked behind the house.) |
| à côté de | Il s'est assis **à côté de** moi. (He sat next to me.) |

### Temps (time)

| Préposition | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| à | On se voit **à** 17h. (We'll meet at 5 PM.) |
| le | La fête est **le** vendredi. (The party is on Friday.) |
| en | Elle est née **en** 1990. (She was born in 1990.) |
| avant | Appelle-moi **avant** midi. (Call me before noon.) |
| après | Nous sommes partis **après** le dîner. (We left after dinner.) |
| pendant | Il a plu **pendant** le voyage. (It rained during the trip.) |
| depuis | Je suis ici **depuis** le matin. (I've been here since morning.) |
| jusqu'à | Elle restera **jusqu'à** dimanche. (She'll stay until Sunday.) |

### Direction / mouvement

| Préposition | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| à | Elle a marché **à** la boutique. (She walked to the shop.) |
| dans | Il a sauté **dans** la piscine. (He jumped into the pool.) |
| sur | Le chat a grimpé **sur** le toit. (The cat climbed onto the roof.) |
| à travers | Nous avons conduit **à travers** le tunnel. (We drove through the tunnel.) |
| à travers / de l'autre côté de | Ils ont traversé la rivière **à la nage**. (They swam across the river.) |
| le long de | Nous avons marché **le long de** la plage. (We walked along the beach.) |
| vers | Le chien a couru **vers** nous. (The dog ran towards us.) |

### Autres relations

| Préposition | Relationship | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| par | agent | Le livre a été écrit **par** elle. (The book was written by her.) |
| avec | instrument / accompaniment | J'ai mangé **avec** une fourchette. Elle est venue **avec** sa sœur. |
| comme | comparison | Il chante **comme** un professionnel. (He sings like a professional.) |
| de | topic | Nous avons parlé **de** la météo. (We talked about the weather.) |

## Conjonctions

A conjunction joins *mots*, *groupes*, or *propositions* together.

### Conjonctions de coordination (coordinating)

Join two equal, independent parts. French has exactly seven, traditionally remembered with the mnemonic sentence **"Mais où est donc Ornicar ?"** ("But where is Ornicar, then?") — each word of the mnemonic is one of the conjunctions.

| Conjunction | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| mais | C'est petit **mais** confortable. (It's small but comfortable.) |
| ou | Tu veux du thé **ou** du café ? (Do you want tea or coffee?) |
| et | J'aime le thé **et** le café. (I like tea and coffee.) |
| donc | Il pleuvait, **donc** nous sommes restés. (It was raining, so we stayed.) |
| or | Il devait venir ; **or**, il n'est jamais arrivé. (He was supposed to come; yet/however, he never showed up.) |
| ni | Il ne fume **ni** ne boit. (He doesn't smoke nor drink.) |
| car | Elle est partie tôt, **car** elle était fatiguée. (She left early, for she was tired.) |

### Conjonctions de subordination (subordinating)

Join a *proposition subordonnée* to a *proposition principale* (see [Proposition](./04_sentence_structure.md#proposition-clause)).

| Conjunction | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| parce que | Elle est partie tôt **parce qu'**elle était fatiguée. (She left early because she was tired.) |
| bien que | **Bien qu'**il ait plu, nous sommes sortis. (Although it rained, we went out.) |
| si | Je viendrai **si** j'ai le temps. (I'll come if I have time.) |
| quand | Appelle-moi **quand** tu arrives. (Call me when you arrive.) |
| puisque | **Puisque** tu demandes, je vais expliquer. (Since you asked, I'll explain.) |
| à moins que | Nous n'irons pas **à moins qu'**il **ne** s'arrête de pleuvoir. (We won't go unless it stops raining.) |
| pendant que | Elle lisait **pendant qu'**il cuisinait. (She read while he cooked.) |
| alors que / tandis que | Il est grand, **alors qu'**elle est petite. (He's tall, whereas she's short.) |

### Locutions conjonctives corrélatives (correlative)

Work in pairs to join equal parts.

| Conjunction pair | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| soit...soit / ou...ou | **Soit** tu pars, **soit** tu restes. (Either you go or you stay.) |
| ni...ni | **Ni** le chat **ni** le chien n'est là. (Neither the cat nor the dog is here.) |
| et...et | **Et** lui **et** elle ont accepté. (Both he and she agreed.) |
| non seulement...mais aussi | Elle est **non seulement** intelligente **mais aussi** gentille. (She is not only smart but also kind.) |
| que...ou (que) | Je ne sais pas si je dois rire **ou** pleurer. (I don't know whether to laugh or cry.) |

## Points to note for French

### 1. Prepositions contract with the definite article

**à** and **de** fuse with **le** and **les** — you can never write them side by side.

| | + le | + les |
|---|---|---|
| à | **au** | **aux** |
| de | **du** | **des** |

- Je vais **au** marché. (I'm going to the market — never *"à le marché."*)
- Elle parle **des** enfants. (She's talking about the children — never *"de les enfants."*)

*à + la* and *à + l'* (and *de + la* / *de + l'*) don't contract — only the masculine/plural forms do.

### 2. Place prepositions depend on the place name's gender

Unlike English "to/in Paris," "to/in France," "to/in the USA" (all just "to/in"), French preposition choice for countries depends on gender and number:

- **à** — cities: **à** Paris, **à** Tokyo.
- **en** — feminine countries (most countries ending in *-e*): **en** France, **en** Chine.
- **au** — masculine countries: **au** Japon, **au** Canada.
- **aux** — plural-named countries: **aux** États-Unis, **aux** Pays-Bas.

### 3. English "for"/"since"/"ago" split into four different French words

This is one of the most common time-preposition errors for English speakers, because English's "for" covers ground that French splits by whether the action is finished, ongoing, or being measured backward:

| French | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **depuis** | ongoing action, started in the past and still true now | J'habite ici **depuis** deux ans. (I've lived here for two years [and still do].) |
| **pendant** | a duration, viewed as a completed/bounded block | J'ai habité là **pendant** deux ans. (I lived there for two years [then stopped].) |
| **pour** | an intended/planned duration, decided in advance | Je pars **pour** deux ans. (I'm leaving for two years [planned].) |
| **il y a** | "ago," measuring back from now | Je suis arrivé **il y a** deux ans. (I arrived two years ago.) |

### 4. *depuis* also means spatial "from," not just time

Beyond time, **depuis** marks a starting point in space too — especially paired with **jusqu'à** ("to/until") to frame a range from one point to another:

- Il a couru **depuis** son bureau **jusqu'à** la gare. (He ran from his office to the station.)
- Je vous écris **depuis** Paris. (I'm writing to you from Paris.)

The nuance versus plain **de** ("from" as simple origin, e.g. *venir de Paris* — "to come from Paris"): *depuis* implies a continuous span or a vantage point (a journey's start, or "I'm currently here, writing"), while *de* just states where something originated, with no sense of continuity.

### 5. "Since" is a false-friend trap across word classes

English "since" covers both a time preposition and a causal conjunction with the same word. French does **not** reuse one word this way:

- **depuis** — time only ("since morning"): Je suis ici **depuis** le matin.
- **puisque** — causal ("since" = because): **Puisque** tu demandes, je vais expliquer.

Using *depuis* to mean "because" (or vice versa) is a direct translation error, not just unidiomatic phrasing.

### 6. Many subordinating conjunctions trigger the subjunctive

Several conjunctions from the table above force the verb in the clause they introduce into the **subjonctif** mood — English has nothing equivalent triggered by "although" or "so that." Common subjunctive-triggers: **bien que**, **quoique** (although), **à moins que** (unless), **pour que** (so that), **avant que** (before), **afin que** (in order that).

- **Bien qu'**il **ait** plu, nous sommes sortis. (*ait* is subjunctive of *avoir*, not indicative *a*.)

### 7. *que* is the productive building block

Notice how many subordinating conjunctions above are literally another word + **que**: *parce **que***, *bien **que***, *pendant **que***, *alors **que***, *tandis **que***, *à moins **que***. This is a genuinely productive pattern in French — when in doubt about how to introduce a subordinate clause with a new nuance, "[preposition/adverb] + que" is usually the shape to reach for, unlike English's more varied, non-formulaic set of subordinators.
