# Adjectives (Les adjectifs)

An *adjectif* describes or gives more information about a *nom* (see [Adjectif](./03_classes.md#adjectif-adjective)). French adjectives share the same basic categories as English ones, but two things make them behave very differently: they must **agree** with the noun they modify, and most of them sit **after** the noun rather than before it.

## Accord de l'adjectif (agreement)

Unlike English, a French adjective changes form to match the **gender** and **number** of the noun it describes — it's not optional, the same way [nouns carry gender](./09_nouns.md#genre-grammatical-grammatical-gender).

| | Masculine sing. | Feminine sing. | Masculine pl. | Feminine pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regular pattern | grand | grand**e** | grand**s** | grand**es** |
| Already ends in -e | jeune | jeune *(no change)* | jeune**s** | jeune**s** |

- Sentence: un **grand** homme, une **grande** femme, des **grands** hommes, des **grandes** femmes.

Several common endings form the feminine irregularly:

| Masculine ending | Feminine ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -eux | -euse | heureux → heureuse |
| -f | -ve | actif → active |
| -on / -en | -onne / -enne | bon → bonne, ancien → ancienne |
| -er | -ère | cher → chère |

**A few very common adjectives — beau, nouveau, vieux — have a *third* masculine form**, used only right before a masculine noun starting with a vowel sound, purely to make the liaison sound right:

| Masc. (consonant) | Masc. (vowel sound) | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| un **beau** jardin | un **bel** homme | une **belle** femme |
| un **nouveau** livre | un **nouvel** ami | une **nouvelle** voiture |
| un **vieux** chien | un **vieil** arbre | une **vieille** maison |

## Adjectifs descriptifs (qualitative adjectives)

These describe a quality of the noun — size, age, shape, color, or opinion. (Shown here in the base masculine singular form; remember each one still has to agree, per above.)

| Category | Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Taille (size) | grand, petit, minuscule, énorme | C'est une **énorme** maison. |
| Âge (age) | vieux, jeune, nouveau, ancien | Elle a un vase **ancien**. |
| Forme (shape) | rond, carré, plat | La table est **ronde**. |
| Couleur (color) | rouge, bleu, vert | Il portait une chemise **rouge**. |
| Opinion / qualité | joli, laid, gentil, ennuyeux | Elle est très **gentille**. |

## Position: before or after the noun

**Most French descriptive adjectives go *after* the noun** — the opposite of English's default. A small, memorable set of short, common adjectives goes *before* instead, often grouped under the mnemonic **BAGS** (Beauty, Age, Goodness, Size):

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Beauty | beau/belle, joli/jolie |
| Age | jeune, vieux/vieille, nouveau/nouvelle |
| Goodness | bon/bonne, mauvais/mauvaise |
| Size | grand/grande, petit/petite, gros/grosse |

- Before (BAGS): Elle a une **belle** maison. (She has a beautiful house.)
- After (default): Elle a une maison **confortable**. (She has a comfortable house.)

**Several adjectives change meaning depending on which side of the noun they sit on** — this trips up learners more than the general before/after rule itself:

| Adjective | Before noun | After noun |
|---|---|---|
| ancien | former — un **ancien** professeur (a former teacher) | old/ancient — un tableau **ancien** (an old painting) |
| grand | great/important — un **grand** homme (a great man) | tall — un homme **grand** (a tall man) |
| pauvre | pitiable — un **pauvre** homme (a poor, pitiable man) | penniless — un homme **pauvre** (a man with no money) |
| propre | own — mon **propre** livre (my own book) | clean — un livre **propre** (a clean book) |
| cher | dear/beloved — mon **cher** ami (my dear friend) | expensive — un livre **cher** (an expensive book) |
| dernier | final in a series — la **dernière** semaine (the final week [of a sequence]) | most recent — la semaine **dernière** (last week, the one just past) |
| même | same — la **même** chose (the same thing) | itself/very — la vérité **même** (the truth itself) |
| seul | only — le **seul** livre (the only book) | alone — un homme **seul** (a man alone) |

## Origin, purpose, and material

English often turns a noun into an adjective just by placing it in front of another noun (a "coffee table," a "bamboo wall" — see [Compound nouns](./09_nouns.md#noms-composés-compound-nouns)). **French almost never does this.** Instead, it links the two nouns with **en** (material) or **de** (origin/purpose), and true origin/material adjectives — when they exist — still follow the default after-the-noun rule.

| Category | French pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Origin (true adjective) | noun + adjective | un rubis **birman** (a Burmese ruby) |
| Purpose | noun + **de** + noun | une table **de** cuisine (a kitchen table) |
| Material | noun + **en**/**de** + noun | un mur **en** bambou (a bamboo wall), une chaise **en** bois (a wooden chair) |

## Degrees of comparison (Les degrés de comparaison)

French doesn't have an "-er"/"-est" ending at all — comparison is always built with a separate word in front of the adjective.

| Degree | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative (more) | **plus** + adjective + **que** | Cette montagne est **plus haute que** celle-là. |
| Comparative (as) | **aussi** + adjective + **que** | Cette montagne est **aussi haute que** celle-là. |
| Comparative (less) | **moins** + adjective + **que** | Cette montagne est **moins haute que** celle-là. |
| Superlative | **le/la/les plus** (or **moins**) + adjective | C'est la montagne **la plus haute du monde**. |

The superlative's article (*le/la/les*) still has to agree with the noun's gender and number, just like the adjective itself.

A few common adjectives are irregular, the same way English "good → better" doesn't follow "-er":

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| bon(ne) (good) | **meilleur(e)** *(never "plus bon")* | le/la **meilleur(e)** |
| mauvais(e) (bad) | **pire** / plus mauvais(e) | le/la **pire** / le/la plus mauvais(e) |

## Proper adjectives (Adjectifs de nationalité)

Formed from a proper noun — but unlike English, **they stay lowercase**, matching the [same rule already noted for nouns](./09_nouns.md#noms-communs-vs-noms-propres-common-vs-proper-nouns): the person/nationality-as-noun is capitalized, but the same word used as an adjective is not.

| Proper noun (capitalized) | Proper adjective (lowercase) | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| la France | français(e) | J'aime la pâtisserie **française**. |
| l'Amérique | américain(e) | J'aime le football **américain**. |
| Shakespeare | shakespearien(ne) | Ça a un ton **shakespearien**. |
| Victoria (époque) | victorien(ne) | Ils vivent dans une maison **victorienne**. |

## Other adjective types (cross-links)

These words double as *déterminants* when they sit before a noun, and as pronouns when they stand alone — already covered in detail elsewhere, so this just ties the two together.

- **Adjectifs possessifs** (mon, ta, ses...) vs. **pronoms possessifs** (le mien, la tienne...) — different words in French, unlike English's my/mine pair. See [Possessive pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#possessive-pronouns-pronoms-possessifs).
- **Adjectifs démonstratifs** (ce, cette, ces) vs. **pronoms démonstratifs** (celui, celle...). See [Demonstrative pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#demonstrative-pronouns-pronoms-démonstratifs).
- **Adjectifs interrogatifs** (quel, quelle...) vs. **pronoms interrogatifs** (lequel, laquelle...). See [Interrogative pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#interrogative-pronouns-pronoms-interrogatifs).
- **Adjectifs indéfinis** (chaque, quelques, certains...) vs. **pronoms indéfinis** (chacun, quelqu'un...). See [Indefinite pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#indefinite-pronouns-pronoms-indéfinis).
