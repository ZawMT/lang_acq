# Non-finite Verbs (Les formes non conjuguées)

A **finite** verb is tied to a subject and a tense — "elle **parle**," "elle **a parlé**." A **non-finite** verb doesn't show tense or agree with a subject; it acts more like a noun, adjective, or adverb inside the sentence. English has three kinds (gerund, infinitive, participle); **French has four** — it splits English's "-ing" job across two different forms depending on function, rather than using one form for everything the way English does.

## Infinitif

The dictionary form — and, crucially, **French's stand-in for the English gerund** wherever English would use a noun-like "-ing."

| Function | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| As a noun (subject) | **Nager** est un bon exercice. (Swimming is good exercise.) |
| As a noun (object) | J'aime **lire**. (I enjoy reading.) |
| After a preposition | Avant de **manger**, lave-toi les mains. (Before eating, wash your hands.) |

**This is the single biggest transfer trap between English and French non-finite forms.** English reaches for a gerund as a noun or after a preposition; French reaches for the **infinitive** in both spots — never a participe présent. "Swimming is good exercise" is **Nager** est un bon exercice, never *"Nageant est..."* And after *any* preposition except **en** (see Gérondif, below), French uses the infinitive where English would use "-ing": *avant de manger* (before eating), *sans parler* (without speaking) — never *"avant de mangeant"* or *"sans parlant."*

## Participe présent

The **-ant** form. It exists, but it's used far more narrowly than English "-ing" — mainly as an adjective, and as the building block of the gérondif below. It is **not** used to build a continuous tense the way "-ing" is in English (see [why French has no grammaticalized continuous aspect](./12_verbs_overview.md#action-vs-stative-verbs-verbes-daction-vs-détat)).

- Adjective use: un film **intéressant** (an interesting film), des histoires **intéressantes** (interesting stories — agrees in number, and some participes présents even have a distinct feminine adjective spelling: fatigant → fatigante).

## Gérondif

**En** + participe présent — a genuine fourth category with no single-word English equivalent. It functions adverbially, expressing manner, simultaneity, or cause: roughly "while/by/through doing something."

- Sentence: Elle chante **en conduisant**. (She sings while driving.)
- Sentence: Il a appris le français **en regardant** des films. (He learned French by watching movies.)

The **en** is what turns a bare participe présent into this adverbial gérondif — drop it, and *conduisant* on its own reads as a plain adjective or a more literary, self-standing clause, not "while driving."

## Participe passé

Same job as the English past participle: builds [compound tenses](./12_verbs_overview.md#temps-simples-vs-temps-composés-simple-vs-compound-tenses) with avoir/être, and can act as an adjective — agreeing in gender/number when it does.

- Compound tense: Elle **a fini** le travail. (She finished the work.)
- Adjective: la porte **fermée** (the closed door), un travail **fini** (finished work).

## Which link (if any) connects a verb to a following infinitive

English's classic memorization problem — does this verb take a gerund, an infinitive, or either? — has a direct French parallel, just reshaped: since French almost always uses the **infinitive** after a conjugated verb, the real question is **which preposition (if any) introduces it**.

| Pattern | Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| verb + infinitive directly (no preposition) | vouloir, pouvoir, devoir, aimer, détester | Elle **veut partir**. (She wants to leave.) |
| verb + **de** + infinitive | décider de, essayer de, arrêter de, finir de | Elle **décide de partir**. (She decides to leave.) |
| verb + **à** + infinitive | commencer à, aider à, réussir à, apprendre à | Elle **commence à comprendre**. (She's starting to understand.) |

There's no reliable shortcut for which group a given verb falls into — like English's gerund/infinitive split, it has to be learned per verb.
