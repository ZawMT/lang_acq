# Mood and Conditionals (Le mode et les conditionnelles)

[Verbs Overview](./12_verbs_overview.md#les-quatre-modes-the-four-moods) already previewed French's four moods — indicatif, subjonctif, conditionnel, impératif. This file details the two that need real depth: the **subjonctif**, which is a fully alive, fully conjugated mood in French (unlike English's nearly-extinct subjunctive), and the **conditionnel**, both as a mood and as the backbone of *si*-conditional sentences.

## Le subjonctif, in depth

English's subjunctive survives in only two narrow spots ("I suggest that he **be** on time," "if I **were** you"). **French's subjonctif is a complete, productive mood with its own endings for every verb**, used constantly in ordinary speech — not a fossil.

### When it's triggered

Mainly after **que**, following verbs/expressions of doubt, desire, necessity, or emotion — this is the same trigger list already flagged in [Prepositions and Conjunctions](./08_prepositions_conjunctions.md#6-many-subordinating-conjunctions-trigger-the-subjunctive):

- Necessity: **il faut que**, **il est nécessaire que**
- Desire: **vouloir que**, **souhaiter que**
- Doubt: **douter que**
- Emotion: **être content/triste/surpris que**
- Certain conjunctions: **bien que**, **quoique**, **avant que**, **pour que**, **à moins que**

### Conjugation

Present subjonctif endings are largely the same across verbs (-e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent), attached to a stem — note that the **nous/vous** forms borrow their shape from the imparfait, a useful pattern to recognize rather than memorize as arbitrary.

| Person | parler (subjonctif présent) |
|---|---|
| que je | parl**e** |
| que tu | parl**es** |
| qu'il / elle / on | parl**e** |
| que nous | parl**ions** |
| que vous | parl**iez** |
| qu'ils / elles | parl**ent** |

- Sentence: Il faut que tu **partes**. (You have to leave.)
- Sentence: Je doute qu'elle **vienne**. (I doubt she's coming.)
- Sentence: Bien qu'il **pleuve**, nous sortons. (Although it's raining, we're going out.)

There's also a compound **subjonctif passé** (avoir/être in the subjonctif + participe passé) for a completed action in a subjunctive context: *Je suis content qu'elle **soit arrivée**.* (I'm glad she arrived.)

Two more subjonctif tenses exist — **imparfait du subjonctif** and **plus-que-parfait du subjonctif** — but they belong to the [literary-only tense family](./14_tenses.md#temps-littéraires-literary-only-tenses): recognize them in formal writing, but use subjonctif présent/passé for everything you actually say.

## L'impératif (imperative)

Gives a command, dropping the subject pronoun entirely.

- Sentence: **Pars** ! (Leave!) **Finissons** ! (Let's finish!) **Partez** ! (Leave! [vous])

One spelling quirk already flagged in [en and y](./06_pronouns.md#points-to-note-for-eny): -er verbs (including *aller*) drop the final **-s** from the *tu* form — *tu parles* → **Parle !** — except right before *y* or *en*, where the *-s* comes back purely for the liaison: **Vas-y !**, **Manges-en !**

## Le conditionnel (as a mood)

| Tense | Built from | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Conditionnel présent | futur stem + imparfait endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient) | Elle **partirait** si elle pouvait. (She would leave if she could.) |
| Conditionnel passé | conditionnel of avoir/être + participe passé | Elle **serait partie** si elle avait pu. (She would have left if she had been able to.) |

Beyond hypotheticals, conditionnel présent is also the standard way to **soften a request** — see [vouloir → voudrais](./16_modal_verbs.md#2-nuance-shifts-by-tensemood-not-by-swapping-words): *Je **voudrais** un café.* (I would like a coffee.)

## Les phrases conditionnelles avec "si" (si-conditional sentences)

Structurally close to English's conditionals, but organized into three named types rather than four-plus-mixed.

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Réel/possible | Si + présent, futur simple (or présent, or impératif) | Si tu **viens**, je **serai** content. (If you come, I'll be happy.) |
| Irréel du présent | Si + imparfait, conditionnel présent | Si j'**avais** de l'argent, je **voyagerais**. (If I had money, I would travel.) |
| Irréel du passé | Si + plus-que-parfait, conditionnel passé | Si j'**avais su**, je **serais venu**. (If I had known, I would have come.) |

A **general-truth (zero-conditional) statement** just uses the same *réel* structure with présent on both sides instead of a futur result: *Si on **chauffe** l'eau, elle **bout**.* (If you heat water, it boils.)

## Points to note for French

### 1. Never futur or conditionnel directly after "si" meaning "if"

Just like English forbids "if it **will** rain" (only "if it **rains**"), French strictly forbids futur or conditionnel right after a conditional **si** — a very common error for English speakers who slip and translate too literally in the other direction (*"si je serai riche"* is wrong; it must be *si je **suis** riche*). So this rule actually mirrors English's own restriction, rather than being some extra new trap.

### 2. ...but "si" meaning "whether" has no such restriction

When **si** introduces an indirect question ("whether") rather than a condition ("if"), futur and conditionnel are completely fine — because it's not a conditional *si* at all:

- Sentence: Je ne sais pas si elle **viendra**. (I don't know if/whether she will come.) — perfectly correct, because this *si* means "whether," not "if."

### 3. No separate "mixed conditional" category

English formalizes a "mixed conditional" for combining a past condition with a present result (or vice versa). French doesn't name this as its own type — you just combine plus-que-parfait and conditionnel présent as the sense requires: *Si j'**avais pris** ce poste, je **serais** riche **maintenant**.* (If I had taken that job, I would be rich now.) It behaves the same way English's mixed conditional does; French simply doesn't give it a separate label the way it does for the three main types above.
