# Adverbs (Les adverbes)

An *adverbe* describes or gives more information about a verb, an adjective, or another adverb (see [Adverbe](./03_classes.md#adverbe-adverb)). Unlike *adjectifs*, adverbs are **invariable** — they never agree with anything, which makes them the one major word class in French that behaves closer to English.

## By formation

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Derived adverb | built from an adjective's **feminine** form + **-ment** | heureux → heureuse → **heureusement**, lent → lente → **lentement** |
| Simple (flat) adverb | not built from an adjective; a word of its own | **souvent**, **bientôt**, **très**, **vite**, **bien**, **toujours** |

- Sentence: Elle chante **magnifiquement** et se produit **souvent** en direct.

**The "-ment" rule has its own exceptions, worth learning as a set:**

- Adjectives whose masculine already ends in a vowel skip the feminine step entirely: vrai → **vraiment**, poli → **poliment** *(not "vraiement")*.
- Adjectives ending in **-ent** → **-emment**; adjectives ending in **-ant** → **-amment** — and both endings are pronounced identically, /amɑ̃/, despite the different spelling: patient → **patiemment**, constant → **constamment**.
- A few are fully irregular: bon → **bien** *(not "bonnement")*, mauvais → **mal**, gentil → **gentiment** *(no doubled consonant)*, bref → **brièvement**.

## By function

### Manner — how something happens (Manière)

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| bien, mal, vite, lentement, prudemment | Elle conduit **prudemment**. |

### Place — where something happens (Lieu)

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| ici, là, partout, dehors | Mets-le **là**. |

### Time — when something happens (Temps)

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| maintenant, hier, bientôt, plus tard | Nous partirons **bientôt**. |

### Frequency — how often something happens (Fréquence)

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| toujours, souvent, parfois, jamais, rarement | Il arrive **toujours** à l'heure. |

**jamais needs *ne*, like other negative words.** Same pattern as *personne*/*rien*/*aucun* from [Indefinite pronouns](./06_pronouns.md#indefinite-pronouns-pronoms-indéfinis): *Je **ne** sors **jamais** le lundi.* (I never go out on Mondays.)

### Degree — how much/to what extent (Degré/Intensité)

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| très, assez, trop, presque, extrêmement | Il fait **trop** chaud aujourd'hui. |

## Word order with compound tenses

**This is the biggest structural difference from English.** With a compound tense like the *passé composé*, most short/common adverbs slot **between** the auxiliary and the past participle — not after the whole verb phrase, the way English puts them at the end.

- Il **a bien mangé**. (He ate well — literally "he has well eaten.")
- Elle **a déjà fini**. (She already finished.)
- Nous **avons trop parlé**. (We talked too much.)

Longer adverbs (especially -ment adverbs describing manner) are more flexible and can also follow the participle, but short high-frequency ones (bien, mal, déjà, encore, trop, toujours, souvent) almost always go in the middle slot.

## Interrogative and relative adverbs

The same small set — **quand, où, pourquoi, comment** — plays two roles, like the adjective/pronoun pairs in [Adjectives](./10_adjectives.md#other-adjective-types-cross-links):

- **Interrogative adverb** — asks a question.
  - **Quand** es-tu arrivé ? **Pourquoi** est-elle partie ?
- **Relative adverb** — introduces a clause describing a noun (see [Proposition](./04_sentence_structure.md#proposition-clause)).
  - La raison **pour laquelle** elle est partie. *(not "la raison pourquoi")*

**Two asymmetries with English are worth flagging:**

- **pourquoi is essentially interrogative-only.** English happily reuses "why" as a relative adverb ("the reason why she left"), but standard French doesn't extend *pourquoi* that way — it swaps in **pour laquelle** instead.
- **où covers both place *and* time as a relative adverb**, where English needs two separate words ("where" vs. "when"): *le jour **où** nous nous sommes rencontrés* (the day **when** we met) — not *"le jour quand."* *Quand* isn't used as a relative adverb in standard French at all.

## Conjunctive adverbs (Adverbes de liaison)

Connects two independent clauses or sentences, showing a logical relationship. Usually followed by a comma.

| Examples | Example sentence |
|---|---|
| cependant, donc, néanmoins, pourtant, toutefois, par ailleurs | Il pleuvait ; **cependant**, nous sommes quand même sortis. |

## Degrees of comparison

Same structure as [adjective comparison](./10_adjectives.md#degrees-of-comparison-les-degrés-de-comparaison) — no "-er"/"-est" ending, just **plus/aussi/moins + adverb + que**.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| vite | plus vite | **le** plus vite |
| prudemment | plus prudemment | **le** plus prudemment |
| bien (well) | **mieux** *(never "plus bien")* | **le mieux** |
| mal (badly) | plus mal / pis *(archaic, survives in "tant pis")* | le plus mal |

- Sentence: Elle court **vite**, il court **plus vite**, mais le champion court **le plus vite**.

**The superlative article is always the invariable *le*, never *la/les*.** Unlike an adjective's superlative — which agrees with the noun's gender ([see Adjectives](./10_adjectives.md#degrees-of-comparison-les-degrés-de-comparaison)) — an adverb modifies a verb, not a noun, so there's no gender to agree with: *Elle court **le** plus vite.*, not *"la plus vite."*
