# Verb Forms (Les formes verbales)

A **form** is the shape a verb takes as a word. English gets away with only 5 forms total because most of its tense system leans on auxiliary verbs rather than changing the main verb's ending. French can't do that — most tenses are built by changing the verb's own ending for **each of the six grammatical persons** (je/tu/il-elle-on/nous/vous/ils-elles), not just a single "-s" tweak for 3rd person singular like English. So instead of a tidy table of 5 forms, this file covers the small set of **non-personal building-block forms** (used to construct compound tenses) and then shows why the *personal* forms multiply the way they do.

## The building-block forms

These three don't change per person — they're the forms [compound tenses](./12_verbs_overview.md#temps-simples-vs-temps-composés-simple-vs-compound-tenses) and [non-finite constructions](./18_non_finite_verbs.md) are built from.

| Form | parler (1er groupe) | finir (2e groupe) | être (irregular) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infinitif (dictionary form) | parler | finir | être |
| Participe présent | parlant | finissant | étant |
| Participe passé | parlé | fini | été |

## Personal conjugation — why French needs more than 5 forms

Unlike English, where only 3rd person singular present gets a special ending ("she go**es**" vs. "I/you/we/they go"), **every person has its own ending** in a French simple tense.

| Person | parler (présent) |
|---|---|
| je | parl**e** |
| tu | parl**es** |
| il / elle / on | parl**e** |
| nous | parl**ons** |
| vous | parl**ez** |
| ils / elles | parl**ent** |

**A quirk worth flagging: four of these six forms sound identical.** *Je parle*, *tu parles*, *il parle*, and *ils parlent* are all pronounced the same way, /paʁl/ — the differences are purely written, carried by the subject pronoun rather than by anything audible in the verb ending. This is the spoken-French mirror of the [silent plural -s](./09_nouns.md#3-silent-plural--s) noted for nouns.

## Regular vs. irregular verbs

Same basic distinction as English — regular verbs follow a predictable pattern from their [conjugation group](./12_verbs_overview.md#les-trois-groupes-de-conjugaison-the-three-conjugation-groups); irregular verbs (mostly in the 3rd group) have to be memorized individually.

| Infinitif | Group | Participe passé | Présent (il/elle) |
|---|---|---|---|
| parler | 1er (regular) | parlé | parle |
| finir | 2e (regular) | fini | finit |
| être | 3e (irregular) | été | est |
| avoir | 3e (irregular) | eu | a |
| aller | 3e (irregular, despite -er) | allé | va |
| faire | 3e (irregular) | fait | fait |
| prendre | 3e (irregular) | pris | prend |

## Past participle agreement

The participe passé isn't always fixed — it can pick up adjective-like endings (-e, -s, -es), depending on the auxiliary, per the [rule previewed in Verbs Overview](./12_verbs_overview.md#le-choix-de-lauxiliaire--avoir-ou-être-auxiliary-choice).

| Auxiliary | Agreement rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| être (motion/state verbs) | agrees with the **subject** | Elle **est allée** au marché. Ils **sont allés** au marché. |
| être (reflexive verbs) | agrees with the **subject** (usually) | Elle **s'est lavée**. |
| avoir | agrees with a **preceding direct object**, if any | Elle **a mangé** la pomme. → La pomme qu'elle **a mangée**. |

**The avoir rule is genuinely the trickiest spelling point in French**, because agreement only kicks in when the direct object comes *before* the verb — which normally means a preceding COD pronoun, or a relative clause built with **que**.

- No agreement: Elle **a mangé** la pomme. (The direct object, *la pomme*, comes *after* the verb — participle stays in its base form.)
- Agreement: Elle **l'a mangée**. (*l'* = *la pomme*, now placed *before* the verb by [normal object-pronoun word order](./06_pronouns.md#personal-pronouns-pronoms-personnels) — participle takes the feminine -e.)
- Agreement: La pomme **qu'**elle **a mangée** était verte. (*que* refers back to *la pomme*, again placed before the verb by the relative clause structure — participle agrees again.)

## Why forms matter

Every [tense](./14_tenses.md) is a specific combination of these forms — either a personal ending on the verb's own stem, or an auxiliary (conjugated in a simple tense) plus a fixed building-block form:

- Présent = personal ending on the stem → "Elle **parle**."
- Passé composé = avoir/être (conjugated in the présent) + participe passé → "Elle **a parlé**."
- Futur proche = aller (conjugated in the présent) + infinitif → "Elle **va parler**."
- Gérondif = **en** + participe présent → "**En parlant**, elle a souri." (see [Non-finite Verbs](./18_non_finite_verbs.md#gérondif))
