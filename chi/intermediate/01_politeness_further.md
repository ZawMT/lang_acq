# Politeness: Further (禮貌進階)

Basics are in [Politeness](../basic/23_politeness.md). This note covers honorific and humble vocabulary, forms of address in more detail, history, and social customs.

## Honorific and humble words (敬詞 · 謙詞)

Chinese has no polite grammar, but it does have **vocabulary** that raises the other person (**敬詞** ㄐㄧㄥˋ ㄘˊ *jìngcí*, honorifics) or lowers oneself (**謙詞** ㄑㄧㄢ ㄘˊ *qiāncí*, humble words). Most are now **formal**: speeches, letters, ceremonies, business.

| Honorific (yours) | Humble (mine) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **貴姓** *guìxìng* | **敝姓** *bìxìng* | surname |
| **府上** *fǔshàng* | **寒舍** *hánshè* | home |
| **貴公司** *guì gōngsī* | **敝公司** *bì gōngsī* | company |
| **令尊** *lìngzūn* | **家父** *jiāfù* | father |
| **令堂** *lìngtáng* | **家母** *jiāmǔ* | mother |
| **令郎** *lìngláng* | **犬子** *quǎnzǐ* (lit. dog son) | son |
| **令嬡** *lìng'ài* | **小女** *xiǎonǚ* | daughter |
| **高見** *gāojiàn* | **拙見** *zhuōjiàn* | opinion |
| **大作** *dàzuò* | **拙作** *zhuōzuò* | (written) work |
| **光臨** *guānglín* | — | (your) coming, visit |
| **指教** *zhǐjiào* | — | (your) guidance |

The prefixes form a pattern:

| Prefix | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **貴** | precious | your… (貴姓, 貴國, 貴校) |
| **令** | fine | your family member (令尊, 令郎) |
| **敝** | shabby | my… (敝姓, 敝公司) |
| **家** | family | my **older** relatives (家父, 家兄) |
| **舍** | house | my **younger** relatives (舍弟, 舍妹) |
| **拙** | clumsy | my work, my view (拙作, 拙見) |
| **小** | small | my… (小女, 小弟 = I, humbly) |

**Still common today:** 您貴姓, 歡迎光臨, 請多指教, 貴公司 (in business), 府上 (polite "your home").

## Addressing people

### Titles

Job titles are used **as names**, with or without the surname:

| Title | Use |
|---|---|
| **老師** | teachers; also artists, experts, and as a general respectful title (common in Taiwan) |
| **醫生** | doctors |
| **經理, 主任, 老闆** | manager, head, boss |
| **師傅** | skilled workers: drivers, cooks, repairmen (mainland) |
| **同學** | classmates; students in general |

> 王經理，您好。 (Hello, Manager Wang.)
> 老師好！ (Hello, teacher! — students' greeting)

### Family terms for strangers

Strangers are often addressed as if they were **family**:

| Term | Literal | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **爺爺 / 奶奶** | grandpa / grandma | elderly people |
| **伯伯** | uncle (father's elder brother) | older men |
| **叔叔 / 阿姨** | uncle / auntie | people about your parents' age |
| **大哥 / 大姐** | big brother / big sister | slightly older adults |
| **小弟 / 小妹** | little brother / sister | young boys, girls |

Children are taught to greet adults this way: 叔叔好！ 阿姨好！

### Regional differences

| Address | Taiwan | Mainland China |
|---|---|---|
| **小姐** (Miss) | normal and polite | can suggest a bar hostess in some contexts; often avoided for staff |
| **服務員** (waiter) | rarely used to call staff | standard |
| **同志** (comrade) | not used | old-fashioned; now often means "gay" |
| **帥哥 / 美女** (handsome / beauty) | casual, friendly | very common for strangers of any age |
| **老闆** (boss) | calling shop and stall owners | same |

## History

**Grammar:** Chinese has **never** had grammatical politeness. Old and Classical Chinese (文言文) didn't conjugate either.

**Vocabulary:** imperial China had a much richer set of honorific and humble words. People of status often avoided plain 我 and 你:

| Humble "I" | Who used it |
|---|---|
| **朕** *zhèn* | the emperor only |
| **寡人** *guǎrén* ("the lonely one") | kings and lords |
| **臣** *chén* ("your servant") | officials to the ruler |
| **在下** *zàixià* ("the one below") | men, politely |
| **鄙人** *bǐrén* ("the lowly person") | formal |
| **奴才** *núcai* ("slave") | Manchu officials to the Qing emperor |

| Honorific "you" | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **陛下** *bìxià* | Your Majesty |
| **閣下** *géxià* | Your Excellency (still used formally) |
| **足下** *zúxià* | "at your feet" (in letters) |

There were also **name taboos** (避諱 *bìhuì*): the emperor's personal name couldn't be spoken or written. Adults were addressed by their **courtesy name** (字 *zì*), not their given name.

### What changed

| Period | Change |
|---|---|
| **1911**: fall of the Qing | court language (朕, 臣, 奴才) and kowtowing ended with the empire |
| **1919**: May Fourth / New Culture Movement | everyday vernacular (白話文) replaced Classical Chinese; Confucian hierarchy was criticized; heavy honorifics began to seem old-fashioned |
| **1949 onward** (mainland) | equality was promoted; **同志** (comrade) became the standard address for anyone |
| **1966–1976**: Cultural Revolution (mainland) | traditional etiquette attacked as "feudal" |
| **1980s onward** (mainland) | 先生, 女士, 老師, 師傅 came back; 同志 faded |

**Taiwan and Hong Kong** didn't go through 1949 and the Cultural Revolution in the same way, so more traditional polite vocabulary survives there: formal letters, 敝姓, 府上 feel more natural.

## Social customs

### Face (面子)

**面子** (*miànzi*, face) is a person's dignity and reputation. Politeness is largely about **giving face** (給面子) and not making anyone **lose face** (丟面子):

- Don't criticize or contradict someone directly in front of others.
- Praise people in public; raise problems in private.
- Accepting an invitation or gift can be a way of giving face.

### Indirect refusals

A direct "no" (不行, 不要) can sound harsh. Common soft refusals:

| Expression | Meaning | Real meaning, often |
|---|---|---|
| **我考慮看看** | I'll think about it | probably no |
| **再說吧** | let's talk later | no |
| **不太方便** | it's not very convenient | no |
| **有點困難** | it's a bit difficult | no |
| **我再問問** | I'll ask around | probably no |

### Offers and paying

- Offers are refused **once or twice** first (不用不用, 不要客氣); the host keeps insisting.
- **Fighting to pay the bill** (搶著付錢) is a ritual of generosity, especially among friends and family.
- Hosts **keep refilling** your plate and cup. Leaving a little food shows you've had enough.

### Gifts

- Gifts are often **refused** politely at first, then accepted.
- Gifts aren't usually **opened in front of** the giver.
- Give and receive with **both hands**: gifts, business cards, documents.
- Some gifts are avoided because of **sound puns**:

| Gift | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **鐘** (clock) | 送鐘 *sòng zhōng* = 送終 | attending someone's death |
| **傘** (umbrella) | 傘 *sǎn* = 散 | to separate |
| **梨** (pear, to share) | 分梨 *fēn lí* = 分離 | to part |
| **四** (four of something) | 四 *sì* ≈ 死 *sǐ* | death |

### Toasting

At meals, people **toast** (敬酒) each other, especially elders and guests:

- **乾杯** (*gānbēi*, lit. dry the glass) = cheers.
- Hold your glass **lower** than an elder's or superior's when clinking.
- 隨意 (*suíyì*, as you like) = drink as much as you want, no need to finish.
