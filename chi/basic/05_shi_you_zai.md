# 是, 有, 在: Be, Have, Be At

English "be" covers many jobs. Chinese splits them among three verbs:

| Verb | Zhuyin | Pinyin | Job | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **是** | ㄕˋ | shì | links two nouns: A **is** B | 不是 |
| **有** | ㄧㄡˇ | yǒu | **has**; **there is** | 沒有 (never 不有) |
| **在** | ㄗㄞˋ | zài | **is at** a place | 不在 |

## 是: A is B

是 links a noun to another noun: what or who something **is**.

> 我是學生。 → *Wǒ shì xuéshēng.* (I'm a student.)
> 這是我的手機。 → *Zhè shì wǒ de shǒujī.* (This is my phone.)
> 他不是老師。 → *Tā bú shì lǎoshī.* (He isn't a teacher.)

**Trap:** **not with adjectives**. Adjectives are the predicate on their own (see [Adjectives](./06_adjectives.md)):

> 她很漂亮。 (She's beautiful.) 她是漂亮 ✗

**Dates, ages, prices** often drop 是: 今天星期一 (today's Monday), 我二十歲 (I'm twenty), 這個一百塊 (this is 100 dollars). In the negative, 是 comes back: 今天**不是**星期一.

### 是…的: focusing on a detail

To stress **when, where, how, or with whom** something already happened, wrap the detail in 是…的:

> 我**是**坐火車來**的**。 → *Wǒ shì zuò huǒchē lái de.* (I came **by train**.)
> 你**是**什麼時候到**的**？ → *Nǐ shì shénme shíhou dào de?* (**When** did you arrive?)

The action is known; the question is about the detail. 是 can be dropped in speech: 我坐火車來的.

## 有: have / there is

**Possession:**

> 我有一隻貓。 → *Wǒ yǒu yì zhī māo.* (I have a cat.)
> 我沒有錢。 → *Wǒ méiyǒu qián.* (I have no money.)

**Existence:** place + 有 + thing

> 桌子上有一本書。 → *Zhuōzi shàng yǒu yì běn shū.* (There's a book on the table.)
> 附近有沒有便利商店？ → *Fùjìn yǒu méiyǒu biànlì shāngdiàn?* (Is there a convenience store nearby?)

**Trap:** 有 is the only verb negated with **沒**, never 不: 不有 ✗.

## 在: be at

Thing + 在 + place: **where** something known is.

> 我在家。 → *Wǒ zài jiā.* (I'm at home.)
> 你的書在桌子上。 → *Nǐ de shū zài zhuōzi shàng.* (Your book is on the table.)
> 他不在。 → *Tā bú zài.* (He's not here / not in.)

在 is also a preposition (在家吃飯) and a progressive marker (在吃飯). See [Word Classes](./02_word_classes.md#兼類-one-word-several-classes).

## 有 vs 在

Both talk about location, but the order and the information differ:

| | Pattern | Thing is… | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **有** | place + 有 + thing | new, indefinite ("a book") | 桌子上**有**一本書。 (There's a book on the table.) |
| **在** | thing + 在 + place | known, definite ("the book") | 那本書**在**桌子上。 (The book is on the table.) |

## Location words

Ordinary nouns need a **location word** after them to become a place:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 上 | on | 桌子上 (on the table) |
| 下 | under | 椅子下 (under the chair) |
| 裡 | in | 房間裡 (in the room) |
| 外 | outside | 門外 (outside the door) |
| 前 / 前面 | in front | 學校前面 (in front of the school) |
| 後 / 後面 | behind | 房子後面 (behind the house) |
| 旁邊 | beside | 銀行旁邊 (next to the bank) |
| 中間 | between, middle | 兩棟樓中間 (between the two buildings) |
| 對面 | opposite | 車站對面 (across from the station) |

> 我在房間**裡**。 (I'm in the room.) 我在房間 ✗ (sounds unfinished)

**Place names** and place nouns don't need one: 我在台北, 我在學校, 我在家.
