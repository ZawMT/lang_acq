# 的, 地, 得: Three *de*

Three structural particles all sound the same, ˙ㄉㄜ *de* (neutral tone), but are written differently and do different jobs:

| Particle | Job | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **的** | modifies a **noun** | modifier + 的 + noun | 漂亮**的**花 (beautiful flower) |
| **地** | modifies a **verb** (manner) | adverbial + 地 + verb | 慢慢**地**走 (walk slowly) |
| **得** | adds a **complement** after a verb | verb + 得 + result/degree | 跑**得**很快 (run fast) |

**Memory trick:** 的 + **noun**; 地 + **verb**; **verb** + 得.

## 的: before nouns

的 joins **anything that describes a noun** to the noun. The describing part always comes first:

| Modifier | Example |
|---|---|
| owner | 我**的**書 (my book), 老師**的**車 (the teacher's car) |
| adjective | 漂亮**的**花 (beautiful flowers), 很大**的**房子 (a very big house) |
| clause | 我昨天買**的**書 (the book I bought yesterday) |
| noun | 木頭**的**桌子 (a wooden table) |

> 坐在那邊**的**人是我哥哥。 → *Zuò zài nàbiān de rén shì wǒ gēge.* (The person sitting over there is my brother.)

### 的 without a noun: "the one…"

When the noun is clear, drop it and keep 的:

> 這是**我的**。 (This is mine.)
> 我要**紅的**。 (I want the red one.)
> **賣菜的** (vegetable seller: the one who sells vegetables)

### When 的 drops

- One-syllable adjectives: 好人, 新書 (see [Adjectives](./06_adjectives.md))
- Close relationships: 我媽媽, 我家 (see [Pronouns](./09_pronouns.md))
- Set phrases: 中文老師 (Chinese teacher)

### 是…的

是…的 focuses on a detail of a past event: 我是坐火車來**的**。 (I came by train.) See [是, 有, 在](./05_shi_you_zai.md).

## 地: before verbs

地 turns a description into an **adverb**, saying **how** the action is done. It comes **before** the verb:

> 他**高興地**說：「我考上了！」 → *Tā gāoxìng de shuō…* (He said happily, "I passed!")
> 請**慢慢地**說。 → *Qǐng mànmàn de shuō.* (Please speak slowly.)
> 她**認真地**學習。 → (She studies hard.)

- Often used with **two-syllable** adjectives or **reduplicated** ones (慢慢, 好好).
- In casual writing, many people write **的** for 地. Both are read *de*.

地 also has another reading, **ㄉㄧˋ** *dì* (ground, land): 地方 (place), 地圖 (map).

## 得: after verbs

得 comes **after a verb or adjective** and introduces **how well, how much, or with what result**:

> 他跑**得很快**。 → *Tā pǎo de hěn kuài.* (He runs fast.)
> 你說**得對**。 → (You're right. Lit. you spoke correctly.)
> 我累**得要命**。 → (I'm dead tired.)
> 她高興**得跳了起來**。 → (She was so happy she jumped up.)

### With an object: repeat the verb, or move the object

The object can't go between the verb and 得. Either **repeat the verb** or **put the object first**:

> 他**說中文說得**很好。 (He speaks Chinese well.)
> 他**中文說得**很好。 (same)
> 他說中文得很好 ✗

### Negatives and questions

> 他跑得**不快**。 (He doesn't run fast.)
> 他跑得**快不快**？ (Does he run fast?)

### Potential: 得 / 不 in the middle

得 also makes "can": 看**得**懂 (can understand), 看**不**懂 (can't understand). See [Complements](./14_complements.md).

得 has two other readings: **ㄉㄜˊ** *dé* (obtain: 得到) and **ㄉㄟˇ** *děi* (have to: 我得走了).

## 地 vs 得: before or after

| | 地 (before) | 得 (after) |
|---|---|---|
| focus | **how** the action is done, as it happens | **evaluating** the result |
| example | 他**很快地**跑了過去。 (He quickly ran over.) | 他跑**得很快**。 (He runs fast — his running is fast.) |

**Trap:** native speakers mix up 的/地/得 in writing too, but in standard writing they're kept apart. Listen for the position: before a noun, before a verb, or after a verb.
