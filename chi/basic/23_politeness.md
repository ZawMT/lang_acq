# Politeness (禮貌)

Chinese has **no grammatical politeness system**. Verbs never change for politeness: 吃 is 吃 whether you're talking to a friend or your boss. Politeness comes from:

1. **polite words** (您, 請, 謝謝…)
2. **how you address people** (titles, family terms)
3. **softening** requests
4. **modesty** and polite behaviour

The grammar is easy; the social expectations are just as real.

## 您: polite "you"

| Word | Zhuyin | Pinyin | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **你** | ㄋㄧˇ | nǐ | normal "you" |
| **您** | ㄋㄧㄣˊ | nín | polite "you": elders, customers, superiors |

- 您 is very common in **northern China** (Beijing).
- In **Taiwan**, 您 is used in service language and writing, but in everyday speech 你 with a soft tone and particles is normal.
- For a group, use **各位** (everyone) or **大家**: 各位好 (Hello, everyone). 您們 is rare.

## Everyday polite words

| Expression | Zhuyin | Pinyin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **請** | ㄑㄧㄥˇ | qǐng | please |
| **請問** | ㄑㄧㄥˇ ㄨㄣˋ | qǐngwèn | excuse me, may I ask… |
| **謝謝** | ㄒㄧㄝˋ ˙ㄒㄧㄝ | xièxie | thank you |
| **不客氣** | ㄅㄨˊ ㄎㄜˋ ㄑㄧˋ | bú kèqì | you're welcome |
| **對不起** | ㄉㄨㄟˋ ˙ㄅㄨ ㄑㄧˇ | duìbuqǐ | sorry |
| **沒關係** | ㄇㄟˊ ㄍㄨㄢ ˙ㄒㄧ | méi guānxi | it's OK, never mind |
| **不好意思** | ㄅㄨˋ ㄏㄠˇ ㄧˋ ˙ㄙ | bù hǎoyìsi | excuse me; sorry (light) |
| **麻煩你** | ㄇㄚˊ ㄈㄢˊ ㄋㄧˇ | máfán nǐ | sorry to trouble you; could you… |
| **辛苦了** | ㄒㄧㄣ ㄎㄨˇ ˙ㄌㄜ | xīnkǔ le | thanks for your hard work |

**不好意思** is everywhere in Taiwan: to get attention, to apologize lightly, or before asking a favour.

## Addressing people

**Titles** and **family terms** are central to politeness. Using a full name alone can sound cold.

| Address | Use |
|---|---|
| surname + **先生** (Mr) | 王先生 |
| surname + **小姐** (Miss) / **女士** (Ms, formal) | 李小姐 |
| surname + **title** | 王**老師** (Teacher Wang), 陳**經理** (Manager Chen), 林**醫生** (Dr Lin) |
| **老師** | teachers, and also as a general respectful title |
| **阿姨 / 叔叔** | auntie / uncle: older strangers, friends' parents |

When you meet someone:

> 您貴姓？ → *Nín guìxìng?* (May I ask your surname?)
> 我姓王。 → *Wǒ xìng Wáng.* (My surname is Wang.)

## Softening requests

Bare commands sound abrupt. Add softeners:

| Softener | Example |
|---|---|
| **請** | **請**坐。 (Please sit down.) |
| **一下** | 你看**一下**。 (Take a look.) |
| **可以…嗎？** | **可以**幫我**嗎**？ (Could you help me?) |
| **…好嗎？** | 我們明天再說，**好嗎**？ (Let's talk tomorrow, OK?) |
| **吧** | 你先走**吧**。 (You go ahead.) |
| verb reduplication | 你**試試**。 (Give it a try.) |

## Modesty

Accept compliments by **declining** them:

> A: 你的中文說得真好！ (Your Chinese is really good!)
> B: **哪裡哪裡**。 / **還好啦**。 / **過獎了**。 (Oh, not at all. / It's OK. / You flatter me.)

Saying "Thank you!" is becoming common, especially among young people, but modesty is still the classic reply.

## Ritual refusals

Offers of food, drinks, gifts, or paying the bill are often **politely refused once or twice** before being accepted:

> A: 再吃一點吧！ (Have some more!)
> B: **不用不用**，我吃飽了。 (No, no, I'm full.)

Hosts are expected to **insist**. Don't take the first "no" too literally.
