# 把 and 被

Two constructions move the **object** before the verb:

| | Zhuyin | Pinyin | Order | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **把** | ㄅㄚˇ | bǎ | S + 把 + O + V + result | what the subject **does to** the object |
| **被** | ㄅㄟˋ | bèi | O + 被 (+ agent) + V + result | what **happened to** the object (passive) |

> 我**把**杯子打破了。 → *Wǒ bǎ bēizi dǎpò le.* (I broke the cup.)
> 杯子**被**我打破了。 → *Bēizi bèi wǒ dǎpò le.* (The cup was broken by me.)

## 把: doing something to it

把 brings the object forward to show that something **is done to it**, and **how it ends up**:

> S + **把** + object + verb + **something more**

> 我**把**作業寫**完**了。 → (I finished my homework.)
> 請**把**門關**上**。 → (Please close the door.)
> 他**把**錢放**在桌子上**。 → (He put the money on the table.)
> 你**把**這句話翻譯**成**英文。 → (Translate this sentence into English.)

### Rules

1. **The verb can't be bare.** It needs a result, direction, location, 了, or similar:
   > 我把飯吃**完**了。 ✓ / 我把飯吃 ✗
2. **The object is known, specific**: "the cup", not "a cup".
   > 我把**那本書**看完了。 ✓ / 我把一本書看完了 (odd)
3. **The verb must act on the object.** Not with verbs of feeling, perceiving, or being:
   > 喜歡, 知道, 看見, 是, 有, 覺得 → 我把他喜歡 ✗
4. **Negatives and modals go before 把:**
   > 我**沒**把門關好。 (I didn't close the door properly.)
   > 你**應該**把房間打掃乾淨。 (You should clean your room.)
   > **別**把手機忘了。 (Don't forget your phone.)

### When 把 is needed

With **V + 在 / 到 / 給 / 成** and an object, 把 is usually the natural (or only) way:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| V + 在 (put somewhere) | **把**書放**在**桌上。 (Put the book on the table.) |
| V + 到 (move somewhere) | **把**車開**到**門口。 (Drive the car to the door.) |
| V + 給 (give to) | **把**這個交**給**老師。 (Hand this to the teacher.) |
| V + 成 (turn into) | **把**中文翻譯**成**英文。 (Translate Chinese into English.) |

把 is very common in **instructions** and **requests**: 請把…, 把…一下.

## 被: passive

被 marks the **passive**: the subject **receives** the action.

> 我的手機**被**偷了。 → *Wǒ de shǒujī bèi tōu le.* (My phone was stolen.)
> 他**被**老闆罵了。 → *Tā bèi lǎobǎn mà le.* (He got told off by the boss.)
> 蛋糕**被**弟弟吃完了。 → (The cake was eaten up by my little brother.)

- The **agent** (who did it) is optional: 被(人)偷了.
- Like 把, the verb usually needs **something more** (了, a result).
- Negatives and modals go **before 被**: 我**沒**被罵。 (I didn't get scolded.)

### 被 is usually for bad things

Traditionally, 被 describes **unwelcome** events: being stolen from, scolded, hit, cheated.

> 他**被**騙了。 (He was cheated.)

Modern Chinese also uses it for neutral or good events, especially in writing: 他**被**選為班長。 (He was elected class leader.)

### Spoken alternatives: 讓, 叫, 給

| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| **讓** *ràng* | 我的蛋糕**讓**他吃了。 |
| **叫** *jiào* | 我的蛋糕**叫**他吃了。 |
| **給** *gěi* | 我的蛋糕**給**他吃了。 |

With 讓 and 叫, the **agent must be stated**.

## Passive without 被

Very often, Chinese has **no passive marker** at all. When the object can't possibly do the action, the sentence is understood as passive:

> 信寫好了。 → (The letter has been written.)
> 飯做好了。 → (The meal is ready.)
> 這本書賣完了。 → (This book is sold out.)

Using 被 here would sound odd. Use 被 mainly when you want to show something **happened to** someone, often unpleasantly.
