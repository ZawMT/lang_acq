# Word Order (語序)

Chinese words don't change form, so **word order does the grammar**. Swap two words and you change the meaning:

> 狗咬人。 → *Gǒu yǎo rén.* (A dog bites a man.)
> 人咬狗。 → *Rén yǎo gǒu.* (A man bites a dog.)

## Basic order: subject – verb – object

> 我喝茶。 → *Wǒ hē chá.* (I drink tea.)
> 他學中文。 → *Tā xué Zhōngwén.* (He studies Chinese.)

The order stays the same in questions (see [Questions](./10_questions.md)):

> 你喝什麼？ → *Nǐ hē shénme?* (What are you drinking?)

## Modifiers come before what they modify

This is one of the strongest rules in Chinese: **everything that describes something comes first**.

| Modifier | Example |
|---|---|
| adjective + noun | **新**書 (new book), **漂亮的**花 (beautiful flower) |
| owner + noun | **我的**書 (my book) |
| clause + noun | **我昨天買的**書 (the book I bought yesterday) |
| adverb + verb | **慢慢地**走 (walk slowly), **也**去 (also go) |
| place + verb | **在家**吃飯 (eat at home) |

Even long descriptions go before the noun, joined by 的:

> 坐在窗邊看書的那個人 → *zuò zài chuāng biān kàn shū de nà ge rén* (that person sitting by the window reading)

## The fixed order: time → place → verb

Time and place go **before** the verb, in this order:

> Subject + **time** + **place** + verb + object

> 我**昨天在圖書館**看書。 → *Wǒ zuótiān zài túshūguǎn kàn shū.* (I read at the library yesterday.)

The time word can also go **before the subject**, for emphasis or setting the scene:

> **昨天**我在圖書館看書。 (Yesterday, I read at the library.)

But never at the end: 我在圖書館看書昨天 ✗.

### Full order of a sentence

| Subject | Time | Adverbs | Negation | Modal | Place / prep. phrase | Verb | Complement | Object |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 我 | 明天 | 也 | 不 | 想 | 在家 | 吃 | | 飯 |

> 我明天也不想在家吃飯。 → *Wǒ míngtiān yě bù xiǎng zài jiā chī fàn.* (I don't want to eat at home tomorrow either.)

## After the verb: how long, how many times, how well

What comes **after** the verb tells the **result** of the action:

| After the verb | Example |
|---|---|
| duration | 我學了**三年**。 (I studied for three years.) |
| frequency | 我去過**兩次**。 (I've been twice.) |
| result | 我吃**完**了。 (I've finished eating.) |
| degree | 他跑得**很快**。 (He runs fast.) |

Compare: **before** the verb = the setting; **after** the verb = the outcome.

> 他**在椅子上**坐。 (He sits on the chair: where he does it.)
> 他坐**在椅子上**。 (He sits down on the chair: where he ends up.)

## Order changes meaning

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 我們**都不**去。 | None of us is going. |
| 我們**不都**去。 | Not all of us are going. |
| 我**不常**去。 | I don't go often. |
| 我**常不**去。 | I often don't go. |

## Topic–comment

Chinese often puts the **topic** first, then says something about it. The topic can be the object, or something that isn't part of the verb at all:

> **這本書**我看過。 → *Zhè běn shū wǒ kàn guo.* (This book, I've read.)

> **那家餐廳**，菜很好吃。 → *Nà jiā cāntīng, cài hěn hǎochī.* (That restaurant, the food is good.)

> **大象**鼻子很長。 → *Dàxiàng bízi hěn cháng.* (Elephants have long trunks. Lit. elephants, trunk is long.)

Topic first is very natural, especially for things already known.

## Dropping words

When the context is clear, Chinese freely **drops the subject or object**:

> A: 你吃飯了嗎？ B: 吃了。 → (Have you eaten? — (I) ate.)
> A: 你看過那部電影嗎？ B: 看過。 → (Have you seen that film? — (I've) seen (it).)

## Big to small

Chinese goes from the **largest unit to the smallest**:

| | Order | Example |
|---|---|---|
| names | surname + given name | 王小明 (Wang Xiaoming) |
| dates | year → month → day | 2026年11月25日 |
| time | day → part of day → hour | 明天下午三點 (3 p.m. tomorrow) |
| addresses | country → city → street → number | 台灣台北市中山路10號 |
