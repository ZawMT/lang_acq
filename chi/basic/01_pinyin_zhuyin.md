# Pinyin and Zhuyin (拼音 · 注音符號)

Chinese is written in **characters** (漢字), and a character doesn't show how it is said. Two systems are used to write Mandarin **pronunciation**:

| | Pinyin | Zhuyin |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | 漢語拼音 *Hànyǔ Pīnyīn* ("spelling the sounds of Chinese") | 注音符號 *zhùyīn fúhào* ("sound-marking symbols"), informally **Bopomofo** |
| Writes sounds with | the Latin alphabet | 37 special symbols |
| Created | 1958, mainland China | 1913, made official in 1918 by the Republic of China |
| Used mainly in | mainland China and internationally | Taiwan |

| Character | Zhuyin | Pinyin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 你好 | ㄋㄧˇ ㄏㄠˇ | nǐ hǎo | hello |
| 謝謝 | ㄒㄧㄝˋ ˙ㄒㄧㄝ | xièxie | thank you |
| 台灣 | ㄊㄞˊ ㄨㄢ | Táiwān | Taiwan |
| 中文 | ㄓㄨㄥ ㄨㄣˊ | Zhōngwén | Chinese language |

Both write **the same sounds**. Only the symbols and some spelling rules differ.

- **Pinyin** is used to learn pronunciation, in dictionaries, and for typing. Taiwan has also used it as its official romanization since **2009** (street signs, names).
- **Zhuyin** is learned by children in Taiwan in the first weeks of primary school, before characters. Children's books and textbooks print it beside the characters (in vertical text, to the right of each character), and dictionaries in Taiwan give pronunciations in it. Many people in Taiwan type with a Zhuyin keyboard.

## The syllable

Every character is **one syllable**, and every syllable has the same shape:

> **initial** + **medial** + **final** + **tone**

Each part except the tone is optional, but there is always at least a medial or a final (or, in a few syllables, only an initial). Pinyin counts the medial as part of the final; Zhuyin writes it as a separate symbol, so a Zhuyin syllable has **at most three symbols**.

| Built from | Character | Zhuyin | Pinyin |
|---|---|---|---|
| final only | 愛 (love) | ㄞˋ | ài |
| initial + final | 媽 (mother) | ㄇㄚ | mā |
| medial only | 五 (five) | ㄨˇ | wǔ |
| initial + medial | 綠 (green) | ㄌㄩˋ | lǜ |
| medial + final | 要 (want) | ㄧㄠˋ | yào |
| initial + medial + final | 學 (to study) | ㄒㄩㄝˊ | xué |
| initial + medial + final | 裝 (to install) | ㄓㄨㄤ | zhuāng |
| initial only | 詩 (poem) | ㄕ | shī |

Mandarin has only about **400 syllables** (before tones), so many characters share the same sound. Tones and context tell them apart.

## Initials (聲母)

There are **21 initials**. Each is named with a vowel attached so it can be said aloud, and learners recite them in groups, in this order (this is where the name *Bopomofo* comes from):

| Group | Zhuyin | Pinyin | Said as | How to say them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lips | ㄅ ㄆ ㄇ ㄈ | b p m f | bo po mo fo | |
| tongue tip | ㄉ ㄊ ㄋ ㄌ | d t n l | de te ne le | |
| back of tongue | ㄍ ㄎ ㄏ | g k h | ge ke he | **h** is rougher than English h, made further back, close to a light scrape in the throat |
| tongue flat, front | ㄐ ㄑ ㄒ | j q x | ji qi xi | tongue tip down behind the lower teeth, middle of the tongue raised. **q** ≈ "ch" with a strong puff; **x** is between "s" and "sh" |
| tongue curled back | ㄓ ㄔ ㄕ ㄖ | zh ch sh r | zhi chi shi ri | tongue tip curled up toward the roof of the mouth. **r** is between English "r" and the "s" in "measure" |
| tongue tip, teeth | ㄗ ㄘ ㄙ | z c s | zi ci si | **z** ≈ "ds" in "kids"; **c** ≈ "ts" in "cats", with a strong puff |

**ㄐㄑㄒ j q x** are only ever followed by **ㄧ i** or **ㄩ ü**.

### Aspiration

In each pair below, the two sounds differ only by a **puff of air**, not by voicing. Pinyin **b d g j zh z** are not "voiced": they are just said without the puff. Hold your hand in front of your mouth: 怕 *pà* (afraid) should blow on it, 爸 *bà* (dad) should not.

| No puff | With puff | Example pair |
|---|---|---|
| ㄅ b | ㄆ p | 爸 ㄅㄚˋ bà (dad) / 怕 ㄆㄚˋ pà (afraid) |
| ㄉ d | ㄊ t | 大 ㄉㄚˋ dà (big) / 他 ㄊㄚ tā (he) |
| ㄍ g | ㄎ k | 哥 ㄍㄜ gē (older brother) / 渴 ㄎㄜˇ kě (thirsty) |
| ㄐ j | ㄑ q | 雞 ㄐㄧ jī (chicken) / 七 ㄑㄧ qī (seven) |
| ㄓ zh | ㄔ ch | 知 ㄓ zhī (to know) / 吃 ㄔ chī (to eat) |
| ㄗ z | ㄘ c | 字 ㄗˋ zì (character) / 次 ㄘˋ cì (time, occurrence) |

## Medials and finals (介音 · 韻母)

Zhuyin has **3 medials** (ㄧ ㄨ ㄩ) and **13 finals**. In the tables below they are grouped by how they start: plain, with **i-**, with **u-**, or with **ü-**.

Pinyin often spells a final **differently when there is no initial**. Both spellings are shown; the rules are in [Pinyin spelling rules](#pinyin-spelling-rules).

| ㄩㄝ | üe | **ue** | yue | 略 ㄌㄩㄝˋ lüè (brief), 學 ㄒㄩㄝˊ xué (to study), 月 ㄩㄝˋ yuè (moon) |

## Vowels with more than one sound

### ㄜ vs ㄝ (both e in Pinyin)

| | ㄜ | ㄝ |
|---|---|---|
| Pinyin | **e** | **ê** (written *e* in *ie*, *üe*) |
| Sound | back of the mouth, lips spread (not rounded) | front of the mouth, like the *e* in English "yes" |
| Where it appears | alone or after an initial | almost only after ㄧ or ㄩ |
| Examples | 喝 ㄏㄜ *hē* (to drink), 餓 ㄜˋ *è* (hungry), 的 ˙ㄉㄜ *de* ('s) | 謝 ㄒㄧㄝˋ *xiè* (to thank), 也 ㄧㄝˇ *yě* (also), 學 ㄒㄩㄝˊ *xué* (to study) |
| Shape | ㄛ with an extra stroke on top | from 也 |

- **ㄜ**: start from "uh", then spread the lips slightly without moving the tongue forward.
- **ㄝ**: ㄧㄝ sounds close to the "ye" in "yes".
- ㄝ alone appears only in the interjection 欸 *ê* ("hey!").

Pinyin **e** also appears in **ei** (ㄟ, like the "ay" in "say": 給 gěi) and **en, eng** (ㄣ ㄥ, a short, weak "uh": 人 rén, 冷 lěng). Zhuyin writes each of these as one symbol.

### Pinyin i: three sounds

| Pinyin | Zhuyin | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| **ji qi xi yi**, and after b p m d t n l | ㄐㄧ ㄑㄧ ㄒㄧ ㄧ … | a real "ee" |
| **zi ci si** | ㄗ ㄘ ㄙ | no "ee" at all: hold the buzz of z / c / s and let the voice continue |
| **zhi chi shi ri** | ㄓ ㄔ ㄕ ㄖ | no "ee" at all: keep the tongue curled back and let the voice continue |

Pinyin needs the **-i** because every syllable must have a vowel letter. Zhuyin writes these syllables with the **initial alone**:

- 知 ㄓ zhī (to know), 吃 ㄔ chī (to eat), 是 ㄕˋ shì (to be), 日 ㄖˋ rì (day, sun)
- 字 ㄗˋ zì (character), 次 ㄘˋ cì (time), 四 ㄙˋ sì (four)

**Trap:** 四 sì is not "see", and 是 shì is not "she".

### a in ㄧㄢ ian

In **ian / yan**, the **a** moves toward "e": 天 tiān sounds close to "tyen", 錢 qián (money) close to "chyen". In **iang / yang** it stays "a": 想 xiǎng.

### o after b, p, m, f

**ㄅㄛ ㄆㄛ ㄇㄛ ㄈㄛ bo po mo fo** have a slight "w" glide: 波 bō sounds close to "bwo".

## Tones (聲調)

| Tone | Zhuyin mark | Pinyin mark | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (high, level) | none | ā | 媽 ㄇㄚ mā | mother |
| 2nd (rising) | ˊ | á | 麻 ㄇㄚˊ má | hemp |
| 3rd (low, dipping) | ˇ | ǎ | 馬 ㄇㄚˇ mǎ | horse |
| 4th (falling) | ˋ | à | 罵 ㄇㄚˋ mà | to scold |
| neutral (light, short) | ˙ | none | 嗎 ˙ㄇㄚ ma | question particle |

Note that the unmarked tone is different: **1st** in Zhuyin, **neutral** in Pinyin.

### Where the mark goes: Zhuyin

- Tone marks go **after** the syllable in horizontal text, and **to the right** of it in vertical text.
- The 1st-tone bar ˉ exists, but it is almost never written.
- The **neutral mark ˙ goes before** the syllable (above it in vertical text): 謝謝 ㄒㄧㄝˋ ˙ㄒㄧㄝ, 媽媽 ㄇㄚ ˙ㄇㄚ.

### Where the mark goes: Pinyin

The mark goes on a **vowel**. When there is more than one, follow these rules in order:

1. If there is an **a** or **e**, it takes the mark: hǎo, xiè, guān, lèi
2. In **ou**, the **o** takes it: zǒu, yǒu
3. Otherwise, the **last** vowel takes it: guó, liù, duì

When **i** takes a mark, it loses its dot: nǐ, xīn. **ü** keeps its dots under the mark: nǚ, lǜ.

Where marks can't be typed, tones are written as **numbers** after the syllable: ma1, ma2, ma3, ma4, and ma5 (or ma0) for neutral. ü is often written **v** or **u:**: nv3 / nu:3 = nǚ.

Words in dictionaries are listed with each syllable's **own** tone. In speech, some tones change next to each other; that is a separate topic.

## Pinyin spelling rules

Zhuyin writes each sound as it is. Pinyin has a few extra rules, all shown in the [finals tables](#medials-and-finals-介音--韻母) above.

### 1. y and w: finals with no initial

When an **i-**, **u-**, or **ü-** final has no initial, Pinyin starts it with **y** or **w**. They are spelling letters only, not extra sounds.

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| **i, in, ing** → add y | yi, yin, ying |
| other **i-** finals → i becomes y | ya, ye, yao, you, yan, yang |
| **u** → add w | wu |
| other **u-** finals → u becomes w | wa, wo, wai, wei, wan, wen, wang, weng |
| all **ü-** finals → add y, drop the dots | yu, yue, yuan, yun, yong |

### 2. Short forms: iu, ui, un

Three finals **lose their middle vowel** after an initial, but keep it with no initial. Zhuyin always shows it:

| Zhuyin | Full form | After an initial | No initial |
|---|---|---|---|
| ㄧㄡ | iou | 六 liù | 有 yǒu |
| ㄨㄟ | uei | 對 duì | 為 wèi |
| ㄨㄣ | uen | 春 chūn | 問 wèn |

The dropped vowel is still faintly heard: *duì* sounds close to "dway", *liù* close to "lyo".

### 3. ü and u

- After **n** and **l**, the dots are kept, because *nu* / *nü* and *lu* / *lü* are different syllables: 怒 nù (anger) vs 女 nǚ (woman), 路 lù (road) vs 綠 lǜ (green).
- After **j, q, x** and in **yu-**, the dots are dropped, because these never take a plain *u*.

### 4. Apostrophe

A syllable starting with **a, o, e** that is **not the first** in a word gets an apostrophe, so the syllable break is clear:

- 西安 **Xī'ān** (city name, two syllables) vs 先 **xiān** (first, one syllable)
- 平安 píng'ān (safe), 女兒 nǚ'ér (daughter), 天安門 Tiān'ānmén

Syllables starting with **y** or **w** don't need it, because y and w already mark the break: 中文 Zhōngwén.

### 5. Writing words and sentences

Zhuyin is written per character, but Pinyin is written **per word**:

- **One word, one block**: syllables of the same word are written together: 朋友 péngyou (friend), 學生 xuéshēng (student). Separate words are separated by spaces.
- **Capital letters** at the start of a sentence and for proper nouns: 台北 Táiběi, 中國 Zhōngguó.
- **Names**: surname first, then a space, then the given name as one word: 王小明 Wáng Xiǎomíng.
- **Retroflex -r (兒化)**: a syllable + 兒 is written with **r** added to it: 花兒 ㄏㄨㄚㄦ huār (flower), 哪兒 ㄋㄚˇㄦ nǎr (where). Common in Beijing speech, less in Taiwan.

> 我是學生。 → *Wǒ shì xuéshēng.* (I am a student.)

**Trap:** many names in Taiwan use **older romanizations**, not Pinyin: Taipei (= Táiběi), Kaohsiung (= Gāoxióng). Read them as names, not as Pinyin.

### Spelling differences at a glance

| Sound | Zhuyin | Pinyin |
|---|---|---|
| zhi, zi … | initial alone: ㄓ, ㄗ | initial + i |
| no initial | ㄧ ㄨ ㄩ as they are | y- / w- |
| iou, uei, uen | ㄧㄡ ㄨㄟ ㄨㄣ | iu, ui, un |
| ü after j q x | ㄩ | u |
| ong, iong | ㄨㄥ, ㄩㄥ | ong, iong |
| e sounds | ㄜ, ㄝ (two symbols) | e (one letter) |
| unmarked tone | 1st | neutral |

## Zhuyin symbols

### Where the shapes come from

The symbols come from **old characters or parts of characters** that had a similar sound:

| Symbol | From | Symbol | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| ㄅ b | 勹 | ㄕ sh | 尸 |
| ㄇ m | 冂 | ㄖ r | 日 |
| ㄈ f | 匚 | ㄙ s | 厶 |
| ㄉ d | 刀 | ㄚ a | 丫 |
| ㄋ n | 乃 | ㄧ i | 一 |
| ㄌ l | 力 | ㄠ ao | 幺 |
| ㄔ ch | 彳 | ㄦ er | 儿 |

### ㄧ changes direction

ㄧ is written **vertically (丨) in horizontal text** and horizontally (一) in vertical text, so it doesn't look like a dash or the character 一.

### Look-alike symbols

| Pair | Tell them apart |
|---|---|
| **ㄛ** o / **ㄜ** e | ㄜ has an extra short stroke on top |
| **ㄣ** en / **ㄥ** eng | ㄣ ends with a hook; ㄥ ends with a flat stroke. The sounds are also easy to mix up, so learn them as a pair. |
| **ㄢ** an / **ㄤ** ang | same issue: -n vs -ng. ㄤ comes from 尢 |

## Typing

### Pinyin keyboard

Type the syllables **without tones**, then choose the characters from a list:

- `nihao` → 你好
- `xiexie` → 謝謝
- Type **ü** as **v**: `nv` → 女, `lv` → 綠. After j, q, x, just type u: `xue` → 學.

Set the keyboard to **traditional characters** (繁體) to get 謝謝 instead of 谢谢.

### Zhuyin keyboard

The standard Zhuyin keyboard (大千 layout) puts **one symbol on each key**. Type the symbols of a syllable in order, then the tone key, and choose the character from a list.

| Key row | Symbols, left to right |
|---|---|
| 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 0 - | ㄅ ㄉ ˇ ˋ ㄓ ˊ ˙ ㄚ ㄞ ㄢ ㄦ |
| q w e r t y u i o p | ㄆ ㄊ ㄍ ㄐ ㄔ ㄗ ㄧ ㄛ ㄟ ㄣ |
| a s d f g h j k l ; | ㄇ ㄋ ㄎ ㄑ ㄕ ㄘ ㄨ ㄜ ㄠ ㄤ |
| z x c v b n m , . / | ㄈ ㄌ ㄏ ㄒ ㄖ ㄙ ㄩ ㄝ ㄡ ㄥ |

- **Space** = 1st tone (and confirms the syllable).
- The keyboard follows the recitation order: **ㄅㄆㄇㄈ** go straight down the first column (1, q, a, z), **ㄉㄊㄋㄌ** down the second (2, w, s, x).
- Example: 你 = **s u 3** (ㄋ ㄧ ˇ).

## Learning tips

- **Recite in groups**: *bo po mo fo, de te ne le, ge ke he, ji qi xi, zhi chi shi ri, zi ci si*, then the medials and finals. This is how Taiwanese children learn Zhuyin, and it works for Pinyin too.
- **Learn sounds, not letters**: don't read Pinyin with English letter values. Learn each sound straight from audio.
- **Learn both if you're going to Taiwan**: once you know one system, the other takes only a few weeks, since the sounds are the same.
- **Read children's books with Zhuyin** beside the characters to practise sounds and characters at once.
