# -고 싶다 vs -고 싶어하다

These two sentences look almost the same, but they talk about different people:

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 뭘 좀 먹**고 싶어요**. | **I** want to eat something. |
| 뭘 좀 먹**고 싶어해요**. | **They** (he / she) want to eat something. |

The difference is **whose** desire is being described. (Basics in [Modality](../basic/18_modality.md).)

## 1. -고 싶다: your own desire

**-고 싶다** states a desire directly, from the inside. Use it for:

- **The speaker:** 저는 뭘 좀 먹고 싶어요. (I want to eat something.)
- **The listener, in a question:** 뭘 좀 먹고 싶어요? (Do you want to eat something?)

## 2. -고 싶어하다: someone else's desire

Korean doesn't normally state another person's feelings directly with 싶다, because you can't feel them yourself. **-어하다** turns the feeling into something you can see from the outside: "they act like / show that they want to."

- 동생이 뭘 좀 먹고 싶어해요. (My younger sibling wants to eat something.)
- 친구가 한국에 가고 싶어해요. (My friend wants to go to Korea.)

**Trap:** 동생이 먹고 싶어요 sounds wrong as a plain statement. You're claiming to know your sibling's feelings from the inside.

## 3. The same rule for other feeling words

Feeling adjectives follow the same pattern (see [Adjectives](../basic/11_adjectives.md)):

| Your feeling | Someone else's feeling |
|---|---|
| 좋다 | 좋아하다 (to like) |
| 슬프다 | 슬퍼하다 (to be sad) |
| 무섭다 | 무서워하다 (to be scared) |
| 싫다 | 싫어하다 (to dislike) |

## 4. Exception: 좋아해 (I like you)

You'll often hear 좋아해 said about the speaker's own feelings. That's because **좋아하다 has become a normal verb meaning "to like"**, and it works for any person, including yourself.

| Form | Type | Particle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 좋다 | adjective ("is good / is liked") | 이/가 | 나는 네**가** 좋아. (I like you.) |
| 좋아하다 | verb ("to like") | 을/를 | 나는 너**를** 좋아해. (I like you.) |

- **네가 좋아:** describes how you feel right now; sounds a little more emotional.
- **너를 좋아해:** states the fact that you like the person.

**Trap:** 좋아해 is "I like you", the usual way to confess a crush. "I love you" is **사랑해**.

Other -어하다 verbs can also describe yourself when you mean a general tendency or habit:

- 저는 개를 무서워해요. (I'm afraid of dogs.)
- 저는 비를 싫어해요. (I don't like rain.)

The rule is strictest for **-고 싶다**. 저는 한국에 가고 싶어해요 sounds as if you're describing yourself from the outside, so say 가고 싶어요.

## 5. Spacing

- Standard: 먹고 싶어요, 먹고 싶어해요
- 싶어 해요, with a space, is also accepted.

## Note: 뭘 좀

- **뭘** = 뭐를 → 무엇을 (what / something)
- **좀** softens the request or wish: "a little, something"

뭘 좀 먹고 싶어요 sounds softer and more natural than 뭘 먹고 싶어요.
