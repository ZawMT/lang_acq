# Polite usage: 어떻게 되세요 and honorific words in practice

Korean raises a respected person in three ways: **-(으)시-** on the verb, **special honorific words**, and **humble words** for yourself. The word lists are in [Speech levels](../basic/07_speech_levels.md); this note is about how they're used in real sentences. (Slot order in [Stacking politeness](./04_verb_polite_forms.md).)

## 1. 되세요 = 되 + 시 + 어요

**되다** + honorific **-시-** + polite **-어요**:

```
되 + 시 + 어요 → 되세요
```

Here 되다 doesn't mean "become" in the sense of a change. It means **"come to, amount to, turn out as"**, in the present tense:

- 전부 얼마**가 돼요**? (What does it all come to?)
- 우리는 사촌이 **돼요**. (We're cousins.)
- 이 분은 제 선생님이 **되세요**. (This is my teacher.)

## 2. 어떻게 되세요?: asking for personal details

**N이/가 어떻게 되세요?** is the standard polite way to ask "What is your N?". Literally it is "How does your N go?" The indirectness is what makes it polite.

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **성함**이 어떻게 되세요? | May I have your name? |
| **연세**가 어떻게 되세요? | How old are you? |
| **전화번호**가 어떻게 되세요? | What's your phone number? |
| **가족**이 어떻게 되세요? | Tell me about your family. |
| **직업**이 어떻게 되세요? | What do you do? |

Compare with the direct question:

| Direct | Polite |
|---|---|
| 이름이 뭐예요? | 성함이 어떻게 되세요? |
| 몇 살이에요? | 연세가 어떻게 되세요? |

Use the polite version with strangers, older people and customers. 나이가 어떻게 되세요? sits in between, fine for adults around your age.

## 3. Honoring a person through their things

In 성함이 어떻게 되세요?, the grammatical subject is **성함**, not the person. It still takes -시-, because the name **belongs to** a respected person. Honoring what belongs to someone honors them indirectly. The same applies to their body, belongings, time and family:

- 시간 **있으세요**? (Do you have time?)
- 할아버지는 귀가 **밝으세요**. (Grandpa has good hearing.)
- 선생님 따님이 정말 **예쁘세요**. (Your daughter is really pretty.)

**Trap:** this only works for things closely tied to the person. It doesn't extend to products, prices or objects in a shop (see section 6).

## 4. Raising the doer vs lowering yourself

The same action can take different verbs depending on **who does it to whom**:

| Situation | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| between equals | 주다 | 친구한테 책을 **줬어요**. (I gave my friend a book.) |
| a respected person gives (to you) | 주시다 | 선생님이 책을 **주셨어요**. (The teacher gave me a book.) |
| you give to a respected person | 드리다 | 선생님**께** 책을 **드렸어요**. (I gave the teacher a book.) |

The same pattern holds for other verb pairs:

| Raise the doer | Lower yourself |
|---|---|
| 말씀하시다 (they speak) | 말씀드리다 (I tell them) |
| 물어보시다 (they ask) | 여쭤보다 (I ask them) |
| 만나시다 (they meet) | 뵙다 (I meet them) |

**말씀** works in both directions: it is honorific for **their** words and humble for **your** words.

- 선생님 **말씀**이 맞아요. (What you said is right.)
- 제가 **말씀**드릴게요. (Let me tell you.)

## 5. All the layers in one sentence

A fully honorific sentence switches several pieces at once:

| Plain | Honorific |
|---|---|
| 할머니**가** **밥**을 **먹어요**. | 할머니**께서** **진지**를 **드세요**. |
| 아버지가 **집**에 **있어요**. | 아버지께서 **댁**에 **계세요**. |
| 제가 할머니**한테** 선물을 **줬어요**. | 제가 할머니**께** 선물을 **드렸어요**. |

In everyday speech, **께서** is often dropped (할머니가 진지 드세요). The verb and the noun carry most of the respect.

## 6. Over-politeness in service speech

Shop and restaurant staff often attach -시- to **things** instead of people:

| What you'll hear | Why it's off | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| 커피 **나오셨습니다**. | the coffee isn't a person | 커피 **나왔습니다**. |
| 이 가격이 만 원**이세요**. | the price isn't a person | 만 원**입니다**. |
| 이쪽으로 오시면 **되세요**. | -시- is on 되다, not the customer | 오시면 **돼요** / **됩니다**. |

This is called **사물 존대** (honoring objects). It's very common and won't offend anyone, but it's grammatically wrong, so don't copy it.

## 7. Set phrases with 되세요

**되세요** is also used in polite wishes:

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 좋은 하루 **되세요**. | Have a nice day. |
| 즐거운 주말 **되세요**. | Have a nice weekend. |
| 새해 복 많이 받으세요. | Happy New Year. (no 되다, for comparison) |

Literally these say "Become a good day", so purists consider them odd. 좋은 하루 **보내세요** (Spend a good day) is the "correct" alternative, but everyone uses 되세요.

## Rule of thumb

- To ask for personal information politely, use **N이/가 어떻게 되세요?**
- -시- goes with a **person**, or with something that belongs to them, never with products or prices.
- When the action goes **from** a respected person, raise them (주시다). When it goes **to** them, lower yourself (드리다).
