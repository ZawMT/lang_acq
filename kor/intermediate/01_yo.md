# How 요 sounds in real speech

In textbooks, **요** is pronounced [jo]. In real speech, though, you'll often hear something closer to [jɔː], or even 여. Speakers aren't confusing the two syllables. There are three separate reasons for what you hear.

## 1. The standard sound: [jo], with no glide

Korean **ㅗ** is a single, pure [o]. Round your lips and keep them still until the vowel ends. Unlike English "yo", it doesn't glide into an "oo" at the end (see [Pronunciation](../basic/02_pronunciation.md)).

| Syllable | Sound | Lips |
|---|---|---|
| 요 | [jo] | rounded, held steady |
| 여 | [jʌ] | unrounded, mouth more open |

## 2. Sentence-final 요 is stretched and lowered

The polite ending **-요** comes at the end of the sentence, so it carries the sentence's intonation:

- **Questions:** rising pitch, as in 어디 가요↗?
- **Friendly or emphatic speech:** a drawn-out vowel, as in 맞아요~, 좋아요~

When the vowel is lengthened, the lips relax a little and it slides toward **[jɔː]**. It's still 요, just said loosely.

## 3. 요 → 여 in casual, cute speech

Seoul ㅓ is often pronounced fairly back and slightly rounded, so it already sounds close to [ɔ]. In casual or cute speech (**애교**), speakers really do swap **-요** for **-여**:

| Standard | Casual / cute |
|---|---|
| 했어**요** | 했어**여** |
| 그래**요** | 그래**여** |
| 배고파**요** | 배고파**여** |
| 안녕하세**요** | 안녕하세**여** |

You'll see this in texts, comments, and chat too. People choose it on purpose for the style. It isn't a spelling mistake.

**Trap:** don't use the 여 form in formal settings or with people you need to be polite to. It sounds childish or overly familiar.

## What to do yourself

- Say 요 as a clean **[jo]**: lips rounded, no glide.
- Recognize **[jɔː]** and **-여** when you hear them as normal variants of 요.
- Use **-여** only with close friends, and only if you want that playful tone.
