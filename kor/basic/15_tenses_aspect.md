# Tenses and Aspect (시제와 상)

Korean marks **tense** (past, present, future) with pre-final endings like -았/었- and -겠-, and **aspect** (ongoing, completed, resulting state) with auxiliary patterns like -고 있다 and -아/어 있다.

| Time | Main forms | Example (가다) |
|---|---|---|
| Present | -아/어요 | 가요 |
| Past | -았/었- | 갔어요 |
| Past (discontinued) | -았/었었- | 갔었어요 |
| Future | -(으)ㄹ 거예요, -겠- | 갈 거예요, 가겠습니다 |
| Ongoing | -고 있다 | 가고 있어요 |
| Resulting state | -아/어 있다 | 가 있어요 |

## Present

The plain present covers a lot of ground:

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| habit, general fact | 저는 매일 운동해요. (I exercise every day.) |
| happening now | 지금 비가 와요. (It's raining now.) |
| near future (with a time word) | 내일 서울에 가요. (I'm going to Seoul tomorrow.) |

## Past: -았/었-

Follows vowel harmony (see [Verb endings](./06_verb_endings.md)):

| Stem | Past stem | 해요체 |
|---|---|---|
| 가- | 갔- | **갔어요** |
| 먹- | 먹었- | **먹었어요** |
| 하- | 했- | **했어요** |
| 마시- | 마셨- | **마셨어요** |
| 춥- (ㅂ irr.) | 추웠- | **추웠어요** |
| 이- (이다) | 이었- / 였- | 학생**이었어요** / 의사**였어요** |

- 어제 영화를 **봤어요**. (I saw a movie yesterday.)
- 날씨가 **좋았어요**. (The weather was nice.)

The Korean past covers both "did" and "have done": 밥 **먹었어요**? (Have you eaten? / Did you eat?).

### Past forms for present states

Some verbs describe a **present state** in the past form, because the state is the result of an event that already happened:

| Past form | Meaning (now) |
|---|---|
| 결혼**했어요** | is married |
| 늦**었어요** | is late |
| 닮**았어요** | looks like, resembles |
| 잘생**겼어요** | is good-looking |
| 다 **됐어요** | it's ready / done |

- 민수 씨는 **결혼했어요**? (Is Minsu married?)
- 늦**었어요**! 빨리 가요. (We're late! Let's hurry.)

## Past discontinued: -았/었었-

Doubling the past marker shows that a past situation **no longer holds**, or happened in a more distant past:

- 서울에 **살았어요**. (I lived in Seoul.) (may or may not still)
- 서울에 **살았었어요**. (I used to live in Seoul.) (not anymore)
- 그 식당에 **갔었어요**. (I had been to that restaurant.)

## Future

Korean has several future forms, each with a different flavor:

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-(으)ㄹ 거예요** | plan or prediction (the everyday future) | 내일 **갈 거예요**. (I'm going to go tomorrow.) / 비가 **올 거예요**. (It'll rain.) |
| **-겠-** | intention (formal), conjecture, set phrases | 제가 **하겠습니다**. (I will do it.) / 맛있**겠어요**! (That looks tasty!) |
| **-(으)ㄹ게요** | promise or decision **to the listener** | 제가 **할게요**. (I'll do it [for you].) |
| **-(으)ㄹ래요** | what I want to do (casual) / asking what you want | 저는 커피 **마실래요**. (I'll have coffee.) / 뭐 **먹을래요**? (What do you want to eat?) |

**Trap:** -(으)ㄹ게요 and -(으)ㄹ래요 are **first-person** statements (and -ㄹ래요 can ask the listener). Never use them about a third person: ✗ 민수가 할게요.

**Trap:** -겠- is not only future. It shows **guessing** from what you see or hear:

- 피곤하**겠어요**. (You must be tired.)
- 비가 오**겠어요**. (It looks like it'll rain.)

And appears in fixed polite phrases: **알겠습니다** (Understood), **모르겠어요** (I don't know), **잘 먹겠습니다** (Thanks for the meal, before eating), **처음 뵙겠습니다** (Nice to meet you).

## Ongoing: -고 있다

**Stem + -고 있다** describes an action in progress:

- 지금 밥을 **먹고 있어요**. (I'm eating now.)
- 요즘 한국어를 **배우고 있어요**. (These days I'm learning Korean.)
- 할머니는 **주무시고 계세요**. (Grandma is sleeping.) (honorific: **-고 계시다**)

The plain present also works for "now": 지금 뭐 **해요**? = 지금 뭐 **하고 있어요**? (What are you doing now?). -고 있다 stresses that it's in progress.

Adjectives can't take -고 있다 (see [Adjectives](./11_adjectives.md)).

## Resulting state: -아/어 있다

**Stem + -아/어 있다** describes the **state that remains** after an action is complete. It's used with intransitive and passive verbs:

| -고 있다 (in progress) | -아/어 있다 (resulting state) |
|---|---|
| 앉고 있어요 (is in the middle of sitting down) | **앉아 있어요** (is seated) |
| — | 문이 **열려 있어요** (the door is open) |
| — | 꽃이 **피어 있어요** (the flowers are in bloom) |
| — | 불이 **켜져 있어요** (the light is on) |

**Trap:** verbs of **wearing** (입다, 신다, 쓰다, 끼다) use **-고 있다** for the state of having something on: 안경을 **쓰고 있어요** (She's wearing glasses). ✗ 써 있어요 would mean "is written".

## Retrospective: -더-

**-더-** marks something the speaker **personally witnessed** in the past and is now recalling. It appears mainly in:

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-더라고요** | "I noticed / found that..." | 그 식당 맛있**더라고요**. (That restaurant was tasty, I found.) |
| **-던** (modifier) | used to / was ...ing (unfinished, habitual) | 제가 자주 가**던** 카페 (the café I used to go to) |

More on -던 in [Modifier clauses](./20_modifier_clauses.md).

## Tense in modifier clauses

Before a noun, tense is shown by the modifier ending itself:

| | Verb (먹다) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Past | **-(으)ㄴ** | 먹**은** 음식 (food I ate) |
| Present | **-는** | 먹**는** 음식 (food I eat) |
| Future | **-(으)ㄹ** | 먹**을** 음식 (food I'll eat) |
| Past (habitual/unfinished) | **-던** | 먹**던** 음식 (food I used to eat / was eating) |

## Time words

| Past | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|
| 어제 (yesterday) | 오늘 (today) | 내일 (tomorrow) |
| 그저께 (the day before yesterday) | 지금 (now) | 모레 (the day after tomorrow) |
| 지난주 (last week) | 이번 주 (this week) | 다음 주 (next week) |
| 지난달 (last month) | 이번 달 (this month) | 다음 달 (next month) |
| 작년 (last year) | 올해 (this year) | 내년 (next year) |
| 아까 (earlier today) | 요즘 (these days) | 이따가 (later today) |
