# Modifier Clauses and Nominalization (관형절과 명사화)

A predicate can be changed so that it works as a **noun modifier** (before a noun) or as a **noun** itself. This is done with **transforming endings** (전성 어미) (see [Verb endings](./06_verb_endings.md)).

| Turns a clause into... | Endings | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a noun modifier | -는, -(으)ㄴ, -(으)ㄹ, -던 | [어제 **본**] 영화 (the movie I saw yesterday) |
| a noun | -기, -(으)ㅁ, -는 것 | [한국어를 배우**기**] (learning Korean) |
| an adverb | -게, -도록 | [맛있**게**] 드세요 (enjoy your meal) |

## Modifier endings (관형사형 어미)

The ending depends on the **word class** and the **time**:

| | Past | Present | Future / unrealized | Past (recalled, unfinished) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Verb** | -(으)ㄴ | -는 | -(으)ㄹ | -던 |
| **Adjective** | -던 / -았/었던 | -(으)ㄴ | -(으)ㄹ | -던 |
| **이다** | 이었던 / 였던 | 인 | 일 | 이던 |
| **있다 / 없다** | 있던 / 있었던 | 있는 | 있을 | 있던 |

Using 먹다 (to eat):

- 어제 **먹은** 음식 (the food I ate yesterday)
- 지금 **먹는** 음식 (the food I'm eating now)
- 내일 **먹을** 음식 (the food I'll eat tomorrow)
- 자주 **먹던** 음식 (the food I used to eat often)

Using adjectives:

- **큰** 집 (a big house), **작은** 방 (a small room)
- **맛있는** 김치 (tasty kimchi) (있다-adjective takes -는)

Irregular stems change as usual: 듣다 → 들은, 춥다 → 추운, 살다 → 사는 / 산 (see [Irregular verbs](./14_irregular_verbs.md)).

## Relative clauses

There's no relative pronoun ("who", "which", "that"). The whole clause goes **before** the noun, ending in a modifier form:

- [제가 어제 **산**] 책 (the book [that I bought yesterday])
- [안경을 **쓴**] 사람 (the person [who is wearing glasses])
- [한국에서 **온**] 친구 (the friend [who came from Korea])
- [우리가 처음 **만난**] 곳 (the place [where we first met])

Rules:

- The subject inside the clause takes **이/가**, not 은/는: [**제가** 산] 책.
- The noun being described is simply left out of the clause: [제가 어제 ___ 산] 책.
- Clauses can be long; everything before the noun belongs to it: [주말에 친구하고 같이 **간**] 식당 (the restaurant I went to with a friend on the weekend).

### -던 vs -(으)ㄴ vs -았/었던

| Form | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-(으)ㄴ** | completed past | 어제 **읽은** 책 (the book I read yesterday) (finished) |
| **-던** | habitual or unfinished past | 제가 **읽던** 책 (the book I was reading / used to read) |
| **-았/었던** | completed, and no longer the case; also memorable past | 작년에 **갔던** 카페 (the café I went to last year) |

## Patterns built on modifier + dependent noun

Many grammar patterns are just a modifier clause + a dependent noun (see [Nouns](./09_nouns.md)). The modifier ending is fixed for each pattern:

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-(으)ㄹ 때** | when | 어릴 **때** (when I was young) / 밥 먹**을 때** (when eating) |
| **-았/었을 때** | when (completed) | 한국에 **왔을 때** (when I came to Korea) |
| **-(으)ㄴ 적이 있다** | have (ever) done | 한국에 가 **본 적이 있어요**. (I've been to Korea.) |
| **-(으)ㄴ 지 (time) 되다** | it's been ... since | 한국에 **온 지** 2년 **됐어요**. (It's been two years since I came to Korea.) |
| **-(으)ㄴ 후에 / 다음에** | after | 밥을 **먹은 후에** 산책해요. (I take a walk after eating.) |
| **-기 전에** | before | **자기 전에** 샤워해요. (I shower before sleeping.) |
| **-는 동안** | during, while | 제가 **없는 동안** (while I'm away) |
| **-는 중이다** | be in the middle of | 지금 **회의하는 중이에요**. (I'm in a meeting now.) |
| **-(으)ㄹ 수 있다** | can | See [Modality](./18_modality.md) |
| **-(으)ㄹ 줄 알다** | know how to | See [Modality](./18_modality.md) |
| **-(으)ㄴ/는 줄 알았다** | thought (wrongly) that | 학생**인 줄 알았어요**. (I thought you were a student.) |
| **-는 대로** | as, as soon as | **도착하는 대로** 연락할게요. (I'll contact you as soon as I arrive.) |
| **-(으)ㄴ/는 것 같다** | seems | See [Modality](./18_modality.md) |
| **-(으)ㄴ 채(로)** | while keeping the state | 신발을 **신은 채로** 들어왔어요. (He came in with his shoes on.) |

**Trap:** **때** always takes **-(으)ㄹ**, whatever the tense: 먹**을** 때 (when eating), 어**릴** 때 (when young). For a completed past, add -았/었-: 먹**었을** 때.

**Trap:** 전에 takes **-기** but 후에 takes **-(으)ㄴ**: 먹**기** 전에 / 먹**은** 후에.

## Nominalization: turning a clause into a noun

Three tools, with different uses:

| Tool | Feel | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **-는 것** (spoken **-는 거**) | general, everyday | subject/object of 좋아하다, 싫어하다, 알다... |
| **-기** | an activity, not yet realized | fixed patterns (-기 전에, -기 쉽다...), lists, hobbies |
| **-(으)ㅁ** | a fact, completed | formal writing, notices, memos |

### -는 것

The most flexible nominalizer. 것 contracts in speech: 것이 → **게**, 것을 → **걸**, 것은 → **건**:

- 저는 요리하**는 것**을 좋아해요. (I like cooking.)
- 한국어로 말하**는 게** 어려워요. (Speaking in Korean is hard.)
- 여기서 사진 찍**는 건** 안 돼요. (Taking photos here isn't allowed.)

Tense changes with the modifier: 먹**은** 것 (what I ate), 먹**을** 것 (what I'll eat / something to eat).

### -기

- 제 취미는 책 읽**기**예요. (My hobby is reading books.)
- 이 단어는 외우**기** 어려워요. (This word is hard to memorize.)
- 비가 오**기** 시작했어요. (It started to rain.)
- 한국어 **말하기**, **듣기**, **읽기**, **쓰기** (speaking, listening, reading, writing skills)

-기 is fixed in many patterns: **-기 전에** (before), **-기 때문에** (because), **-기 위해서** (in order to), **-기로 하다** (decide to), **-기 쉽다 / 어렵다** (easy / hard to), **-기 좋다 / 싫다** (nice / unpleasant to).

### -(으)ㅁ

Used in formal writing and very short notices or memos, where it acts as a sentence ending:

- 오늘 회의 **없음**. (No meeting today.)
- 3시에 도착**함**. (Arrived at 3.)
- 그가 범인**임**이 밝혀졌다. (It was revealed that he was the culprit.)

Many ordinary nouns come from -(으)ㅁ: **잠** (sleep, 자다), **꿈** (dream, 꾸다), **춤** (dance, 추다), **웃음** (laughter, 웃다), **삶** (life, 살다), **기쁨** (joy, 기쁘다), **슬픔** (sadness, 슬프다).

## Adverbial clauses: -게, -도록

- 아이들이 [재미있**게**] 놀아요. (The children play happily.)
- [감기에 걸리지 않**도록**] 조심하세요. (Be careful not to catch a cold.)
