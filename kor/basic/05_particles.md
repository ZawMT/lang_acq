# Particles (조사)

A **particle** attaches directly to the end of a noun, pronoun, or number (**no space**) and tells you the role that word plays in the sentence. Because the particles carry the roles, word order can move around (see [Sentence structure](./04_sentence_structure.md)).

- Sentence: 민수**가** 도서관**에서** 친구**하고** 책**을** 읽어요. (Minsu reads a book with a friend at the library.)

## Consonant or vowel? Choosing the form

Many particles have two forms. Which one you use depends on whether the noun ends in a **consonant (받침)** or a **vowel**:

| Role | After a consonant | After a vowel | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| subject | **이** | **가** | 책**이**, 사과**가** |
| topic | **은** | **는** | 책**은**, 사과**는** |
| object | **을** | **를** | 책**을**, 사과**를** |
| and / with | **과** | **와** | 책**과**, 사과**와** |
| by / toward | **으로** | **로** | 책**으로**, 버스**로** |
| or / as many as | **이나** | **나** | 책**이나**, 사과**나** |
| and / with (casual) | **이랑** | **랑** | 책**이랑**, 사과**랑** |

**Trap:** words ending in **ㄹ** take **로**, not 으로: 연필**로** (with a pencil), 서울**로** (to Seoul).

**Trap:** 와/과 is written with the vowel form first, unlike 이/가 and 을/를, but the rule is the same: **과** after a consonant, **와** after a vowel.

## Overview

| Particle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 이/가 | subject | 비**가** 와요. (It's raining.) |
| 은/는 | topic, contrast | 저**는** 학생이에요. (I'm a student.) |
| 을/를 | object | 물**을** 마셔요. (I drink water.) |
| 에 | at (existence), to, at (time) | 집**에** 있어요. (I'm at home.) |
| 에서 | at (action), from (place) | 집**에서** 쉬어요. (I rest at home.) |
| 에게 / 한테 / 께 | to (a person) | 친구**한테** 줬어요. (I gave it to a friend.) |
| 에게서 / 한테서 | from (a person) | 친구**한테서** 받았어요. (I got it from a friend.) |
| (으)로 | by means of, toward, as | 버스**로** 가요. (I go by bus.) |
| 의 | 's (possession) | 친구**의** 집 (a friend's house) |
| 와/과, 하고, (이)랑 | and, with | 빵**하고** 우유 (bread and milk) |
| 도 | also, too | 저**도** 가요. (I'm going too.) |
| 만 | only | 물**만** 마셔요. (I only drink water.) |
| 부터 / 까지 | from / until | 9시**부터** 6시**까지** (from 9 to 6) |
| 보다 | than | 저**보다** 커요. (He's taller than me.) |
| 처럼 / 같이 | like | 가수**처럼** 노래해요. (She sings like a singer.) |
| 마다 | every | 날**마다** 운동해요. (I exercise every day.) |
| (이)나 | or; as many as | 커피**나** 차 (coffee or tea) |
| 밖에 | only (with a negative) | 천 원**밖에** 없어요. (I only have 1,000 won.) |
| 께서 | subject (honorific) | 할머니**께서** 오셨어요. (Grandma came.) |
| 아/야 | calling someone (casual) | 민수**야**! 지민**아**! |

## 이/가: subject

Marks the grammatical subject: who does the action or what the state is about.

- 고양이**가** 자요. (The cat is sleeping.)
- 날씨**가** 좋아요. (The weather is nice.)

Also used with **있다/없다** for what exists or what you have, and with **되다/아니다** (see [보어](./04_sentence_structure.md)):

- 저는 차**가** 있어요. (I have a car.)
- 저는 의사**가** 아니에요. (I'm not a doctor.)

Pronouns change shape before 가: 나 → **내가**, 저 → **제가**, 너 → **네가** (in speech often **니가**), 누구 → **누가** (who).

## 은/는: topic and contrast

Marks what the sentence is **about** ("as for X"), or sets X **in contrast** with something else.

- 저**는** 민수예요. (As for me, I'm Minsu. = I'm Minsu.)
- 고기**는** 안 먹어요. (Meat, I don't eat. [other things, yes])

## 이/가 vs 은/는

This is the most asked-about point in Korean grammar. Rules of thumb:

| Use 이/가 when... | Use 은/는 when... |
|---|---|
| the thing is **new** information | the thing is **already known** / the topic |
| answering a **question word** (누가, 뭐가) | making a **general statement** (facts, definitions) |
| describing what you **see happening** | **contrasting** with something else |
| inside a **modifying clause** | introducing yourself / changing topic |

- A: 누**가** 왔어요? (Who came?) B: 민수**가** 왔어요. (**Minsu** came.)
- 옛날에 한 왕**이** 살았어요. 그 왕**은** 욕심이 많았어요. (Long ago there lived a king. That king was greedy.)
- 개**는** 동물이에요. (Dogs are animals.)
- 저는 커피**는** 좋아하지만 차**는** 안 좋아해요. (Coffee I like, but tea I don't.)
- [제**가** 산] 책 (the book **I** bought): inside a modifying clause, the subject is 이/가, not 은/는.

Both can appear in one sentence: 저**는** 눈**이** 나빠요. (My eyesight is bad. Literally "As for me, the eyes are bad.")

## 을/를: object

Marks what the action is done to.

- 영화**를** 봐요. (I watch a movie.)
- 한국어**를** 배워요. (I learn Korean.)

In speech 를 often shortens to **ㄹ**: 나를 → **날**, 너를 → **널**, 무엇을 → **뭘**, 이것을 → **이걸**.

**Trap:** some predicates take **이/가**, not 을/를, for what looks like an object. They're usually adjectives or state verbs:

| Takes 이/가 | Takes 을/를 |
|---|---|
| 저는 사과**가** 좋아요. (I like apples.) | 저는 사과**를** 좋아해요. (I like apples.) |
| 돈**이** 필요해요. (I need money.) | 돈**을** 원해요. (I want money.) |
| 한국어**가** 어려워요. (Korean is hard.) | |

좋다 is an adjective ("to be liked/good"); 좋아하다 is a verb ("to like"). See [Adjectives](./11_adjectives.md).

## 에 vs 에서

Both can translate as "at", and mixing them up is a very common mistake.

| | 에 | 에서 |
|---|---|---|
| Place | where something **is / exists** (있다, 없다, 살다) | where an **action happens** |
| Movement | destination: **to** (가다, 오다) | starting point: **from** |
| Time | **at** a time: 3시**에**, 월요일**에** | — |

- 학교**에** 있어요. (I'm at school.)
- 학교**에서** 공부해요. (I study at school.)
- 학교**에** 가요. (I go to school.)
- 학교**에서** 왔어요. (I came from school.)

**Trap:** 오늘, 어제, 내일, 지금 **don't** take 에: 오늘 가요 (I'm going today), not 오늘에.

에 also means "per": 한 개**에** 천 원이에요. (It's 1,000 won each.)

## 에게 / 한테 / 께: to a person

For "to" with **people and animals**, use these instead of 에:

| Particle | Style |
|---|---|
| **에게** | neutral, common in writing |
| **한테** | spoken |
| **께** | honorific (to someone you respect) |

- 동생**한테** 선물을 줬어요. (I gave a present to my younger sibling.)
- 할머니**께** 전화를 드렸어요. (I called my grandmother.)
- 꽃**에** 물을 줘요. (I water the flowers.) (a thing, so 에)

"From a person" is **에게서 / 한테서**, often shortened to 에게 / 한테 with verbs like 받다 (receive) and 배우다 (learn): 선생님**한테** 배웠어요. (I learned it from the teacher.)

## (으)로: means, direction, role

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| means / tool | 젓가락**으로** 먹어요. (I eat with chopsticks.) |
| transport | 지하철**로** 가요. (I go by subway.) |
| direction | 오른쪽**으로** 가세요. (Go to the right.) |
| change into | 원을 달러**로** 바꿨어요. (I changed won into dollars.) |
| role / capacity | 선생님**으로** 일해요. (I work as a teacher.) |

에 = arrival point; (으)로 = direction toward: 서울**에** 가요 (go to Seoul) / 서울**로** 가요 (head for Seoul). Both are fine for most trips.

## 의: possessive

- 친구**의** 이름 (a friend's name)

Usually pronounced **[에]**. It's often **dropped** in speech: 친구 이름, 우리 엄마 (my mom).

Pronouns contract: 나의 → **내**, 저의 → **제**, 너의 → **네**: **제** 이름은 민수예요. (My name is Minsu.)

## 와/과, 하고, (이)랑: "and" / "with"

All three join nouns ("A and B") or mean "together with":

| Particle | Style |
|---|---|
| **와/과** | written, formal |
| **하고** | spoken, neutral |
| **(이)랑** | spoken, casual |

- 빵**과** 우유 / 빵**하고** 우유 / 빵**이랑** 우유 (bread and milk)
- 친구**하고** 영화를 봤어요. (I saw a movie with a friend.)

## 도 and 만: "also" and "only"

These **replace** 이/가 and 을/를 (and usually 은/는):

- 저**도** 학생이에요. (I'm a student too.) (not 저가도)
- 커피**도** 마셔요. (I drink coffee too.)
- 물**만** 마셔요. (I only drink water.)

With other particles they're **added after**: 학교**에서도** (at school too), 친구**한테만** (only to a friend).

## (이)나: "or", "as many as"

**이나** after a consonant, **나** after a vowel. It has several uses:

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **or** (between nouns) | 커피**나** 차 (coffee or tea) / 빵**이나** 떡 (bread or rice cake) |
| **...or something** (softens a suggestion) | 커피**나** 마실까요? (Shall we have a coffee or something?) |
| **as many as**, a whole... (surprise at a large amount) | 두 시간**이나** 기다렸어요. (I waited a whole two hours.) |
| **about** (asking for an estimate, with 몇 / 얼마) | 몇 명**이나** 왔어요? (About how many people came?) |
| **any-** (with question words) | 누구**나** (anyone), 언제**나** (always, any time), 어디**나** (anywhere), 아무거**나** (anything) |

- 주말에는 영화**나** 드라마를 봐요. (On weekends I watch movies or dramas.)
- 심심한데 산책**이나** 할까? (I'm bored. Shall we go for a walk or something?)
- 사과를 열 개**나** 먹었어요? (You ate ten apples?!)
- 이 노래는 누구**나** 알아요. (Everyone knows this song.)
- 아무거**나** 괜찮아요. (Anything is fine.)

"As many as" is the opposite of **밖에** (only, below): 열 개**나** 먹었어요 (I ate ten, that's a lot) vs 한 개**밖에** 안 먹었어요 (I only ate one).

To say "or" between two **clauses**, use the ending **-거나** instead: 책을 읽**거나** 쉬어요 (I read or rest). See [Connective endings](./19_connective_endings.md).

## Other useful particles

| Particle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **부터 / 까지** | from / until, up to | 월요일**부터** 금요일**까지** 일해요. (I work Monday to Friday.) |
| **에서 ... 까지** | from (place) to | 서울**에서** 부산**까지** (from Seoul to Busan) |
| **보다** | than | 동생이 저**보다** 키가 커요. (My sibling is taller than me.) |
| **처럼 / 같이** | like, as | 아이**처럼** 웃어요. (She laughs like a child.) |
| **마다** | every | 주말**마다** 등산해요. (I go hiking every weekend.) |
| **밖에** | only, nothing but (**needs a negative**) | 한 명**밖에** 안 왔어요. (Only one person came.) |
| **께서 / 께서는** | honorific 이/가 / 은/는 | 아버지**께서는** 회사원이세요. (My father is an office worker.) |

**Trap:** 밖에 must be followed by a negative predicate (안, 못, 없다, 모르다). 만 is used with positive predicates: 천 원**만** 있어요 = 천 원**밖에** 없어요 (I only have 1,000 won).

## Dropping particles

In casual speech, **이/가, 을/를, 의** are often dropped when the meaning is clear:

- 밥(을) 먹었어요? (Did you eat?)
- 나(는) 지금 가. (I'm leaving now.)

Particles with real meaning (에서, 한테, 도, 만, 부터) are **not** dropped.

## Stacking particles

Particles can stack, in a fairly fixed order: **role particle first, then 은/는/도/만**.

- 친구**한테는** (to a friend, at least)
- 서울**에서도** (in Seoul too)
- 저**만의** 방법 (my own way)

이/가 and 을/를 don't stack with 은/는, 도, 만; they get replaced.
