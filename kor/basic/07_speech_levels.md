# Speech Levels and Honorifics (높임법)

Korean builds respect into the grammar itself. Two separate systems work together:

| System | Question it answers | How it's marked |
|---|---|---|
| **Speech level** (상대 높임) | How polite am I to the **listener**? | the final ending: -습니다, -아요, -아... |
| **Subject honorifics** (주체 높임) | Do I respect the person I'm **talking about**? | -(으)시- in the verb, honorific words, 께서 |

They're independent. You can talk casually to a friend about your grandmother (할머니 오셨**어**, Grandma came) or politely to a stranger about yourself (제가 갔**어요**, I went).

## Speech levels (상대 높임법)

Traditional grammar lists six levels. Two of them (하오체, 하게체) are now old-fashioned and mostly seen in dramas and older speakers' speech. The four you need:

| Level | Name | Feel | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **합쇼체** | formal polite | official, deferential | news, announcements, presentations, service staff, the military, first meetings in business |
| **해요체** | polite | polite but warm | the default with strangers, colleagues, older people |
| **해체** (반말) | casual | close, relaxed | friends of the same age, younger people, family |
| **해라체** | plain | neutral written / blunt spoken | books, newspapers, diaries, essays; speaking down to children or close juniors |

**존댓말** (polite speech) = 합쇼체 + 해요체. **반말** (casual speech) = 해체 + 해라체.

### The four levels by sentence type

Using 가다 (to go) and 먹다 (to eat):

| | Statement | Question | Command | "Let's" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **합쇼체** | 갑니다 / 먹**습니다** | 갑니까? / 먹**습니까**? | 가**십시오** / 드**십시오** | 갑**시다** / 먹**읍시다** |
| **해요체** | 가**요** / 먹**어요** | 가요? / 먹어요? | 가**세요** / 드**세요** | 가요 / 먹어요 |
| **해체** | 가 / 먹**어** | 가? / 먹어? | 가 / 먹어 | 가 / 먹어 |
| **해라체** | 간**다** / 먹**는다** | 가**니**? / 먹**니**? (가냐? / 먹냐?) | 가**라** / 먹**어라** | 가**자** / 먹**자** |

Forms:

- 합쇼체: **-ㅂ니다** after a vowel (갑니다), **-습니다** after a consonant (먹습니다).
- 해체: the 해요체 form **minus 요**.
- 해라체 statement: verbs take **-ㄴ다/-는다** (간다, 먹는다); adjectives take **-다** (좋다); past takes **-었다** (갔다).

**Trap:** 갑시다 ("let's go") sounds like an instruction, so it's awkward to say to a superior. Use **가시죠** or **같이 가실까요?** (Shall we go together?) instead.

### 이다 at each level

| 합쇼체 | 해요체 | 해체 | 해라체 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 학생**입니다** | 학생**이에요** / 의사**예요** | 학생**이야** / 의사**야** | 학생**이다** / 의사**다** |

### Choosing a level

- **Strangers and adults you've just met:** 해요체. It's the safe default.
- **Formal settings (speeches, business, customer service):** 합쇼체, often mixed with 해요체.
- **Close friends your age, younger siblings:** 해체.
- **Switching to 반말** with someone is a relationship step. It's usually proposed by the older person: **말 놓을까요?** (Shall we speak casually?) / **말 편하게 하세요.** (Speak comfortably [casually] with me.)

Age matters a lot. Koreans often ask age early (**몇 살이에요?** / **나이가 어떻게 되세요?**) because it decides the speech level and what to call each other.

## Subject honorifics (주체 높임법)

When the **subject** of the sentence is someone you respect (older, higher status, a customer), add **-(으)시-** to the predicate:

| Plain | Honorific (해요체) | Honorific past |
|---|---|---|
| 가요 | 가**세요** | 가**셨어요** |
| 읽어요 | 읽**으세요** | 읽**으셨어요** |
| 바빠요 | 바쁘**세요** | 바쁘**셨어요** |
| 학생이에요 | 학생**이세요** | 학생**이셨어요** |

- 선생님이 지금 책을 읽**으세요**. (The teacher is reading a book now.)
- 할아버지가 부산에 사**세요**. (My grandfather lives in Busan.)

**Trap:** **never use -(으)시- about yourself.** 제가 가세요 is wrong; it's 제가 **가요**.

**Trap:** 가세요 has two meanings: "(someone respected) goes" and "please go". The context tells you which.

### Honorific verbs

Some common verbs don't just add -시-; they have a **separate honorific verb**:

| Plain | Honorific | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 먹다 / 마시다 | **드시다** (잡수시다) | to eat / drink |
| 자다 | **주무시다** | to sleep |
| 있다 (be somewhere) | **계시다** | to be (located), to stay |
| 있다 (have) | **있으시다** | to have |
| 말하다 | **말씀하시다** | to speak |
| 죽다 | **돌아가시다** | to pass away |
| 아프다 | **편찮으시다** | to be sick |
| 배고프다 | **시장하시다** | to be hungry |

- 할머니는 방에 **계세요**. (Grandma is in her room.)
- 사장님은 지금 **안 계세요**. (The boss isn't in right now.)

**Trap:** 계시다 is only "to be somewhere / to exist" for a person. For "to have", use 있으시다: 선생님, 시간 **있으세요**? (Teacher, do you have time?), not 시간 계세요.

### Honorific nouns

| Plain | Honorific | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 이름 | **성함** | name |
| 나이 | **연세** | age |
| 집 | **댁** | home |
| 밥 | **진지** | meal |
| 말 | **말씀** | words, speech |
| 생일 | **생신** | birthday |
| 사람 | **분** | person |

- 성함이 어떻게 되세요? (May I ask your name?)
- 할아버지, **진지** 드세요. (Grandpa, please eat.)

The suffix **-님** adds respect to titles: 선생**님** (teacher), 사장**님** (company president), 부모**님** (parents), 고객**님** (customer).

### Honorific particles

| Plain | Honorific |
|---|---|
| 이/가 | **께서** |
| 은/는 | **께서는** |
| 에게 / 한테 | **께** |

- 아버지**께서** 말씀하셨어요. (Father said.)
- 선생님**께** 선물을 드렸어요. (I gave a present to the teacher.)

These are more formal. In everyday speech, 이/가 + -(으)시- is enough: 아버지**가** 말씀하셨어요.

## Humble forms (겸양)

Instead of raising the other person, you can **lower yourself**:

| Plain | Humble | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 나 | **저** | I |
| 우리 | **저희** | we (not including the listener) |
| 주다 | **드리다** | to give (to someone respected) |
| 묻다 | **여쭈다 / 여쭙다** | to ask (someone respected) |
| 보다 / 만나다 | **뵙다 / 뵈다** | to see / meet (someone respected) |
| 데리다 | **모시다** | to accompany / take (someone respected) |
| 말 | **말씀** | (my) words |

- 제가 **드릴게요**. (I'll give it to you.)
- 뭐 하나 **여쭤봐도** 될까요? (May I ask you something?)
- 처음 **뵙겠습니다**. (Pleased to meet you. Literally "I see you for the first time.")

**Trap:** 주다 vs 드리다 depends on the **receiver**. Giving to a friend: 줘요. Giving to a teacher: 드려요. When someone respected gives to you: **주세요 / 주셨어요** (please give me / gave me).

## Putting it together

| Situation | Sentence |
|---|---|
| To a friend, about a friend | 민수 밥 먹었어? (Did Minsu eat?) |
| To a friend, about your grandmother | 할머니 진지 드셨어? (Did Grandma eat?) |
| To a stranger, about yourself | 저는 밥 먹었어요. (I ate.) |
| To your boss, about your boss | 사장님, 식사하셨어요? (Sir, have you eaten?) |
| Weather forecast | 내일은 전국이 맑겠습니다. (Tomorrow will be clear nationwide.) |
