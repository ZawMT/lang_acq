# Pronouns (대명사)

Korean pronouns change with **politeness**, not with case. The role (subject, object...) is shown by the particle after the pronoun, the same as with any noun. Pronouns are also **dropped** whenever the context makes them clear (see [Sentence structure](./04_sentence_structure.md)).

## Personal pronouns

| | Plain | Humble / polite | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **나** | **저** | 저 with anyone you'd speak 존댓말 to |
| we | **우리** | **저희** | 저희 excludes the listener |
| you (sg.) | **너** | (avoided) | 너 only to close friends / younger people |
| you (pl.) | **너희** | (avoided) | |
| he / she | **걔** (casual), **그 사람** | **그분** | 그 / 그녀 are mainly written |
| they | **걔네**, **그 사람들** | **그분들** | |

### Pronoun + particle contractions

Several pronouns change shape before a particle. These forms are very common:

| | + 이/가 (subject) | + 의 (possessive) | + 는 (topic, spoken) | + 를 (object, spoken) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 나 | **내가** | **내** | 난 | 날 |
| 저 | **제가** | **제** | 전 | 절 |
| 너 | **네가** (spoken 니가) | **네** (spoken 니) | 넌 | 널 |
| 누구 (who) | **누가** | 누구의 | — | 누굴 |

- **제가** 할게요. (I'll do it.)
- **제** 이름은 지민이에요. (My name is Jimin.)
- **내** 친구 (my friend)

Spoken Korean pronounces 네 (your) as **니** to keep it apart from 내 (my), since ㅐ and ㅔ sound alike.

**Trap:** 저 plus 가 is always **제가**, never 저가.

### 우리 for "my"

Korean often uses **우리** (our) where a single person owns something that's shared with a group, like family, home, or country:

- **우리** 엄마 (my mom), **우리** 집 (my home), **우리** 회사 (my company), **우리**나라 (our country, Korea)

In polite speech, the humble **저희** replaces it: 저희 회사 (our company).

## Avoiding "you"

There's no neutral polite word for "you". Instead:

| Instead of "you" | Example |
|---|---|
| **drop it** | 어디 가세요? (Where are [you] going?) |
| **name + 씨** | 민수 **씨**는 어디 살아요? (Where do you live, Minsu?) |
| **title / job** | **선생님**, 이거 뭐예요? (Teacher, what's this?) |
| **kinship term** | **언니**, 같이 가요! (Sis, let's go together!) |
| **고객님, 손님** | **손님**, 뭐 드릴까요? (What can I get you?) |

**Trap:** **당신** is in the dictionary as "you", but in conversation it's used between spouses, in song lyrics and ads, or as a confrontational "you" in an argument. Avoid it with people you've just met.

**Trap:** 씨 is not used with your own name, and it's too low for a superior. For people above you, use their title: **김 과장님** (Manager Kim), **박 선생님** (Teacher Park).

### Kinship terms used for non-family

Korean uses family words for friends and strangers too. For older friends, **the speaker's gender** matters:

| Older... | Said by a man | Said by a woman |
|---|---|---|
| male | **형** | **오빠** |
| female | **누나** | **언니** |

Younger people are called **동생** or by name. For strangers: **아저씨** (middle-aged man), **아주머니 / 아줌마** (middle-aged woman), **이모** (friendly, e.g. to a restaurant owner), **할머니 / 할아버지** (elderly woman / man).

## Demonstratives: 이 / 그 / 저

Korean has a three-way system:

| | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **이** | near the **speaker** ("this") |
| **그** | near the **listener**, or something **already mentioned** ("that") |
| **저** | far from **both** ("that over there") |

These combine with nouns into whole families:

| | 이 (this) | 그 (that) | 저 (that over there) |
|---|---|---|---|
| thing | 이것 (**이거**) | 그것 (**그거**) | 저것 (**저거**) |
| thing + 이/가 | **이게** | **그게** | **저게** |
| thing + 을/를 | **이걸** | **그걸** | **저걸** |
| place | **여기** | **거기** | **저기** |
| direction / person (polite) | **이쪽** | **그쪽** | **저쪽** |
| person (respectful) | **이분** | **그분** | **저분** |
| person (casual) | **얘** | **걔** | **쟤** |
| "like this" (adverb) | **이렇게** | **그렇게** | **저렇게** |
| "this kind of" (before a noun) | **이런** | **그런** | **저런** |

- **이거** 얼마예요? (How much is this?)
- **저기** 화장실이 있어요. (There's a restroom over there.)
- **이분**은 제 선생님이세요. (This is my teacher.)
- **그런** 말 하지 마. (Don't say that kind of thing.)

In speech, the shortened forms (이거, 그거, 저거, 이게...) are used almost always.

**Trap:** for something mentioned in the conversation but not physically present, use **그**, not 저: A: 어제 영화 봤어요. B: **그** 영화 재미있었어요? (A: I saw a movie yesterday. B: Was that movie good?)

## Question words

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **누구** (누가 as subject) | who | **누가** 왔어요? (Who came?) |
| **무엇 / 뭐** (뭐가, 뭘) | what | **뭐** 해요? (What are you doing?) |
| **어디** | where | **어디**에 살아요? (Where do you live?) |
| **언제** | when | **언제** 와요? (When are you coming?) |
| **왜** | why | **왜** 안 왔어요? (Why didn't you come?) |
| **어떻게** | how | **어떻게** 가요? (How do I get there?) |
| **어떤** | what kind of, which | **어떤** 음식 좋아해요? (What kind of food do you like?) |
| **무슨** | what (kind of) | **무슨** 일이에요? (What's the matter?) |
| **어느** | which (of a set) | **어느** 나라 사람이에요? (What country are you from?) |
| **몇** | how many | **몇** 살이에요? (How old are you?) |
| **얼마** | how much (price, amount) | 이거 **얼마**예요? (How much is this?) |

Question words stay in the position of the answer; the word order doesn't change (see [Sentence structure](./04_sentence_structure.md)).

### Question words as "some-" / "any-"

The same words also mean "someone, something, somewhere". Intonation decides:

- **뭐** 먹었어요? ↘ (What did you eat?) / **뭐** 먹었어요? ↗ (Did you eat something?)
- **어디** 가요? (Where are you going? / Are you going somewhere?)
- **누가** 왔어요? (Who came? / Did someone come?)

With **도** and a negative they mean "no one, nothing, nowhere":

- **아무도** 없어요. (There's no one.)
- **아무것도** 안 먹었어요. (I didn't eat anything.)
- **아무 데도** 안 가요. (I'm not going anywhere.)
- **아무 때나** 오세요. (Come any time.) (with **나**: "any at all")

## Reflexive: 자기, 자신

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **자기** | oneself (3rd person); also "honey" between couples | 민수는 **자기** 방에 있어요. (Minsu is in his own room.) |
| **자신** | oneself (emphatic, any person) | **자신**을 믿으세요. (Believe in yourself.) |
| **스스로** | by oneself (adverb) | **스스로** 했어요. (I did it by myself.) |
