# Adjectives (형용사)

A Korean adjective is a **descriptive verb**: it conjugates like a verb and forms a full predicate on its own, with no "to be" needed.

- 날씨가 **좋아요**. (The weather is nice.)
- 이 가방은 **비싸요**. (This bag is expensive.)

Dictionary forms end in -다 like verbs: 크다 (to be big), 예쁘다 (to be pretty), 좋다 (to be good). See [Word classes](./03_word_classes.md) for how they differ from verbs.

## Common adjectives

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 크다 (big) | 작다 (small) | 많다 (many, much) | 적다 (few, little) |
| 길다 (long) | 짧다 (short) | 높다 (high) | 낮다 (low) |
| 좋다 (good) | 나쁘다 (bad) | 쉽다 (easy) | 어렵다 (difficult) |
| 덥다 (hot, weather) | 춥다 (cold, weather) | 뜨겁다 (hot, to touch) | 차갑다 (cold, to touch) |
| 비싸다 (expensive) | 싸다 (cheap) | 빠르다 (fast) | 느리다 (slow) |
| 맛있다 (tasty) | 맛없다 (not tasty) | 재미있다 (fun) | 재미없다 (boring) |
| 바쁘다 (busy) | 피곤하다 (tired) | 아프다 (sick, hurting) | 배고프다 (hungry) |
| 기쁘다 (glad) | 슬프다 (sad) | 행복하다 (happy) | 무섭다 (scary) |

**Trap:** Korean splits "hot" and "cold" by what they describe: **덥다 / 춥다** for weather and how your body feels, **뜨겁다 / 차갑다** for things you touch (food, water).

Many adjectives are irregular (춥다 → 추워요, 빠르다 → 빨라요). See [Irregular verbs](./14_irregular_verbs.md).

## As a predicate

Conjugate exactly like a verb (see [Verb endings](./06_verb_endings.md)):

| | 작다 | 예쁘다 | 조용하다 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 해요체 | 작아요 | 예뻐요 | 조용해요 |
| Past | 작았어요 | 예뻤어요 | 조용했어요 |
| 합쇼체 | 작습니다 | 예쁩니다 | 조용합니다 |
| Honorific | 작으세요 | 예쁘세요 | 조용하세요 |
| Plain (해라체) | 작**다** | 예쁘**다** | 조용하**다** |

In the plain style (해라체) adjectives keep the **bare -다** form, while verbs take -는다/-ㄴ다: 작**다** (it's small) vs 먹**는다** (he eats).

## Before a noun: -(으)ㄴ

To put an adjective in front of a noun, add the modifier ending **-(으)ㄴ**:

| Adjective | Rule | Before a noun |
|---|---|---|
| 크다 | vowel stem + ㄴ | **큰** 집 (a big house) |
| 작다 | consonant stem + 은 | **작은** 방 (a small room) |
| 예쁘다 | vowel stem + ㄴ | **예쁜** 꽃 (a pretty flower) |
| 길다 | ㄹ stem: ㄹ drops, + ㄴ | **긴** 머리 (long hair) |
| 춥다 | ㅂ irregular: ㅂ → 우, + ㄴ | **추운** 날씨 (cold weather) |
| 빨갛다 | ㅎ irregular: ㅎ drops, + ㄴ | **빨간** 사과 (a red apple) |

**Trap:** adjectives ending in **있다 / 없다** take **-는**, like verbs: 맛있**는** 음식 (tasty food), 재미없**는** 영화 (a boring movie).

A noun + 이다 works the same way: 학생**인** 동생 (my younger sibling, who is a student).

For a past state, use **-던** or **-았/었던**: 어렸을 때 **작던** 나무 (the tree that used to be small). See [Modifier clauses](./20_modifier_clauses.md).

## Linking several adjectives: -고

- 이 방은 **넓고** 밝아요. (This room is spacious and bright.)
- **크고 예쁜** 집 (a big, pretty house)

Only the **last** adjective takes the modifier ending; the others use -고.

## As an adverb: -게

Add **-게** to turn an adjective into an adverb:

- 예쁘다 → 예쁘**게** (prettily): 예쁘게 웃어요. (She smiles prettily.)
- 크다 → 크**게** (loudly, big): 크게 말해 주세요. (Please speak up.)

More in [Adverbs](./12_adverbs.md).

## Becoming: -아/어지다

**-아/어지다** means "to become (more) ~" and turns the adjective into a verb of change:

- 날씨가 **추워졌어요**. (The weather got cold.)
- 한국어가 **쉬워지고** 있어요. (Korean is getting easier.)
- 더 **예뻐졌네요**! (You've become even prettier!)

## Comparison

| Meaning | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| more than | A**가** B**보다** (더) Adj | 동생이 저**보다** (더) 커요. (My younger sibling is taller than me.) |
| less than | B보다 **덜** Adj | 어제보다 **덜** 추워요. (It's less cold than yesterday.) |
| as ... as | B**만큼** Adj | 형**만큼** 키가 커요. (He's as tall as his brother.) |
| most | **제일 / 가장** Adj | 이게 **제일** 맛있어요. (This one is the tastiest.) |
| "among" | ...중에서 **제일** | 과일 **중에서** 딸기를 **제일** 좋아해요. (Of all fruits, I like strawberries best.) |

더 is optional when 보다 is present. 가장 is more formal than 제일.

## Feelings: -아/어하다

Adjectives of feeling (좋다, 싫다, 슬프다, 무섭다...) normally describe **the speaker's** own feelings. You can't directly see into someone else's feelings, so for a third person Korean turns the adjective into a verb with **-아/어하다** ("to show signs of feeling"):

| Adjective (my feeling) | Verb (someone's visible feeling) |
|---|---|
| 좋다 (to be liked, good) | **좋아하다** (to like) |
| 싫다 (to be disliked) | **싫어하다** (to dislike) |
| 슬프다 (to be sad) | **슬퍼하다** (to grieve, act sad) |
| 무섭다 (to be scared) | **무서워하다** (to be afraid of) |
| 부럽다 (to be envious) | **부러워하다** (to envy) |

- 저는 강아지가 **무서워요**. (I'm scared of dogs.)
- 동생은 강아지를 **무서워해요**. (My younger sibling is afraid of dogs.)

Notice the particle change: the adjective takes **이/가**, the -아/어하다 verb takes **을/를**. See [Particles](./05_particles.md).

**Trap:** 민수는 슬퍼요 (Minsu is sad) sounds odd because you're claiming to know his inner feeling. Say 민수는 **슬퍼해요**, 슬픈 **것 같아요** (he seems sad), or 슬프**대요** (he says he's sad).

## What adjectives can't do

Because they describe states, adjectives don't take:

- **commands or "let's"**: ✗ 예쁘세요! (as "be pretty!"), ✗ 조용하자. Use a verb: **조용히 하세요** (Please be quiet.)
- **-고 있다** (progressive): ✗ 바쁘고 있어요. Just 바빠요 (I'm busy).
- **-는** before a noun (except 있다/없다 adjectives): ✗ 예쁘는 꽃, ✓ 예쁜 꽃.

## Color words

Basic colors are ㅎ-irregular adjectives:

| Adjective | 해요체 | Before a noun |
|---|---|---|
| 빨갛다 (red) | 빨개요 | **빨간** 색 |
| 노랗다 (yellow) | 노래요 | **노란** 색 |
| 파랗다 (blue) | 파래요 | **파란** 색 |
| 하얗다 (white) | 하얘요 | **하얀** 색 |
| 까맣다 (black) | 까매요 | **까만** 색 |

Other colors are nouns + 색 (color): 초록색 (green), 보라색 (purple), 분홍색 (pink), 회색 (gray), 갈색 (brown).
