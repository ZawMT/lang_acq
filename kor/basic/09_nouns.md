# Nouns (명사)

Korean nouns are simple in form: **no gender, no articles, no case endings**. A noun never changes; everything it needs (role, "also", "only"...) is added with particles after it.

- 책 = a book / the book / books, depending on context.

## Plural -들

**-들** marks a plural, but it's **optional** and usually left out when the context is clear:

- 학생**들**이 왔어요. (The students came.)
- 사과 많이 샀어요. (I bought a lot of apples.) (no 들 needed)

-들 is most common with **people** and when you want to stress "each of them / all of them". With things, and after a number + counter, it's usually dropped:

- 학생 세 명 (three students), rather than 학생들 세 명
- 친구**들**하고 놀았어요. (I hung out with friends.)

## Three layers of vocabulary

(See [Hangul](./01_hangul.md) for more.)

| Layer | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **고유어** (native) | everyday basics | 물 (water), 집 (house), 사람 (person), 하늘 (sky) |
| **한자어** (Sino-Korean) | formal, academic, technical words (about 60% of the dictionary) | 학교 (school), 가족 (family), 시간 (time), 전화 (telephone) |
| **외래어** (loanwords) | modern items, mostly from English | 커피 (coffee), 버스 (bus), 컴퓨터 (computer) |

Many ideas have a native and a Sino-Korean word, with the Sino-Korean one sounding more formal: 이 (tooth) / 치아 (tooth, formal), 나이 (age) / 연령 (age, formal).

## Noun + noun

Nouns stack directly in front of each other, with no particle needed, to make compounds or descriptions:

- 한국 음식 (Korean food), 학교 식당 (school cafeteria), 커피 한 잔 (a cup of coffee)
- 눈 (eye) + 물 (water) → **눈물** (tears)
- 손 (hand) + 목 (neck) → **손목** (wrist)

Some compounds insert a **사이시옷** (a ㅅ added between the parts), which causes tensing: 바다 + 가 → **바닷가** [바닫까] (beach), 해 + 빛 → **햇빛** [핻삗] (sunlight). See [Pronunciation](./02_pronunciation.md).

## 하다 nouns

Many Sino-Korean nouns become verbs (actions) or adjectives (states) by adding **하다**:

| Noun | + 하다 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 공부 (study) | 공부하다 | to study |
| 운동 (exercise) | 운동하다 | to exercise |
| 전화 (phone call) | 전화하다 | to phone |
| 건강 (health) | 건강하다 | to be healthy |
| 행복 (happiness) | 행복하다 | to be happy |

Action nouns can also take 을/를 before 하다: 공부**를** 해요 = 공부해요 (I study). This lets you insert modifiers: 한국어 공부를 **열심히** 해요 (I study Korean hard).

For a passive-like meaning, many take **되다** instead: 시작하다 (to start something) → 시작되다 (to be started, to begin). See [Voice](./17_voice.md).

## Dependent nouns (의존 명사)

These nouns **can't stand alone**. They always need a modifier in front (a 관형사, a noun, or a modifying clause). They're the backbone of many grammar patterns.

| Noun | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **것 (거)** | thing, fact | 이건 제 **거**예요. (This is mine.) / 먹는 **것**을 좋아해요. (I like eating.) |
| **수** | way, possibility | 한국어를 할 **수** 있어요. (I can speak Korean.) |
| **줄** | how (to), the fact that | 운전할 **줄** 알아요. (I know how to drive.) |
| **데** | place; situation | 조용한 **데** 없어요? (Isn't there a quiet place?) |
| **적** | experience (time when) | 한국에 가 본 **적**이 있어요. (I've been to Korea.) |
| **때문** | because of | 비 **때문**에 못 갔어요. (I couldn't go because of the rain.) |
| **지** | time since | 한국에 온 **지** 1년 됐어요. (It's been a year since I came to Korea.) |
| **뿐** | only | 친구는 너**뿐**이야. (You're my only friend.) |
| **만큼** | as much as | 먹을 **만큼** 가져가세요. (Take as much as you'll eat.) |
| **대로** | as, according to | 말한 **대로** 했어요. (I did as you said.) |
| **분** | person (honorific) | 저 **분**은 누구세요? (Who is that person?) |
| counters | 개, 명, 마리... | 사과 두 **개** (two apples). See [Numbers and counters](./10_numbers_counters.md) |

These patterns are expanded in [Modifier clauses](./20_modifier_clauses.md).

## Common noun suffixes

| Suffix | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-씨** | Mr./Ms. (polite, after a name) | 민수 **씨** |
| **-님** | honorific | 선생**님**, 사장**님**, 고객**님** |
| **-들** | plural | 사람**들** |
| **-쯤** | about, around | 세 시**쯤** (around 3 o'clock) |
| **-짜리** | worth, costing / of that size | 만 원**짜리** (a 10,000-won one) |
| **-끼리** | among (only) ourselves | 우리**끼리** 가요. (Let's go, just us.) |
| **-씩** | each, at a time | 하나**씩** (one each / one at a time) |
| **-어 / -말** | language | 한국**어** / 한국**말** (Korean language) |
| **-사람 / -인** | person of (a country) | 한국 **사람** / 한국**인** (a Korean) |

## Nouns from verbs and adjectives

Any predicate can be turned into a noun with **-기**, **-(으)ㅁ**, or **-는 것**:

- 읽다 → 읽**기** (reading), 걷다 → 걷**기** (walking)
- 웃다 → 웃**음** (laughter), 기쁘다 → 기쁨 (joy), 자다 → **잠** (sleep), 추다 → **춤** (dance)
- 요리하다 → 요리하**는 것** (cooking)

When to use which is covered in [Modifier clauses](./20_modifier_clauses.md).
