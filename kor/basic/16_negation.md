# Negation (부정문)

Korean has two kinds of negation, and each comes in a **short** and a **long** form:

| | Short form | Long form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **안** (will / fact) | **안** + predicate | stem + **-지 않다** | don't, doesn't, isn't |
| **못** (ability / circumstance) | **못** + verb | stem + **-지 못하다** | can't, couldn't |

- 저는 고기를 **안** 먹어요. = 고기를 먹**지 않아요**. (I don't eat meat.)
- 저는 고기를 **못** 먹어요. = 고기를 먹**지 못해요**. (I can't eat meat.)

The short forms are more common in speech; the long forms are a bit more formal and common in writing.

## 안: "don't" (choice or fact)

**안** is placed directly before the predicate. It works with both verbs and adjectives:

- 오늘은 학교에 **안** 가요. (I'm not going to school today.)
- 이 김치는 **안** 매워요. (This kimchi isn't spicy.)
- 어제 **안** 왔어요. (He didn't come yesterday.)

Long form with -지 않다 (않다 then conjugates like a normal verb):

- 가**지 않아요**, 가**지 않았어요**, 가**지 않습니다**

## 못: "can't" (unable)

**못** says the action is **impossible**: lack of ability, or circumstances prevent it. It's used with **verbs**:

- 수영을 **못** 해요. (I can't swim.)
- 바빠서 **못** 갔어요. (I was busy, so I couldn't go.)
- 매운 음식을 먹**지 못해요**. (I can't eat spicy food.)

안 vs 못 changes the message:

| 안 | 못 |
|---|---|
| 숙제 **안** 했어요. (I didn't do my homework.) (I chose not to) | 숙제 **못** 했어요. (I couldn't do my homework.) (something prevented me) |

**Trap:** 못 is not used with adjectives: ✗ 못 예뻐요. Use 안: 안 예뻐요.

### 못하다: "be bad at"

As one word, **못하다** means "to be bad at": 노래를 **못해요** (I'm bad at singing / I can't sing). The softer **잘 못하다** is very common: 한국어를 **잘 못해요** (I'm not very good at Korean).

## Position with 하다 verbs

For **noun + 하다** verbs, 안 / 못 go **between the noun and 하다**:

| ✓ | ✗ |
|---|---|
| 공부 **안** 해요 | 안 공부해요 |
| 운동 **못** 했어요 | 못 운동했어요 |
| 전화 **안** 했어요 | 안 전화했어요 |

But **하다 adjectives** don't split: **안** 피곤해요 (I'm not tired), **안** 조용해요 (It's not quiet).

The long form avoids the question entirely: 공부하**지 않아요**.

## Words with their own negative

Some predicates don't use 안 at all. They have a separate negative word:

| Positive | Negative | ✗ Don't say |
|---|---|---|
| **이다** (to be) | **아니다** | ✗ 안 학생이에요 |
| **있다** (to exist, have) | **없다** | ✗ 안 있어요 |
| **알다** (to know) | **모르다** | ✗ 안 알아요 |
| **맛있다** (tasty) | **맛없다** | |
| **재미있다** (fun) | **재미없다** | |

- 저는 학생이 **아니에요**. (I'm not a student.)
- 시간이 **없어요**. (I don't have time.)
- 잘 **몰라요**. (I don't really know.)

## Negative commands and suggestions: -지 말다

안 and 못 **can't** be used for "don't!" or "let's not". Use **-지 말다** instead:

| Level | Negative command | Negative "let's" |
|---|---|---|
| 합쇼체 | 가**지 마십시오** | 가**지 맙시다** |
| 해요체 | 가**지 마세요** | 가**지 말아요** |
| 해체 | 가**지 마** | 가**지 말자** |

- 여기서 사진 찍**지 마세요**. (Please don't take photos here.)
- 걱정하**지 마**. (Don't worry.)
- 오늘은 나가**지 말자**. (Let's not go out today.)

**Trap:** 안 가세요 is not "don't go". It means "(someone respected) doesn't go" or "aren't you going?".

## Negative-only words

These words need a negative predicate after them:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **별로** | not particularly | **별로** 안 좋아해요. (I don't really like it.) |
| **전혀** | not at all | **전혀** 몰랐어요. (I had no idea.) |
| **절대(로)** | never | **절대** 말하지 마세요. (Never tell anyone.) |
| **하나도** | not a single one, not at all | **하나도** 안 추워요. (It's not cold at all.) |
| **아무도 / 아무것도** | nobody / nothing | **아무것도** 없어요. (There's nothing.) |
| **밖에** (particle) | only, nothing but | 한 개**밖에** 안 남았어요. (There's only one left.) |
| **아직** | not yet | **아직** 안 왔어요. (He hasn't come yet.) |

See [Particles](./05_particles.md) for 밖에 vs 만.

## Negative questions

The long form is used in questions that **expect agreement** ("isn't it?"):

- 춥**지 않아요**? (Isn't it cold?)
- 이거 너무 비싸**지 않아요**? (Isn't this too expensive?)

**Trap:** answering negative questions, **네** agrees with the question and **아니요** disagrees:

- A: 커피 **안** 마셔요? (You don't drink coffee?)
- B: **네**, 안 마셔요. (Right, I don't.) / **아니요**, 마셔요. (No, I do.)

See [Sentence structure](./04_sentence_structure.md).

## Double negation: "must"

Two negatives make a strong positive. The most useful one is **-지 않으면 안 되다** ("if you don't..., it's not okay" = must):

- 오늘까지 끝내**지 않으면 안 돼요**. (I have to finish it by today.)

The everyday way to say "must" is -아/어야 하다; see [Modality](./18_modality.md). "Cannot" in the sense of "not possible" is also covered there (-(으)ㄹ 수 없다).
