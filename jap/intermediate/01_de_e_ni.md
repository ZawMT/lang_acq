# で, へ and に

Both **に** and **へ** can mark where you're going, and with 行（い）く, 来（く）る, and 帰（かえ）る they're usually interchangeable. They don't picture the movement the same way, though, and only に has a large set of uses that have nothing to do with direction. **で** also marks a place, but a different kind: where an action happens (see [に vs で](#に-vs-で-where-something-is-vs-where-something-happens)).

**Pronunciation:** as a particle, **へ** is said **e**, not he (see [Kana](../basic/01_kana.md#three-particles-with-special-spelling)).

## Part 1: direction

### The core difference

| | に | へ |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | the **destination**: the point you arrive at | the **direction**: the way you're heading |
| Picture | ● (the end point) | → (the arrow) |
| Rough meaning | to, into, onto | toward, for |
| In speech | more common | a bit more written or formal |

- 東京（とうきょう）**に**行きます。 (I'm going to Tokyo. → Tokyo is where I'll end up)
- 東京**へ**行きます。 (I'm heading to Tokyo. → the trip, the heading)

In everyday sentences like these, the difference is small, and both are correct.

### Where only に works: arriving, entering, getting on

Verbs that describe **reaching or getting into** a point need に. へ sounds wrong or odd with them, because there's no "heading" involved, only the end point.

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| 着（つ）く (arrive) | 駅（えき）**に**着きました。 (I arrived at the station.) |
| 入（はい）る (enter) | 部屋（へや）**に**入ってください。 (Please come into the room.) |
| 乗（の）る (get on) | バス**に**乗ります。 (I get on the bus.) |
| 座（すわ）る (sit) | 椅子（いす）**に**座る。 (I sit on the chair.) |
| 置（お）く (put) | 机（つくえ）**に**本（ほん）を置く。 (I put the book on the desk.) |
| 書（か）く (write on) | ノート**に**名前（なまえ）を書く。 (I write my name in the notebook.) |

### Where へ fits better: a heading, not an end point

When the sentence is about **direction** with no clear stopping point, へ is the natural choice.

- 南（みなみ）**へ**向（む）かっています。 (We're heading south.)
- 前（まえ）**へ**進（すす）んでください。 (Please move forward.)
- 鳥（とり）が空（そら）**へ**飛（と）んでいった。 (The bird flew off into the sky.)
- 未来（みらい）**へ** (toward the future)

に is also possible in some of these (前**に**進む), but へ brings out the "toward" feeling.

### Where only へ works: before の

To turn the destination into a noun modifier, use **への**. ✗ にの doesn't exist.

- 日本（にほん）**への**旅行（りょこう） (a trip to Japan)
- 母（はは）**への**プレゼント (a present for my mother)
- 先生（せんせい）**への**手紙（てがみ） (a letter to the teacher)

### Addressing letters and messages: へ

On cards, letters, and notes, **name + へ** means "To ~". に isn't used this way.

- 田中（たなか）さん**へ** (To Tanaka-san)
- お母（かあ）さん**へ**、いつもありがとう。 (To Mom: thank you for everything.)

### Signs and announcements

Written guidance often uses へ:

- 出口（でぐち）**へ** (To the exit)
- 2番線（にばんせん）**へ**お進みください。 (Please proceed to platform 2.)

### Adding は and も

Both particles can take **は** and **も**:

| | に | へ |
|---|---|---|
| + は | 京都（きょうと）**には**行きました。 (Kyoto, I did go to.) | 京都**へは**行きました。 |
| + も | 大阪（おおさか）**にも**行きたい。 (I want to go to Osaka too.) | 大阪**へも**行きたい。 |

### Quick guide

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| 行く / 来る / 帰る + place | either (に is more common in speech) |
| arriving, entering, getting on, putting on | **に** |
| a general direction (south, forward, the sky) | **へ** is more natural |
| before a noun (〜の…) | **への** |
| "To ~" on a letter or card | **へ** |

## Part 2: uses that aren't about direction

### へ

へ is almost purely a direction particle. Apart from the extended uses above (letters, への), there's one more pattern worth knowing:

**〜ところへ**: just as (something was happening), (something else) came along.

- 出（で）かけようとしたところ**へ**、電話（でんわ）がかかってきた。 (Just as I was about to go out, the phone rang.)
- 困（こま）っていたところ**へ**、友達（ともだち）が来てくれた。 (Just when I was in trouble, a friend came along.)

Even here, the idea is that something "arrives" into the situation. ところ**に** is also used, with the same meaning.

### に

に has many uses that aren't about movement at all. The common thread is that に marks **a point** that something is aimed at, located at, or measured against.

#### Time

Clock times, dates, and days of the week take に:

- 7時（しちじ）**に**起（お）きます。 (I get up at 7.)
- 月曜日（げつようび）**に**会（あ）いましょう。 (Let's meet on Monday.)

**Trap:** relative time words (今日, 明日, 毎日, 来週) take no に (see [Particles](../basic/06_particles.md)).

#### Where something exists

With **ある** and **いる**:

- 公園（こうえん）**に**桜（さくら）の木（き）があります。 (There's a cherry tree in the park.)
- 兄（あに）は大阪**に**住（す）んでいます。 (My older brother lives in Osaka.)

Compare **で**, which marks where an **action** happens: 公園**で**遊（あそ）ぶ (play in the park). See the next section.

#### に vs で: where something is vs where something happens

| Particle | Marks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **で** | where an **action** happens | 図書館（としょかん）**で**勉強（べんきょう）する。 (I study at the library.) |
| **に** | where something **exists** | 図書館**に**本がある。 (There are books in the library.) |
| **に** | where something **ends up** | 図書館**に**行く。 (I go to the library.) |

働（はたら）く (work) is an action, so it takes で:

- あなたはどこ**で**働いていますか。 (Where do you work?)
- ✗ どこ**に**働いていますか

**Trap:** **住む** feels like an activity, but it takes に. Living somewhere is treated as being located there, like いる.

- 東京**に**住んでいます。 (I live in Tokyo.)
- 東京**で**働いています。 (I work in Tokyo.)

To ask about someone's job with に, use **勤（つと）める** (be employed at). It's about belonging to an organization, so it takes に:

- どこ**に**勤めていますか。 (Where are you employed?)
- 銀行（ぎんこう）**に**勤めています。 (I work at a bank.)

**Same verb, two places:** with one verb, に and で can mark different places.

- 黒板（こくばん）**に**書く。 (write **on** the blackboard → where the writing ends up)
- 教室（きょうしつ）**で**書く。 (write **in** the classroom → where the action happens)

**Tips for the question itself:**

- For someone's current job, **働いていますか** is more natural than 働きますか. 働きますか sounds like a question about the future or a habit.
- **あなた** is usually dropped, or replaced with name + さん: 田中さんはどこで働いていますか。

#### Recipient: to someone

- 友達**に**本を貸（か）した。 (I lent a book to a friend.)
- 母**に**電話した。 (I called my mother.)

#### Source or agent: from / by someone

With **もらう**, **習（なら）う**, **聞く**, and with **passive** verbs, に marks the person the thing comes from:

- 先生**に**褒（ほ）められた。 (I was praised by the teacher.)
- 姉（あね）**に**ピアノを習った。 (I learned piano from my older sister.)
- 祖母（そぼ）**に**時計（とけい）をもらった。 (I got a watch from my grandmother.)

With **causative** verbs, に marks the person made or allowed to do something:

- 子供（こども）**に**野菜（やさい）を食べさせる。 (I make my child eat vegetables.)

#### Purpose: going to do

**Verb stem + に + 行く / 来る / 帰る**:

- 映画（えいが）を見**に**行く。 (I'm going to see a movie.)
- 昼（ひる）ご飯（はん）を食べ**に**帰ります。 (I'm going home to have lunch.)

#### Result of a change

With **なる** (become) and **する** (make into, decide on):

- 医者（いしゃ）**に**なりたい。 (I want to become a doctor.)
- 部屋をきれい**に**した。 (I made the room clean.)
- コーヒー**に**します。 (I'll have coffee. → I decide on coffee)

#### Turning な-adjectives into adverbs

- 静（しず）か**に**話してください。 (Please speak quietly.)
- 本当（ほんとう）**に**ありがとう。 (Thank you, really.)

#### Rate and frequency

- 週（しゅう）**に**2回（にかい）ジムに行く。 (I go to the gym twice a week.)
- 3人（さんにん）**に**1人（ひとり） (one in three people)

#### Point of reference: for, to, toward

The thing something is judged against or affects:

- この問題（もんだい）は私（わたし）**には**難（むずか）しい。 (This question is hard for me.)
- 野菜は体（からだ）**に**いい。 (Vegetables are good for your body.)
- 駅**に**近（ちか）い (close to the station)
- 父（ちち）**に**似（に）ている (look like my father)
- 音楽（おんがく）**に**興味（きょうみ）がある (be interested in music)

#### Adding one item to another

**A に B**: A, with B added. Here に means "with" or "plus", not "to".

- ケーキ**に**紅茶（こうちゃ） (cake with tea)
- 着物（きもの）**に**下駄（げた） (a kimono with geta)
- ご飯**に**味噌汁（みそしる） (rice with miso soup)

This comes from the "onto" sense of に (机に本を置く, put a book on the desk): B is put on top of A, "A, and on top of that, B".

| | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A **と** B | A and B, a simple neutral list | ケーキ**と**紅茶 (cake and tea) |
| A **に** B | A, with B added; often a **natural pairing** | ケーキ**に**紅茶 (cake, and tea to go with it) |

**A に B に C**: chain it to pile items up one after another. It often suggests the list is long, or a bit much.

- 卵（たまご）**に**パン**に**牛乳（ぎゅうにゅう）… (eggs, bread, milk, and so on)
- 仕事（しごと）**に**家事（かじ）**に**育児（いくじ）で忙（いそが）しい。 (I'm busy with work, housework, and childcare.)

### Fixed expressions built on に

These work almost like single words and come up constantly:

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜**について** | about | 日本の歴史（れきし）**について**話す。 (talk about Japanese history) |
| 〜**にとって** | for, from the viewpoint of | 私**にとって**大切（たいせつ）な人 (a person important to me) |
| 〜**によって** | depending on; by | 人**によって**違（ちが）う。 (It differs from person to person.) |
| 〜**に対（たい）して** | toward, against | 客（きゃく）**に対して**丁寧（ていねい）に話す。 (speak politely to customers) |
| 〜**によると** | according to | 天気予報（てんきよほう）**によると**、雨（あめ）だ。 (According to the forecast, it'll rain.) |
| 〜**のために** | for (the sake of) | 家族（かぞく）**のために**働く。 (work for my family) |

## Coming down to some specific usage

### "I don't know what will happen in the future": 未来に or 未来へ?

Usually **neither**. The most natural versions use no particle at all, or rephrase:

- 未来（みらい）のことはわからない。 (I don't know about the future.) → the most common way to say it
- これから何（なに）が起（お）こるかわからない。 (I don't know what will happen from now on.) → very natural in conversation
- 将来（しょうらい）何が起こるかわからない。 (I don't know what will happen in the future.) → 将来 is a time word, so no particle

Why not the particles:

- **未来に** marks a point in time, so 未来**に**何が起こるかわからない is grammatically fine, but it sounds stiff or bookish.
- **未来へ** means "toward the future" (see [Where へ fits better](#where-へ-fits-better-a-heading-not-an-end-point)). It suits slogans and titles like 未来**へ**進（すす）もう (Let's move toward the future), but here nothing is moving toward anything.

**Tip:** 未来 sounds abstract, like "the Future" as a concept. For your own future, 将来 or これから sounds more natural. Like other relative time words (今日, 明日, 来年), they take no に.
