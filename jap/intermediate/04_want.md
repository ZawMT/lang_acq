# Wanting: たい, ほしい, たがる, ほしがる

Japanese has two words for "want", and each one changes form when the desire belongs to someone else:

| | Your own desire | Someone else's desire |
|---|---|---|
| want **to do** | 食（た）べ**たい** | 食べ**たがる** |
| want **a thing** | カメラが**ほしい** | カメラを**ほしがる** |

(Basics in [Modality](../basic/18_modality.md) and [Adjectives](../basic/12_adjectives.md#feelings-only-for-yourself).)

## 1. たい vs ほしい

- **ます stem + たい**: wanting to **do** something. 日本（にほん）へ行（い）き**たい**。 (I want to go to Japan.)
- **noun + が ほしい**: wanting to **have** something. 新（あたら）しい靴（くつ）**が ほしい**。 (I want new shoes.)
- **〜て ほしい**: wanting **someone else** to do something. 早（はや）く来（き）て**ほしい**。 (I want you to come early.)

Both たい and ほしい conjugate like い-adjectives:

| | たい | ほしい |
|---|---|---|
| negative | 行きた**くない** | ほし**くない** |
| past | 行きた**かった** | ほし**かった** |
| polite | 行きたい**です** | ほしい**です** |

**Trap:** don't use ほしい for actions. 行くがほしい ✗ → 行き**たい** ✓.

With たい, the object can take **が** or **を**: 水（みず）**が**飲（の）みたい / 水**を**飲みたい (I want to drink water). が puts more weight on the thing wanted.

## 2. たい and ほしい: your own desire

These words state a desire from the inside, so they're used for:

- **The speaker:** 私（わたし）は少（すこ）し休（やす）み**たい**。 (I want to rest a bit.)
- **The listener, in a question:** 何（なに）が**ほしい**？ (What do you want?)

**Trap:** 弟（おとうと）はカメラがほしい sounds wrong as a plain statement. You're claiming to know your brother's feelings from the inside.

## 3. たがる and ほしがる: someone else's desire

**〜がる** turns the feeling into something visible from the outside: "shows signs of wanting".

- 弟が新しいカメラ**を ほしがって いる**。 (My younger brother wants a new camera.)
- 子供（こども）が外（そと）で遊（あそ）び**たがって いる**。 (The kids want to play outside.)
- 娘（むすめ）が寝（ね）**たがらない**。 (My daughter doesn't want to go to bed.)

Points to remember:

1. **The particle changes to を.** カメラ**が** ほしい → カメラ**を** ほしがる.
2. **ている is usually added** for what someone wants right now: ほしがって**いる**, 行きたがって**いる**. Plain ほしがる / たがる sounds like a habit or general tendency:
   - 子供は甘（あま）いもの**を ほしがる**ものだ。 (Kids always want sweets.)
3. **がる verbs conjugate like godan verbs:** たがる → たがら**ない**, たがり**ます**, たがっ**た**.
4. **がる describes behavior from the outside.** It can sound like a comment on someone acting childish or insistent: 行きたがっている ≈ "keeps saying he wants to go". Avoid it for people you should respect, such as your boss or a customer.

## 4. Softer ways to describe someone else's desire

In everyday speech, these are often more natural than がる:

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜たい **と言（い）って いる** | says they want | 田中（たなか）さんは留学（りゅうがく）したい**と言っています**。 (Tanaka-san says they want to study abroad.) |
| 〜たい **そうだ** | I hear they want | 兄（あに）は車（くるま）がほしい**そうです**。 (I hear my brother wants a car.) |
| 〜たい **らしい** / **みたい** | it seems they want | 彼（かれ）は帰（かえ）りたい**みたい**。 (He seems to want to go home.) |
| 〜た **そうだ** / ほし **そうだ** | looks like they want | 犬（いぬ）が散歩（さんぽ）に行きた**そうな**顔（かお）をしている。 (The dog looks like it wants to go for a walk.) |

**Trap:** 行きたい**そうだ** (I hear) and 行きた**そうだ** (looks like) are different. Hearsay keeps the full たい; "looks like" drops the い.

## 5. When たい is fine for anyone

The "own feelings only" rule applies to the **end of a sentence**. Inside a modifier or condition, たい and ほしい work for anyone:

- 留学したい**学生**は申（もう）し込（こ）んでください。 (Students who want to study abroad, please apply.)
- 行きたい**なら**、行けばいい。 (If you want to go, go.)
- ほしい**人**にあげます。 (I'll give it to whoever wants it.)

In explanations with **のだ / んです**, and in novels, a third person's desire can be stated directly:

- 彼は本当（ほんとう）は帰りたい**んです**。 (The truth is, he wants to go home.)
- 彼女（かのじょ）はただ、誰（だれ）かに話（はな）を聞（き）いてほしかった。 (She just wanted someone to listen to her.) (narration)

## 6. Asking what someone wants, politely

〜たいですか and ほしいですか ask about someone's inner feelings directly. That's fine with friends, but it sounds too direct for a superior or customer. Offer or invite instead:

| Direct | Polite |
|---|---|
| コーヒーが飲みたいですか | コーヒーは**いかがですか** (Would you like some coffee?) |
| 一緒（いっしょ）に行きたいですか | 一緒に行き**ませんか** (Won't you come with us?) |
| 何が食べたいですか | 何を**召（め）し上（あ）がりますか** (What would you like to eat?) |
| これがほしいですか | こちらは**いかがでしょうか** (How about this one?) |

## 7. The same rule for other feeling words

がる works with other adjectives of feeling and sensation:

| Your feeling | Someone else's feeling |
|---|---|
| 怖（こわ）い (scared) | 怖**がる** (be scared of) |
| 嫌（いや）だ (hate) | 嫌**がる** (show dislike, resist) |
| 寂（さび）しい (lonely) | 寂し**がる** (act lonely, miss someone) |
| 痛（いた）い (painful) | 痛**がる** (complain of pain) |
| 悲（かな）しい (sad) | 悲し**がる** (act sad) |
| 恥（は）ずかしい (embarrassed) | 恥ずかし**がる** (be shy) |

- 娘は犬**を** 怖がって いる。 (My daughter is scared of the dog.)
- 猫（ねこ）が薬（くすり）を飲むの**を** 嫌がる。 (The cat hates taking its medicine.)

Some がる verbs also describe yourself when you mean a general tendency:

- 私は暗（くら）い所（ところ）を怖がる子供だった。 (I was a child who was scared of the dark.)
