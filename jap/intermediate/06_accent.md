# Pitch accent (アクセント)

How to read pitch notation, where to look it up, and how pitch behaves in verbs, adjectives and phrases. (Basics in [Pronunciation](../basic/03_pronunciation.md#pitch-accent-アクセント).)

Normal writing never shows pitch: kana, kanji and romaji all leave it out. Only accent dictionaries and learning tools mark it. These notes follow standard Tokyo speech.

## 1. Counting morae

Pitch is counted in **morae** (beats), not syllables. Each of these is one mora:

- a normal kana: か, し
- ん: にほん = に・ほ・ん (3)
- small っ: きって = き・っ・て (3)
- a long vowel: とうきょう = と・う・きょ・う (4)

A small ゃ ゅ ょ **joins** the kana before it: きょ is one mora, not two.

## 2. Writing pitch down

There's no single standard. Three styles are common.

### Numbers

The number says **after which mora the pitch falls**. [0] means it never falls.

| Number | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| [0] | never falls (平板（へいばん）) | 飴（あめ） [0] (candy): L-H |
| [1] | falls after the 1st mora (頭高（あたまだか）) | 雨（あめ） [1] (rain): H-L |
| [2], [3]… smaller than the mora count | falls in the middle (中高（なかだか）) | お菓子（おかし） [2] (sweets): L-H-L |
| same as the mora count | falls after the last mora (尾高（おだか）) | 橋（はし） [2] (bridge): L-H, then a low particle |

One number is enough because a word has **at most one fall**.

### Lines

A line runs **over the high morae** and turns down where the pitch falls. A line that runs to the end without turning down means [0].

### Downstep mark

A **ꜜ** goes right after the mora where the pitch falls:

- あꜜめ = 雨 [1]
- あめ = 飴 [0] (no mark)
- おかꜜし = お菓子 [2]
- はしꜜ = 橋 [2]

## 3. Where to look it up

- **Accent dictionaries:** *NHK 日本語発音アクセント新辞典*, *新明解日本語アクセント辞典*.
- **Some general dictionaries:** 大辞林（だいじりん） and 新明解国語辞典（しんめいかいこくごじてん） print the number next to each entry.
- **OJAD** (Online Japanese Accent Dictionary): shows verb and adjective forms, and marks pitch on any sentence you paste in.
- **Wiktionary:** most Japanese entries list the accent.
- **Yomitan** (browser pop-up dictionary): shows pitch once a pitch dictionary is added.

## 5. Verbs

Every verb is one of two types:

1. **Unaccented [0]:** no fall. 行（い）く, 遊（あそ）ぶ, 着る, 使（つか）う, 始（はじ）める
2. **Accented:** usually falls on the **second-to-last mora** of the dictionary form. かꜜく 書（か）く, よꜜむ 読（よ）む, みꜜる 見（み）る, たべꜜる 食（た）べる

The type stays the same when the verb is conjugated, but the fall moves:

| Form | Accented: 食べる | Unaccented: 行く |
|---|---|---|
| dictionary | たべꜜる | いく |
| ます | たべまꜜす | いきまꜜす |
| て | たꜜべて | いって |
| た | たꜜべた | いった |
| ない | たべꜜない | いかない |

- **ます always falls on ま**, whatever the verb type.
- In the て and た forms, an accented verb falls **earlier** (たꜜべて), and an unaccented verb stays flat.

## 6. Adjectives

い-adjectives also split into two types:

1. **Unaccented [0]:** 赤（あか）い, 甘（あま）い, 冷（つめ）たい
2. **Accented:** falls on the second-to-last mora. たかꜜい 高（たか）い, しろꜜい 白（しろ）い, あつꜜい 暑（あつ）い

In accented adjectives, the fall moves earlier when conjugated: たꜜかくて, たꜜかかった. Many younger speakers say たかꜜかった instead.

## 7. Pitch across a phrase

1. **Particles follow the word.** After a [0] word a particle stays high, and after a fall it's low.
   - 飴が: あめが L-H-H
   - 雨が: あꜜめが H-L-L
2. **One fall per phrase.** After the fall, everything stays low until the next phrase starts.
3. **Compounds get one new accent** for the whole word: 日本（にほん） にꜜほん [2] → 日本語（にほんご） にほんご [0].
