# Aizuchi: Listener Responses (相槌)

**相槌（あいづち）** are the short responses a listener gives **while** someone is speaking: うん, はい, そうですね, へえ, なるほど. They tell the speaker "I'm listening, go on."

The word comes from blacksmithing: two smiths taking turns hammering the same piece of metal (槌, つち = hammer). A conversation is built by **both** people, in rhythm.

## Why they matter

In Japanese conversation, listeners respond **often**: in the pauses of almost every sentence, not just at the end. Nodding counts too.

- With aizuchi, the speaker feels heard and keeps going.
- Without them, the speaker may stop and ask **聞（き）いてる？** (Are you listening?), or think you're confused or disagree.

This is especially true on the **phone**, where nodding can't be seen.

## Basic aizuchi

| Aizuchi | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **はい** | polite | yes, I'm listening |
| **ええ** | polite, soft | yes, mm |
| **うん** | casual | yeah, uh-huh |
| **そうですか** | polite | is that so, I see |
| **そうなんですか** | polite | oh, really? (new information) |
| **そう / そうなんだ** | casual | oh, I see |
| **そうですね** | polite | that's right, yes indeed (agreeing) |
| **そうだね / そうだよね** | casual | yeah, right |
| **なるほど** | neutral | I see, that makes sense |
| **へえ** | neutral | oh, wow (interest, surprise) |
| **本当（ほんとう）ですか / ほんと?** | polite / casual | really? |
| **確（たし）かに** | neutral | true, that's right |

## What each one signals

### Listening: go on

**はい, ええ, うん** are the most frequent. They mean "I hear you", **not** necessarily "I agree":

- A: 昨日（きのう）、駅（えき）の前（まえ）で… B: **うん**。 A: 田中（たなか）さんに会（あ）って… B: **うん、うん**。 A: それで…

**Trap:** **はい** as aizuchi doesn't mean "yes, I agree". A Japanese listener may say はい, はい all the way through and then decline at the end. Don't take each はい as a yes.

### Understanding

- **そうですか** (↘ falling): I see. Calm acknowledgment.
- **なるほど**: I see, that makes sense.
- **そうなんですか / そうなんだ**: Oh, I didn't know that.

**Trap:** **なるほど** said to a superior can sound like you're **judging** their explanation ("that checks out"). Repeating なるほど many times can sound careless. With superiors, use **そうなんですね**, **勉強（べんきょう）になります** (that's very helpful to know), or **おっしゃる通（とお）りです** (exactly as you say).

### Agreement

- **そうですね**: Yes, that's right.
- **確かに**: True.
- **ですよね / だよね**: Exactly, right?
- **わかります / わかる**: I know what you mean. (empathy)

### Surprise and interest

| Aizuchi | Feeling |
|---|---|
| **へえ** (long, rising-falling) | oh, interesting |
| **えっ / ええっ** | what?! |
| **本当ですか / ほんと?** | really? |
| **うそ!** | no way! (casual; "lie!" — not an accusation) |
| **まさか** | no way, it can't be |
| **すごい / すごいですね** | amazing |

### Sympathy

| Aizuchi | Use |
|---|---|
| **大変（たいへん）ですね / 大変だね** | That must be hard |
| **それは残念（ざんねん）でしたね** | That's a shame |
| **それはよかったですね** | That's great (for you) |
| **お疲（つか）れ様（さま）です** | You must be tired (after work or effort) |
| **かわいそうに** | Poor thing |

### Prompting: and then?

| Aizuchi | Level |
|---|---|
| **それで?** | And then? |
| **で?** | So? (casual) |
| **それから?** | And after that? |
| **それでどうなったんですか** | And what happened? (polite) |

## Repeating the speaker's words

A very common aizuchi is to **repeat the key word**, often with rising intonation:

- A: 昨日、富士山（ふじさん）に登（のぼ）ったんです。 B: **富士山に！** すごいですね。 (A: I climbed Mt. Fuji yesterday. B: Mt. Fuji! Amazing.)
- A: 3時間待ったよ。 B: **3時間も？** (A: I waited three hours. B: Three whole hours?)

## Timing and nodding

- Aizuchi come at **natural pauses**, often after particles or て-forms in the speaker's sentence, not only at sentence ends.
- **Nodding** (うなずき) goes with them, and can replace them in face-to-face talk.
- Repeating gives rhythm: **はいはい**, **うんうん**, **ええ、ええ**.

**Trap:** **はいはい** said quickly and flatly can sound like "yeah, yeah, whatever". Keep it gentle, or use a single はい with superiors.

## Polite vs casual

| Polite (to superiors, strangers, customers) | Casual (to friends, family) |
|---|---|
| はい / ええ | うん |
| そうですか | そう / そっか |
| そうなんですか | そうなんだ |
| そうですね | そうだね |
| 本当ですか | ほんと？ / マジで？ (slang) |
| 大変ですね | 大変だね |

**Trap:** **うん** to a superior sounds too casual. Use **はい** or **ええ**.

## On the phone

Phone conversations need **more** aizuchi, because the speaker can't see you nod:

- はい… はい… ええ… そうですか… はい、承知（しょうち）しました。 (Yes… yes… I see… Yes, understood.)

Long silence on the phone makes the other person check: **もしもし？** (Hello? Are you there?)
