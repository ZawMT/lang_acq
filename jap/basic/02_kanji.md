# Kanji and Readings (漢字)

**Kanji** are Chinese characters, brought to Japan from about the 5th century. Each kanji carries a **meaning**, and usually **more than one reading**. Japanese writing uses kanji for the core of most words, and hiragana for the grammar around them (see [Kana](./01_kana.md)).

- **食**べる (to eat): 食 carries the meaning "eat", べる is the verb ending
- **学生** (student): both characters carry meaning: 学 learn + 生 person/life

The official list of everyday kanji, **常用漢字（じょうようかんじ）**, has **2,136** characters (2010 revision). Newspapers and most public writing stay inside this list. Children learn about **1,026** of them in primary school.

## Two kinds of reading: 音読み and 訓読み

Most kanji have two kinds of reading:

| Reading | What it is | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **音読み（おんよみ）** ("sound reading") | the old Chinese pronunciation, as Japanese heard it | in **compounds** of two or more kanji |
| **訓読み（くんよみ）** ("meaning reading") | the native Japanese word with that meaning | a kanji **on its own**, or with hiragana endings |

Dictionaries often write 音読み in **katakana** and 訓読み in **hiragana**.

### Example: 手 (hand)

| Reading | Kind | Words |
|---|---|---|
| **て** | 訓読み | 手 (hand), 手紙（てがみ） (letter), 上手（じょうず） (skilled)* |
| **シュ** | 音読み | 歌手（かしゅ） (singer), 選手（せんしゅ） (athlete), 握手（あくしゅ） (handshake), 拍手（はくしゅ） (applause) |

\* 上手 is an irregular reading; see [Special readings](#special-readings) below.

### More examples

| Kanji | Meaning | 訓読み | 音読み |
|---|---|---|---|
| 山 | mountain | やま: 山 | サン: 富士山（ふじさん） |
| 水 | water | みず: 水 | スイ: 水曜日（すいようび） (Wednesday) |
| 人 | person | ひと: 人 | ジン: 日本人（にほんじん）; ニン: 三人（さんにん） |
| 日 | sun, day | ひ: 日; か: 三日（みっか） | ニチ: 毎日（まいにち）; ジツ: 本日（ほんじつ） |
| 生 | life, birth | い(きる): 生きる; う(まれる): 生まれる | セイ: 学生（がくせい）; ショウ: 一生（いっしょう） |
| 行 | go | い(く): 行く | コウ: 旅行（りょこう）; ギョウ: 行事（ぎょうじ） |
| 大 | big | おお(きい): 大きい | ダイ: 大学（だいがく）; タイ: 大切（たいせつ） |

Some kanji have several 音読み, because they were borrowed from China at different periods. Some have no 訓読み at all (e.g. 茶 チャ / サ, tea), and a few have no 音読み.

**Rule of thumb:** a kanji **alone or with hiragana** → 訓読み. Two or more kanji **together** → 音読み. There are many exceptions, so learn each word with its reading.

## 送り仮名: the hiragana tail

**送り仮名（おくりがな）** is the hiragana written after a kanji to show the ending. It tells you which reading to use and carries the conjugation:

| Word | Kanji part | 送り仮名 |
|---|---|---|
| 食べる (eat) | 食 (た) | べる |
| 食べない (don't eat) | 食 (た) | べない |
| 高い (expensive, high) | 高 (たか) | い |
| 新しい (new) | 新 (あたら) | しい |

The kanji stays the same while the 送り仮名 changes (see [Verb conjugation](./07_verb_conjugation.md)).

## Sound changes in compounds

### 連濁 (rendaku): the second part becomes voiced

When two words join, the first sound of the second word often gets a **゛**:

| Parts | Compound |
|---|---|
| て + かみ (paper) | て**が**み 手紙 (letter) |
| ひと + ひと | ひと**び**と 人々 (people) |
| はな + ひ (fire) | はな**び** 花火 (fireworks) |
| にほん + はし | にほん**ば**し 日本橋 (Nihonbashi) |

### Small っ between 音読み

When a 音読み ending in く, ち, つ is followed by a k-, s-, t-, or p-sound, it often becomes **っ**:

- がく + こう → が**っ**こう 学校 (school)
- いち + かい → い**っ**かい 一回 (once)
- にち + き → に**っ**き 日記 (diary)

## Special readings

Some words are read **as a whole**, not kanji by kanji. These are called **熟字訓（じゅくじくん）**:

| Word | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 今日 | きょう | today |
| 明日 | あした | tomorrow |
| 昨日 | きのう | yesterday |
| 大人 | おとな | adult |
| 一人 / 二人 | ひとり / ふたり | one / two people |
| 上手 / 下手 | じょうず / へた | good at / bad at |
| 時計 | とけい | clock, watch |
| 眼鏡 | めがね | glasses |
| 二十歳 | はたち | 20 years old |

## The repeat mark 々

**々** repeats the kanji before it: 人々（ひとびと） (people), 時々（ときどき） (sometimes), 色々（いろいろ） (various), 我々（われわれ） (we).

## Radicals (部首)

Most kanji are built from smaller parts. The part used to look a kanji up in a dictionary is its **部首（ぶしゅ）**, and it often hints at the meaning:

| Radical | Meaning | Kanji |
|---|---|---|
| 亻 (人) | person | 休 (rest), 体 (body), 作 (make) |
| 氵 (水) | water | 海 (sea), 泳 (swim), 洗 (wash) |
| 扌 (手) | hand | 持 (hold), 押 (push), 指 (finger) |
| 木 | tree, wood | 林 (woods), 森 (forest), 机 (desk) |
| 口 | mouth | 味 (taste), 呼 (call), 吸 (inhale) |
| 言 (訁) | speech | 話 (talk), 語 (language), 読 (read) |
| 糸 | thread | 紙 (paper), 細 (thin), 線 (line) |
| 艹 | plant | 花 (flower), 茶 (tea), 薬 (medicine) |
| 辶 | movement | 道 (road), 近 (near), 遠 (far) |

Many kanji also have a part that hints at the **音読み**: 青 セイ → 晴（セイ）, 清（セイ）, 精（セイ）.

## Kanji or kana?

Not everything with a kanji is written in kanji. In modern writing, these are **usually in hiragana**:

- particles and endings: は, を, ます, ない
- many grammar words: こと, もの, ところ, とき (when used grammatically)
- many adverbs and conjunctions: とても, もう, ちょっと, しかし
- some common words: ありがとう, よろしく, おいしい, きれい

Words with rare kanji are also written in hiragana, sometimes with **ふりがな**, small hiragana printed above the kanji to show its reading. Children's books and learner materials use ふりがな on most kanji.

## Learning tips

- Learn **words**, not isolated kanji: 手紙（てがみ） teaches you a reading, a meaning, and 連濁 at once.
- Learn kanji in **families** that share a part: 持, 待, 特, 時 all contain 寺 (ジ).
- **Stroke order** matters for handwriting and for recognizing handwritten forms. General rules: top to bottom, left to right, horizontal before vertical.
