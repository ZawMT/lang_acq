# Voice: Passive, Causative, Potential (受身・使役・可能)

Japanese changes the **verb ending** to show who does the action and how: being done to (passive), making or letting someone do it (causative), or being able to do it (potential).

## Overview

| Form | Group 1: 書（か）く | Group 2: 食（た）べる | する | 来る（くる） |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Passive** | 書**かれる** | 食べ**られる** | **される** | **こられる** |
| **Potential** | 書**ける** | 食べ**られる** | **できる** | **こられる** |
| **Causative** | 書**かせる** | 食べ**させる** | **させる** | **こさせる** |
| **Causative-passive** | 書**かされる** / 書**かせられる** | 食べ**させられる** | **させられる** | **こさせられる** |

All these new forms are **Group 2 verbs** themselves: 書かれる → 書かれ**ます**, 書かれ**ない**, 書かれ**た**.

**Trap:** for Group 2, the **passive and potential look the same** (食べられる). Context tells them apart.

## Passive (受身)

### Making it

| Group | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Group 1 | final u → **a** + **れる** | 書く → 書**か**れる, 読（よ）む → 読**ま**れる, 言（い）う → 言**わ**れる |
| Group 2 | drop る + **られる** | 見（み）る → 見られる |
| する | される | 招待（しょうたい）する → 招待される |
| 来る | こられる | |

### Direct passive

The person or thing affected becomes the subject. The doer is marked with **に**:

- 私（わたし）は先生（せんせい）**に**ほめ**られた**。 (I was praised by the teacher.)
- 弟（おとうと）は犬（いぬ）**に**かま**れた**。 (My younger brother was bitten by a dog.)

For **creations, discoveries, and formal statements**, the doer takes **によって**:

- この小説（しょうせつ）は夏目漱石（なつめそうせき）**によって**書か**れた**。 (This novel was written by Natsume Soseki.)

When the doer is unimportant, it's left out. This is common in news and formal writing:

- オリンピックは4年ごとに開（ひら）か**れる**。 (The Olympics are held every four years.)

### The "suffering" passive

Japanese also uses the passive for things that **happen to you and bother you**, even with intransitive verbs. This passive has no direct equivalent in many languages:

- 雨**に**降ら**れた**。 (I got rained on.)
- 隣（となり）の人**に**たばこを吸（す）わ**れた**。 (The person next to me smoked, and it bothered me.)
- 子供（こども）**に**泣（な）か**れて**、寝（ね）られなかった。 (The baby cried, so I couldn't sleep.)
- 友達（ともだち）**に**ケーキを食べ**られた**。 (My friend ate my cake.)

**Trap:** the passive often sounds **negative** in Japanese. For something good done for you, use **〜てもらう** instead (see [Giving and Receiving](./22_giving_receiving.md)): 友達に手伝（てつだ）って**もらった** (My friend helped me.)

### Respectful passive

The same form is also a light **respectful** form (see [Keigo](./08_politeness_keigo.md)):

- 社長（しゃちょう）は何時に来**られます**か。 (What time will the president come?)

## Potential: can (可能)

### Making it

| Group | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Group 1 | final u → **e** + **る** | 書く → 書**け**る, 話（はな）す → 話**せ**る, 泳（およ）ぐ → 泳**げ**る |
| Group 2 | drop る + **られる** | 食べる → 食べられる, 見る → 見られる |
| する | **できる** | 運転（うんてん）する → 運転できる |
| 来る | こられる | |

The thing you can do usually takes **が** (を is also used, especially with longer phrases):

- 日本語（にほんご）**が**話**せ**ます。 (I can speak Japanese.)
- 漢字（かんじ）**が**読**め**ない。 (I can't read kanji.)
- 泳**げ**ますか。 (Can you swim?)

### ら抜き: dropping ら

In speech, Group 2 and 来る potentials often drop **ら**: 食べ**れる**, 見**れる**, 来（こ）**れる**. It's very common, especially among younger speakers, but it's considered **non-standard**. Use **〜られる** in writing and formal speech.

### 〜ことができる

**dictionary form + ことができる** also means "can". It's a bit more formal:

- ここでチケットを買う**ことができます**。 (You can buy tickets here.)

### 見える / 聞こえる vs 見られる / 聞ける

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **見える** | be visible (it just comes into view) |
| **見られる** | be able to see (have the chance) |
| **聞こえる** | be audible |
| **聞ける** | be able to listen |

- ここから富士山（ふじさん）が**見える**。 (You can see Mt. Fuji from here.)
- このサイトで映画（えいが）が**見られる**。 (You can watch movies on this site.)

## Causative: make or let (使役)

### Making it

| Group | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Group 1 | final u → **a** + **せる** | 書く → 書**か**せる, 待（ま）つ → 待**た**せる, 言う → 言**わ**せる |
| Group 2 | drop る + **させる** | 食べる → 食べさせる |
| する | させる | |
| 来る | こさせる | |

### Make vs let

The causative means both **make someone do** and **let someone do**. Context decides:

- 母（はは）は弟に野菜（やさい）を食べ**させた**。 (Mom made my brother eat vegetables.)
- 父（ちち）は私に車を運転さ**せて**くれた。 (Dad let me drive the car.)

The person made or allowed to act:

| Verb | Marked with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| has an object (を) | **に** | 子供**に**本を読ま**せる** (have the child read a book) |
| no object | **を** (forced) or **に** (allowed, willing) | 子供**を**行か**せる** (make the child go) / 子供**に**行か**せる** (let the child go) |

### 〜させてください: let me

The causative + **〜てください** is a polite way to **offer or ask permission** to do something yourself:

- 私に払（はら）わ**せてください**。 (Please let me pay.)
- 少（すこ）し考（かんが）え**させてください**。 (Let me think about it a little.)
- 今日は休ま**せていただけませんか**。 (Could I please take today off?)

## Causative-passive: be made to

The **causative + passive** means **being made to do something** you didn't want to:

| Group | Form | Short form (Group 1, common in speech) |
|---|---|---|
| Group 1 | 書か**せられる** | 書か**される** |
| Group 2 | 食べ**させられる** | — |
| する | **させられる** | — |
| 来る | **こさせられる** | — |

- 子供の時（とき）、毎日ピアノを練習（れんしゅう）**させられた**。 (When I was a child, I was made to practice piano every day.)
- 先輩（せんぱい）にお酒（さけ）を飲**まされた**。 (My senior made me drink.)
- 1時間も待た**された**。 (I was kept waiting a whole hour.)

**Trap:** the short form 〜される doesn't work for Group 1 verbs ending in **す**: 話す → 話**させられる** (not ✗ 話さされる).

## Summary

| Meaning | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| X does it | basic | 先生が**ほめた** (The teacher praised me.) |
| X has it done to them | passive | 私が先生に**ほめられた** (I was praised by the teacher.) |
| X can do it | potential | 私は日本語が**話せる** (I can speak Japanese.) |
| X makes/lets Y do it | causative | 母が私に**掃除（そうじ）させた** (Mom made me clean.) |
| X is made to do it | causative-passive | 私は母に**掃除させられた** (I was made to clean by Mom.) |
