# Pronunciation (発音)

Japanese has a **small sound system**: 5 vowels, about 15 consonants, and almost no consonant clusters. What makes it sound Japanese is **rhythm** (every beat takes about the same time) and **pitch accent** (high and low pitch, not stress).

## Vowels (母音)

| Vowel | Sound | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| あ a | [a] | open, central |
| い i | [i] | |
| う u | [ɯ] | **lips not rounded**, relaxed |
| え e | [e] | pure, no glide at the end |
| お o | [o] | rounded, held steady |

Each vowel is **short and pure**: the mouth doesn't move while you say it. The same vowel is always pronounced the same way.

### Devoiced vowels

**い** and **う** often become **whispered** (or almost disappear) between voiceless consonants (k, s, sh, t, ch, ts, h, f, p) or at the end of a sentence after one:

| Written | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| で**す** | des |
| しま**す** | shimas |
| **す**き (like) | s'ki |
| **ち**かてつ (subway) | ch'katetsu |
| **ひ**と (person) | h'to |

The beat is still there; only the voice goes. This is normal in standard (Tokyo) speech.

## Mora: the beat

The basic unit of Japanese rhythm is the **mora (拍, はく)**. Each mora takes about the same time. Each of these counts as **one mora**:

- a kana letter: か, い
- a combined sound: きゃ, しょ
- **ん**
- small **っ** (a one-beat pause)
- the **second half of a long vowel** (ー, or the extra vowel letter)

| Word | Mora | Count |
|---|---|---|
| にほん (Japan) | に・ほ・ん | 3 |
| にっぽん (Japan) | に・っ・ぽ・ん | 4 |
| とうきょう (Tokyo) | と・う・きょ・う | 4 |
| コーヒー (coffee) | コ・ー・ヒ・ー | 4 |
| がっこう (school) | が・っ・こ・う | 4 |

Haiku counts these beats: 5-7-5 mora.

### Length changes meaning

Because each mora counts, a **long vowel** or a **small っ** makes a different word:

| Short | Long |
|---|---|
| おばさん (aunt) | おば**あ**さん (grandmother) |
| おじさん (uncle) | おじ**い**さん (grandfather) |
| ここ (here) | こ**う**こ**う** (high school) |
| ゆき (snow) | ゆ**う**き (courage) |
| きて (come) | き**っ**て (stamp) |
| かこ (past) | か**っ**こ (brackets) |

**Trap:** hold long vowels and っ for a full beat. Shortening them is one of the most common learner mistakes, and it changes the word.

### How long vowels are spelled vs said

- **えい** is usually said as a long **ee**: せんせい → *sensee*, えいご → *eego*
- **おう** is said as a long **oo**: がっこう → *gakkoo*, ありがとう → *arigatoo*

## Consonants (子音)

Most consonants are close to their romaji letter. These need attention:

| Sound | Notes |
|---|---|
| **r** (ら行) | a quick **tap** of the tongue tip behind the teeth, between r, l, and d |
| **ふ** fu | made with **both lips**, blowing gently, not with teeth on lip |
| **し** shi, **ち** chi | the tongue is flatter and further back than in s/t |
| **つ** tsu | "ts" at the start of a syllable: つなみ (tsunami) |
| **ひ** hi | close to a soft hiss: [çi] |
| **じ / ぢ** ji, **ず / づ** zu | sound identical (see [Kana](./01_kana.md#voiced-sounds--and-)) |
| **g** in the middle of a word | traditionally nasal [ŋ] (かがみ → *kaŋami*); now often a plain g, especially among younger speakers (see [Nasal g](#nasal-g-鼻濁音)) |
| **を** | just **o** |

### The sounds of ん

**ん** is a full mora, and its sound depends on what follows:

| Before | ん sounds like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| m, b, p | **m** | さんぽ *sampo* (walk), しんぶん *shimbun* (newspaper) |
| n, t, d, ts, r | **n** | みんな *minna* (everyone), ほんとう |
| k, g | **ng** | りんご *ringo* (apple), げんき |
| end of a word | a soft **n** in the back of the mouth | ほん (book) |
| a vowel or y | nasal vowel, no closure | れんあい *ren'ai* (romance) |

**Trap:** ん before a vowel is not linked to it. **きんえん** (*kin'en*, no smoking) is four mora: き・ん・え・ん, not *ki-ne-n*.

### Nasal g (鼻濁音)

In traditional standard (Tokyo) speech, the が-row (が ぎ ぐ げ ご) is sometimes said with a nasal **[ŋ]** instead of [g]. This is called **鼻濁音（びだくおん）**.

[ŋ] is the "ng" in English **singer**, not in **finger**: there's no g after the nasal. It **replaces** the g; no extra n comes before it:

- わたし**が** → *watashi ŋa*, still four mora: わ・た・し・が
- *watashi n-ga* ✗ sounds like わたしんが, with an extra beat

To find the sound, say "singer", then start from the "nger" part: **ŋa**.

**Always [g]:**

| Where | Examples |
|---|---|
| start of a word | **が**っこう, **ぎ**んこう, **ご**はん |
| ご (five) in numbers | じゅう**ご** (15), さんじゅう**ご** (35) |
| repeated half of sound words | がら**が**ら, ごろ**ご**ろ, ぎら**ぎ**ら |
| after a prefix, or the start of the second word in a compound | お**げ**んき, にほん**ぎ**んこう (日本銀行（にほんぎんこう）) |
| most loanwords | プロ**グ**ラム, **ゴ**ルフ |

**Traditionally [ŋ], but [g] is also fine:**

| Where | Examples |
|---|---|
| inside a word | か**が**み (mirror), か**ぎ** (key), な**が**い (long), す**ぐ** (soon) |
| the particle が | わたし**が**, ねこ**が** |
| g from rendaku (k → g when words join) | て**が**み (手紙（てがみ）: て + かみ), なが**ぐ**つ (長靴（ながぐつ）: なが + くつ) |

[ŋ] is now used less and less, especially by younger speakers, and many regional accents (Kansai, Kyushu) never had it. NHK announcers, actors and choir singers still learn it because it sounds soft and polished. Some dictionaries mark it as か゚ き゚ く゚ け゚ こ゚.

**Trap:** no position *requires* [ŋ] today, so a plain [g] everywhere is correct. The real mistake is using [ŋ] where only [g] is allowed, especially at the start of a word.

## Pitch accent (アクセント)

Japanese words have a **pitch pattern**: each mora is said **high (H)** or **low (L)**. There's no strong stress; loudness stays even. Standard Tokyo pitch follows two rules:

1. The **first and second mora differ** in pitch: if the first is low, the second is high, and vice versa.
2. Once the pitch **falls**, it doesn't rise again inside the word.

So what you learn for each word is **where the pitch falls**, if it falls at all. The fall is called the **accent nucleus**, often marked ↓.

| Pattern | Name | Example | Pitch |
|---|---|---|---|
| falls after the 1st mora | 頭高（あたまだか） | **箸** (chopsticks) | は↓し: H-L |
| falls in the middle | 中高（なかだか） | **お菓子** (sweets) | おか↓し: L-H-L |
| falls after the last mora | 尾高（おだか） | **橋** (bridge) | はし↓: L-H, a following particle drops: はしが L-H-L |
| never falls | 平板（へいばん） | **端** (edge) | はし: L-H, particle stays high: はしが L-H-H |

### Minimal pairs

| Word | Pitch | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| あめ | H-L | 雨 (rain) |
| あめ | L-H | 飴 (candy) |
| かき | H-L | 牡蠣 (oyster) |
| かき | L-H | 柿 (persimmon) |
| はし | H-L | 箸 (chopsticks) |
| はし | L-H (falls on particle) | 橋 (bridge) |

Context almost always makes the meaning clear, so a wrong pitch rarely stops communication. But correct pitch is a big part of sounding natural. Accent dictionaries (e.g. the NHK 日本語発音アクセント辞典) and online tools like OJAD show the pattern of each word.

**Pitch differs by region.** Kansai (Osaka, Kyoto) patterns are very different from Tokyo. These notes follow standard Tokyo speech.

## Intonation (イントネーション)

On top of word pitch, the **sentence** has its own intonation:

- **Questions** rise at the end: 行きますか↗ (Are you going?). In casual speech, rising intonation alone makes a question: 行く↗?
- **Statements** fall: 行きます↘.
- **Sentence-final particles** carry much of the tone: いいね↘ (agreement) vs いいね↗? (isn't it?) (see [Sentence-final Particles](./24_sentence_final_particles.md)).

## Tips

- Count the beats. Clap them if needed: が・っ・こ・う = 4 claps.
- Keep the voice **even** in loudness; change pitch, not volume.
- Don't add vowels where there are none (っ is silence, not *tsu*), and don't drop the ones that are there except when devoiced.
