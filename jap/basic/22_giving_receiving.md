# Giving and Receiving (授受表現)

Japanese has **three verbs** for giving and receiving, and the choice depends on **direction**: who gives to whom, seen from the speaker's side.

| Verb | Direction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **あげる** | me / my side → others | give (away from me) |
| **くれる** | others → me / my side | give (toward me) |
| **もらう** | receive from others | get, receive |

"My side" means you and people close to you: family, friends, your group (see [Keigo](./08_politeness_keigo.md#in-group-and-out-group-内と外)).

## あげる: giving away from me

Giver は / が, receiver **に**, thing **を**:

- 私（わたし）は友達（ともだち）**に**花（はな）を**あげました**。 (I gave flowers to my friend.)
- 弟（おとうと）は彼女（かのじょ）**に**チョコを**あげた**。 (My younger brother gave chocolate to his girlfriend.)
- 田中（たなか）さんは山田（やまだ）さん**に**本（ほん）を**あげた**。 (Tanaka gave a book to Yamada.) ← neither is me

## くれる: giving toward me

Use **くれる** when the receiver is **me or my side**:

- 友達が（私**に**）プレゼントを**くれました**。 (My friend gave me a present.)
- 母（はは）が妹（いもうと）**に**時計（とけい）を**くれた**。 (My mother gave my younger sister a watch.) ← from the speaker's side, mom → sister counts as "toward us"

**Trap:** **no あげる toward me**. ✗ 友達が私にプレゼントを**あげた**. When **I** am the receiver, it must be **くれる**.

## もらう: receiving

The receiver is the subject. The giver is marked with **に** or **から**:

- 私は友達**に** / **から**プレゼントを**もらいました**。 (I got a present from my friend.)
- 妹は先生（せんせい）**から**ほめ言葉（ことば）を**もらった**。 (My younger sister got praise from her teacher.)

**から** is preferred when the giver is an organization: 会社**から**ボーナスを**もらった**. (I got a bonus from the company.)

**Trap:** don't use もらう when **someone else receives from me**: ✗ 友達は私にプレゼントをもらった. Say it from your side: 私は友達にプレゼントを**あげた**.

## The same event, three ways

| | Sentence |
|---|---|
| あげる | 私は田中さんに本を**あげた**。 (I gave Tanaka a book.) |
| くれる | 田中さんが私に本を**くれた**。 (Tanaka gave me a book.) |
| もらう | 私は田中さんに本を**もらった**。 (I got a book from Tanaka.) |

## Politeness levels

| Basic | To / from a superior (polite) | To an inferior, animal, or plant |
|---|---|---|
| **あげる** | **差（さ）し上（あ）げる** (I give to you, sir/ma'am) | **やる** (give to a pet, water plants; also rough among men) |
| **くれる** | **くださる** (you kindly give me) | |
| **もらう** | **いただく** (I humbly receive) | |

- 先生に花を**差し上げました**。 (I gave flowers to my teacher.)
- 先生が本を**くださいました**。 (My teacher gave me a book.)
- 先生に本を**いただきました**。 (I received a book from my teacher.)
- 毎朝（まいあさ）花に水（みず）を**やる**。 (I water the flowers every morning.)

**Trap:** **くださる** takes **-います**: くださ**い**ます (not ✗ くださります). The request form is くださ**い**: ください.

**Trap:** **差し上げる** said directly to someone can sound pushy (like "I'm doing you a favor"). For offers, use **〜ましょうか** or humble forms: お持（も）ち**しましょうか** (Shall I carry it for you?)

## Doing favors: て-form + あげる / くれる / もらう

The same three verbs after a **て-form** mean doing an action **as a favor**. The direction rule is the same:

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜て**あげる** | do something for someone | 友達に道（みち）を教（おし）えて**あげた**。 (I showed my friend the way.) |
| 〜て**くれる** | someone does something for me | 友達が手伝（てつだ）って**くれた**。 (My friend helped me.) |
| 〜て**もらう** | have someone do something for me | 友達に手伝って**もらった**。 (I got my friend to help me. → I had my friend's help.) |

Japanese uses these very often. Leaving them out can sound cold or ungrateful:

- 母が弁当（べんとう）を作（つく）った。 (My mother made a lunch box.) → neutral fact
- 母が弁当を作って**くれた**。 (My mother made me a lunch box.) → I'm grateful

### くれる vs もらう

Both mean "someone did something for me", but:

- **〜てくれる**: the other person **did it on their own**: 先輩（せんぱい）が駅（えき）まで送（おく）って**くれた**。 (My senior kindly took me to the station.)
- **〜てもらう**: I **asked** or arranged for it: 先輩に駅まで送って**もらった**。 (I had my senior take me to the station.)

### Careful with 〜てあげる

**〜てあげる** can sound **condescending** ("I'm doing you a favor"), especially toward superiors. Don't say it to them directly:

- ✗ 先生、荷物（にもつ）を持って**あげます**。
- ✓ 先生、荷物を**お持ちします**。 (Let me carry your bag, sir/ma'am.)

## Requests with くれる and もらう

The **potential** and **negative question** forms of these verbs are the standard ways to make polite requests, from casual to very polite:

| Form | Politeness |
|---|---|
| 〜て**くれる**？ | casual |
| 〜て**くれない**？ | casual, softer |
| 〜て**くれませんか** | polite |
| 〜て**もらえますか** | polite |
| 〜て**もらえませんか** | polite, softer |
| 〜て**いただけますか** | very polite |
| 〜て**いただけませんか** | very polite, softest |

- ちょっと手伝って**くれる**？ (Can you help me a sec?)
- もう一度（いちど）言（い）って**いただけませんか**。 (Could you please say that once more?)

### 〜させていただく: humbly do

**causative + ていただく** literally means "I humbly receive your permission to do". It's a very polite way to say **I'll do**, common in business:

- 本日（ほんじつ）は休（やす）ま**せていただきます**。 (I'll be taking today off.)
- 説明（せつめい）**させていただきます**。 (Allow me to explain.)

It's sometimes overused where no permission is involved. Use it when the action really depends on the listener's acceptance.
