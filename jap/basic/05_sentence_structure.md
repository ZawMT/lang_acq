# Sentence Structure (文の構造)

The core rule of Japanese word order: **the predicate comes last**. Everything else comes before it, marked by [particles](./06_particles.md).

- 私（わたし）**は** 毎朝（まいあさ） パン**を** 食（た）べます。 (I eat bread every morning.)

The basic order is **Subject – Object – Verb (SOV)**.

## Three kinds of sentence

Every Japanese sentence ends in one of three kinds of predicate:

| Kind | Predicate | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun sentence** (名詞文) | noun + だ / です | 田中（たなか）さんは**先生（せんせい）です**。 (Mr. Tanaka is a teacher.) |
| **Adjective sentence** (形容詞文) | adjective (+ です) | この本（ほん）は**おもしろいです**。 (This book is interesting.) |
| **Verb sentence** (動詞文) | verb | 子供（こども）が**寝（ね）ています**。 (The child is sleeping.) |

**だ / です** (the copula) links a noun to what it is. です is polite, だ is plain. In casual speech, だ is often dropped, especially in questions: 学生（がくせい）? (Are you a student?)

## Topic and comment: XはY

A typical Japanese sentence first sets a **topic** with **は**, then says something about it:

- **象（ぞう）は** 鼻（はな）が長（なが）い。 (As for elephants, their noses are long.)
- **今日（きょう）は** 雨（あめ）です。 (Today, it's rain. → It's raining today.)
- **私は** コーヒーです。 (As for me, coffee. → I'll have coffee.)

The topic isn't always the subject. It is "what we're talking about". The rest of the sentence is the comment. See [Particles](./06_particles.md#は-vs-が).

## Order of the other parts

Before the verb, the order is flexible, because particles show the roles. A neutral order is:

**Topic → Time → Place → (with whom) → Object → Verb**

- 私は | 明日（あした） | 図書館（としょかん）で | 友達（ともだち）と | 本を | 読（よ）みます。
  (Tomorrow I'll read books with a friend at the library.)

Moving a part closer to the verb puts a little more focus on it:

- 本を 明日 図書館で 読みます。 (Neutral meaning; slightly more weight on "at the library".)

**The predicate never moves.** In casual speech, a word is sometimes added **after** the verb as an afterthought: 食べた? あれ。 (Did you eat it? That thing.)

## Modifiers come first

Anything that describes something comes **before** it:

| Modifier | Modified |
|---|---|
| 赤（あか）い | 車（くるま） (a red car) |
| 静（しず）かな | 部屋（へや） (a quiet room) |
| 友達**の** | 本 (a friend's book) |
| 昨日（きのう）買（か）った | 本 (the book I bought yesterday) |
| ゆっくり | 話（はな）す (speak slowly) |

Even long clauses go before the noun (see [Modifier Clauses](./21_modifier_clauses.md)).

## Leaving things out

Japanese **leaves out** anything clear from context, especially the subject and the object. A verb alone can be a whole sentence:

- 行（い）きます。 (I'm going. / We're going. / He's going.)
- A: 昨日の映画（えいが）、見（み）た？ B: うん、見た。 (A: Did you see the movie yesterday? B: Yeah, I saw it.)

Saying 私は in every sentence sounds unnatural. Mention the subject when it's new, contrasted, or unclear.

## Questions

Add **か** to the end of a polite sentence. The word order doesn't change:

- 学生です。 → 学生です**か**。 (Are you a student?)
- 行きます。 → 行きます**か**。 (Are you going?)

**Question words** stay where the answer would go:

- 何（なに）を食べますか。 (What will you eat?) → ラーメンを食べます。
- どこへ行きますか。 (Where are you going?) → 駅（えき）へ行きます。
- 誰（だれ）が来（き）ましたか。 (Who came?) → 田中さんが来ました。

**Trap:** a question word can't take は: ✗ 誰は来ましたか. Use **が** (see [Particles](./06_particles.md#は-vs-が)).

In casual speech, か is usually dropped and a **rising tone** makes the question: 行く↗? Casual か can sound rough.

## Existence sentences: ある and いる

Japanese uses two verbs for "there is / to be located":

| Verb | Used for |
|---|---|
| **ある** | things, plants, events |
| **いる** | people and animals (things that move by themselves) |

Two common patterns:

| Pattern | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Place**に** X**が** ある/いる | what's there (new info) | 机（つくえ）の上（うえ）**に** 本**が** あります。 (There's a book on the desk.) |
| X**は** place**に** ある/いる | where something is | 本**は** 机の上**に** あります。 (The book is on the desk.) |

Possession also uses ある/いる: 私は兄（あに）が**います**。 (I have an older brother.) 時間（じかん）が**ありません**。 (I don't have time.)

## Double subject: XはYが…

A very common pattern describes **a feature of the topic**:

- 象**は** 鼻**が** 長い。 (Elephants have long noses.)
- 私**は** 頭（あたま）**が** 痛（いた）い。 (I have a headache.)
- 東京（とうきょう）**は** 人**が** 多（おお）い。 (Tokyo has a lot of people.)

The same pattern is used with **likes, skills, wants, and understanding**. The thing liked or understood takes **が**:

- 私は 猫（ねこ）**が** 好（す）きです。 (I like cats.)
- 弟（おとうと）は 料理（りょうり）**が** 上手（じょうず）です。 (My younger brother is good at cooking.)
- 日本語（にほんご）**が** 分（わ）かります。 (I understand Japanese.)

## Plain style and polite style

Every sentence ending is either **plain** (普通体（ふつうたい）) or **polite** (丁寧体（ていねいたい）, です/ます). Keep one style through a conversation:

| | Plain | Polite |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | 学生**だ** | 学生**です** |
| Adjective | 高**い** | 高**いです** |
| Verb | 食べ**る** | 食べ**ます** |

See [Politeness and Keigo](./08_politeness_keigo.md).

## Ending the sentence

The predicate is often followed by **sentence-final particles** that show the speaker's attitude, such as **ね** (right?) and **よ** (I'm telling you):

- いい天気（てんき）です**ね**。 (Nice weather, isn't it?)
- この店（みせ）、安（やす）い**よ**。 (This shop is cheap, you know.)

See [Sentence-final Particles](./24_sentence_final_particles.md).
